## Maia
- **Day**: 1
- **Fasti status**: F (KAL. MAI.)
- **Deity**: Maia
- **Ritual**: Sacrifice offered by the *flamen Volcanalis*. The victim was a pregnant sow, proper for an earth-goddess. Cornelius Labeo identified Maia with Terra.
- **Aition**: Not stated explicitly; Scullard notes the connection with Vulcan is "quite obscure." The name Maia derives from the root *mag* indicating growth or increase.
- **Participants**: Flamen Volcanalis
- **Page**: p. 116

## Bona Dea ad saxum (temple dedication)
- **Day**: 1
- **Fasti status**: not stated (parenthetical entry, not in main calendar headers)
- **Deity**: Bona Dea
- **Ritual**: Dedication day of Bona Dea's temple on the Aventine slope below a large rock. Cult celebrated by women only; men rigorously excluded. Wine brought in had to be called *lac* (milk) and carried in a vessel called *mellarium* (honey-pot). Head of goddess covered with vine-leaves. Healing herbs and snakes kept in her temple. Sacrifice offered was a sow (*porca*). Victim also called *Damium*, priestess *Damiatrix*, goddess herself *Damia* (Festus). Myrtle was excluded from the temple.
- **Aition**: Myrtle excluded because Faunus beat his daughter Fauna (= Bona Dea) with a myrtle-rod. Possibly a rite of flagellation implied.
- **Participants**: Women only; Vestal Virgins (the temple was dedicated by a Vestal named Claudia, per Ovid, though possibly confused with Licinia)
- **Page**: pp. 116–117

## Laribus Praestites
- **Day**: 1
- **Fasti status**: not stated in main header (listed in calendar as *Laribus*, Ven. Esq.)
- **Deity**: Lares Praestites (Guardian Lares of the State)
- **Ritual**: Worship of the Lares Praestites at their altar (attributed to King Titus Tatius) near the Palatine *pomerium* and their temple at the top of the Via Sacra. Ovid describes small images of the gods worn away with age, with a dog at their feet. Lares Praestites depicted on a denarius (112/111 BC) as two young men with spears and a dog between them.
- **Aition**: Not stated. Plutarch says some Romans interpreted the Lares as spirits of punishment like the Furies, aided by dogs to track evil-doers.
- **Participants**: General public; civic cult
- **Page**: pp. 117–118

## Florae (Ven.)
- **Day**: 3
- **Fasti status**: C (VNON. MAI.)
- **Deity**: Flora
- **Ritual**: Either the last day (ludi) of the Floralia, which had begun 27 April and was linked with Flora's temple near the Circus Maximus, or a separate ceremony at her other temple on the Quirinal slope (foundation date and history unknown).
- **Aition**: Not stated
- **Participants**: Not specified
- **Page**: p. 118

## Lemuria
- **Day**: 9, 11, 13 (three non-successive days)
- **Fasti status**: N, Dies religiosi (VII, V, III ID. MAI.)
- **Deity**: Lemures (wandering spirits of the dead)
- **Ritual**: Public cult (nature of public sacrifice unknown). Private and domestic rites described by Ovid: the paterfamilias rises at midnight barefoot, makes an apotropaic gesture (thumb held between closed fingers), washes hands in pure water, walks through the house spitting black beans from his mouth nine times with head averted, saying "with these beans I redeem me and mine." The ghosts collect the beans unseen. He then washes again, clashes bronze vessels, and repeats nine times "Ghosts of my fathers, be gone" (*manes exite paternae*), then looks back.
- **Aition**: Ovid records an "unacceptable derivation" from Remus, whose violent death was atoned for by establishing the festival; Scullard calls this derivation unacceptable. Lemures defined by Porphyrion as "wandering and terrifying shades of men who died untimely deaths" (*umbras vagantes*).
- **Participants**: Paterfamilias (private rite); public rites unspecified
- **Page**: pp. 118–119

## Ma[niae] sacrifice
- **Day**: 11
- **Fasti status**: N, Dies religiosus (V ID. MAI.) — listed in Ant. mai. as *Ma[niae]*
- **Deity**: Mania (mother of the Lares, *Mater Larum*); name possibly means "the good one," a euphemism for the death-goddess
- **Ritual**: A sacrifice was made, probably to Mania rather than to Mars or Mater Matuta. An effigy of Mania might be hung on the door of a house when danger threatened a family. Ugly human figures made from flour were called *Maniae* or *Maniolae*. The Lemuria was considered a suitable time for Mania to receive sacrifice.
- **Aition**: Not stated
- **Participants**: Not specified; connection with Compitalia worship of Mania noted
- **Page**: p. 119

## Marti Invicto (Ven.)
- **Day**: 14
- **Fasti status**: C (PR ID.)
- **Deity**: Mars Invictus
- **Ritual**: Temple of Mars Invictus in the Circus Flaminius, founded by D. Junius Brutus Callaicus (consul 135 BC). Temple designed by a Greek architect and decorated with verses of the poet Accius; contained a colossal statue of Mars by Scopas.
- **Aition**: Not stated
- **Participants**: Not specified
- **Page**: pp. 119–120

## Argei (procession and throwing)
- **Day**: 14 (Scullard prefers this to Dionysius' date of 15 May)
- **Fasti status**: not stated (parenthetical; not a calendar heading in its own right)
- **Deity**: Uncertain — Ovid and Dionysius believed Saturn; Verrius Flaccus thought Dis Pater; some modern scholars propose the river Tiber
- **Ritual**: Pontiffs, Vestal Virgins, praetors and permitted citizens processed through Rome and threw 27 (or 30, per Dionysius) rush effigies of human form (*Argei*) from the Sublician bridge into the Tiber. The *flaminka* (wife of the *flamen Dialis*) accompanied the procession with dishevelled hair (sign of mourning). Puppets had been deposited in the 27 *sacella* of the Argei during the March procession (16–17 March) and collected on 14 May. Plutarch called the ceremony "the greatest of purifications."
- **Aition**: Dionysius explained the effigies as a later substitution for an ancient human sacrifice to Saturn. Ovid refers to an old tradition of throwing men over sixty from the bridge (*sexagenarios de ponte*), explained away as elders driven from the voting gangways. Modern theories include substitute for human sacrifice, dying Vegetation Spirit, appeasement of the Tiber, purification of accumulated evils — none resolved.
- **Participants**: Pontiffs, Vestal Virgins, praetors, permitted citizens; *flaminka*
- **Page**: pp. 120–122

## Mercurio et Maiae (Mercury's birthday)
- **Day**: 15
- **Fasti status**: NP (ID. MAI.) — also *Feriae Iovi* (Ven.), *Natalis Mercurii* (Phil.), *Sacrum Mercurio* (Menol.)
- **Deity**: Mercury (primary); Maia (associated, sharing Mercury's temple on the Aventine)
- **Ritual**: Mercury's birthday (*Natalis Mercurii*). His temple on the Aventine above the Circus Maximus, outside the *pomerium*, traditionally dedicated 495 BC. The Ides of May became a festival for traders (*mercatores*), with Mercury's temple the centre of their guild (*collegium*). Ovid (F.5.673ff.) describes a merchant drawing water from the *aqua Mercurii* (a spring near the Capena Gate) in fumigated jars, wetting a laurel bough with it, then sprinkling his goods and his own hair; the merchant prayed for forgiveness for past and future perjuries and for profit. Ides also sacred to Jupiter (*Feriae Iovi*).
- **Aition**: Maia was worshipped because she was identified (as a late development) with the Greek Maia, mother of Hermes = Mercury.
- **Participants**: Merchants (*mercatores*); their collegium; Maia's cult apparently shared the temple
- **Page**: pp. 122–123

- **Fasti status**: NP (XII KAL. IUN.)

- **Page**: p. 122–123

## Tubilustrium
- **Day**: 23
- **Fasti status**: NP (X KAL. IUN.)
- **Deity**: No deity named for the Tubilustrium itself; *Feriae Volcano* listed alongside (Ven. and Amit.)
- **Ritual**: Repetition of the Tubilustrium held 23 March — purification of the war-trumpets (*tubae*) used to summon the assembly. Possibly also involved a lustration of the army. The Vulcan festival on the same day was unconnected with the Tubilustrium; the Romans invented a link by noting Vulcan as smith who made the trumpets.
- **Aition**: Not stated explicitly; Scullard suggests the repetition made sense if the ceremony was designed to purify trumpets before the following day's assembly (*comitia*). Vulcan's connection with Maia noted (see 1 May). Vulcan's main festival was 23 August.
- **Participants**: Not specified; army connection suggested for archaic period
- **Page**: p. 123

## Q.R.C.F. (Quando Rex Comitiavit Fas)
- **Day**: 24
- **Fasti status**: F (IX KAL. IUN.)
- **Deity**: None (procedural dies comitialis marker)
- **Ritual**: Formal dismissal of the Comitia by the Rex Sacrorum; after this, legal business (*fas*) could be transacted. Parallel to 24 March entry.
- **Aition**: Not stated
- **Participants**: Rex Sacrorum; Comitia
- **Page**: p. 123

## Fortuna Publica / Fortuna Primigenia
- **Day**: 25
- **Fasti status**: C (VIII KAL. IUN.)
- **Deity**: Fortuna Publica Populi Romani Quiritium (= Fortuna Primigenia)
- **Ritual**: Temple on the Quirinal hill, vowed by P. Sempronius Tuditanus in 204 BC and dedicated ten years later (per Livy 29.36.8). Scullard treats it as the same Fortuna as the other temples (see 5 April), probably Fortuna Populi Romani Quiritium Primigenia. The Venusine calendar may indicate another temple of Fortuna Primigenia on the Capitol (see 13 November).
- **Aition**: Not stated
- **Participants**: Not specified
- **Page**: pp. 123–124

## Ambarvalia (Feriae Conceptivae)
- **Day**: Movable feast (*conceptivae*), not in fixed calendars; 29 May became a customary date
- **Fasti status**: Not recorded in fixed calendars (it is *conceptivae*)
- **Deity**: Mars (primary, in Cato's farmer's prayer); Ceres (Virgil, Tibullus); Dea Dia (worshipped by Arval Brethren in their grove, possibly the same festival)
- **Ritual**: A "beating of the bounds" — purification of fields from evil by leading sacrificial victims (a *suovetaurilia*: pig, sheep, ox) around the boundaries of the farm or settlement, then sacrificing them. Cato (Agr. 141) preserves the farmer's prayer to Janus, Jupiter, and Father Mars. If favourable omens were not obtained, a further atoning offering was made. The public form involved sacrifices at fixed boundary points outside Rome (Strabo 5.3.2 records priests at a spot called *Festi* between the fifth and sixth milestones, and at several other places). Private celebration well-attested from Cato, Virgil (Geor. 1.345), Tibullus (2.1): garlanded oxen rest, women set aside spinning, procession in white with olive leaves, sacrifice, feasting.
- **Aition**: Not stated explicitly. Lustratio is described as ancestral custom (*more maiorum*).
- **Participants**: Public: priests (possibly Arval Brethren or *pontifices*); Private: paterfamilias, household, farm workers; the Arval Brethren held a cognate festival for Dea Dia in May in their sacred grove
- **Page**: pp. 124–125

## Summary

Scullard covers fourteen distinct entries (plus the movable Ambarvalia) for May across PDF pages 116–125. The month's character is consistently described as "not very cheerful" — focused on expelling ghosts (*Lemuria*, 9/11/13 May), purification rituals (*Argei*, *Tubilustrium*, *Ambarvalia*), and cautious propitiation of chthonic and agricultural powers rather than celebration.

Key points for cross-referencing with Roman calendar data:
- **1 May**: Three overlapping entries (Maia, Bona Dea temple, Lares Praestites). Fasti status F.
- **Lemuria** (9, 11, 13 May): Three *dies religiosi* (N). Public sacrifice nature unknown; only domestic rites detailed via Ovid. Even days avoided.
- **Argei** (14 May): Complex processional-depositional rite; deity explicitly uncertain even to the Romans. Scullard prefers 14 May over Dionysius' 15 May.
- **15 May**: NP Ides; Mercury's birthday the dominant entry; Maia shares the temple (late identification with Greek Maia).
- **Agonalia** (21 May): NP; deity identification uncertain (possibly Vediovis per Ven.).
- **Tubilustrium** (23 May): NP; paired with a separate *Feriae Volcano* (Ven./Amit.) — Scullard stresses the two are unconnected despite Roman attempts to link them.
- **Ambarvalia**: Movable; 29 May was customary. Suovetaurilia + Cato's prayer are the primary ritual sources. Possible identity with the Arval Brethren's Dea Dia festival left unresolved.
- Mars Invictus (14 May) and Fortuna Primigenia (25 May) are temple-dedication anniversaries with limited ritual detail.
- Q.R.C.F. (24 May) is procedural, not a cult event.
