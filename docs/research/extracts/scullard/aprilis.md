## Veneralia
- **Day**: 1
- **Fasti status**: F
- **Deity**: Venus Verticordia; Fortuna Virilis
- **Ritual**: Women in crowds supplicated Fortuna Virilis, humbler women doing so even in the men's baths (where men had exposed the parts of the body relevant to female favour), wearing myrtle wreaths. Women of rank worshipped Venus Verticordia for concord and a modest life. Ovid: ornaments removed from the statue of Fortuna Virilis, the statue washed and jewels restored, flowers offered; women offered incense to Fortuna Virilis on entering the baths so that she would conceal physical blemishes from men. A spring-cleaning / bathing of the divine statue is implied. Prostitutes, mothers and brides all called to worship.
- **Aition**: Not explicitly stated as a foundation myth. Historical note only: a temple to Venus Verticordia was dedicated on 1 April 114 BC to atone for a case of incest among the Vestal Virgins; the simulacrum had originally been dedicated by Sulpicia, wife of Q. Fulvius Flaccus (cos. IV 209 BC), as the most chaste woman in Rome, following a directive of the Sibylline Books.
- **Participants**: Women of all social classes (honestiores and humiliores); prostitutes; mothers; brides.
- **Page**: pp. 96–97

## Ludi Matri deum Magnae Idaeae / Ludi Megalesiaci (start)
- **Day**: 4 (runs 4–10 April)
- **Fasti status**: C
- **Deity**: Mater Magna (Cybele)
- **Ritual**: Opening ceremony at the Palatine temple with an offering of herbs (moretum), made by a praetor (or aedile in Republican days). Theatrical performances in temporary wooden constructions (plays of Terence and Plautus attested). Horse-racing in Circus Maximus. Procession of the Galli (Oriental eunuch priests) through the streets of Rome carrying Cybele's image on a litter; they were self-wounded, sang hymns in Greek to drums and clashing cymbals, wore effeminate dress, and sought alms. No Roman citizen could walk in the procession or take part in the cult. In the evening of the 4th, patrician families held banquets (mutitationes) at which senatorial expenditure was capped at 120 asses by decree of 161 BC.
- **Aition**: Cult introduced during the Hannibalic War (204 BC): the sacred black stone of Cybele was brought from Phrygia and arrived in Rome on 4 April. Romans hoped to gain divine help against Hannibal and to stress their supposed Trojan origin. Temple on Palatine dedicated 10 April 191 BC.
- **Participants**: Praetor/aedile (ceremony); Galli (priests); senators (reserved front seats at theatre); patrician clubs (sodalitates) for evening banquets; general public.
- **Page**: pp. 97–100

## Fortunae Publicae Citeriori in Colle
- **Day**: 5
- **Fasti status**: N
- **Deity**: Fortuna Publica (Citerior); related to Fortuna Primigenia on Quirinal
- **Ritual**: Temple dedication anniversary. "On this day of old the temple of Fortuna Publica was dedicated on the hill of Quirinus" (Ovid, F.4.375). One of three Fortuna temples in the area of the Three Fortunes; one near the Colline Gate. The Praenestine Fortuna had a great oracular shrine at Praeneste with inscribed oak tablets drawn at random from a chest.
- **Aition**: Not stated.
- **Participants**: Not stated.
- **Page**: p. 101

## Matri deum Magnae Idaeae in Palatio (culmination of Megalesia)
- **Day**: 10
- **Fasti status**: N
- **Deity**: Mater Magna (Cybele)
- **Ritual**: Culmination of the Megalesia. "Because on that day the temple was dedicated to her" (Praenestine calendar). Great Games in the Circus (ludi in Circo). A procession of statues of the gods paraded around the Circus Maximus before the chariot races began: Victory, Neptune, Mars, Apollo, Minerva, Ceres, Bacchus, Pollux and Castor, Venus — setting out from the Capitol, through the Forum and Velabrum to the Forum Boarium, then into the Circus Maximus. Praetor signalled the start of chariot races.
- **Aition**: Temple dedicated on this day, 10 April 191 BC.
- **Participants**: Praetor; public spectators.
- **Page**: pp. 101–102

## Ludi Cereri / Cerialia (start)
- **Day**: 12–19 April (III ID. APR. – XIII KAL. MAI.)
- **Fasti status**: NP (on final day, 19 April, per calendars)
- **Deity**: Ceres, Liber, Libera
- **Ritual**: Games (Ludi Ceriales) in the Circus Maximus, not the Circus Flaminius. Culminated on 19 April with ludi in Circo. On preceding days lesser entertainments. Fordicidia on the 15th fell in the middle. Foundation date unknown, but established before 202 BC.
- **Aition**: Not stated.
- **Participants**: Public; Games organized by aediles.
- **Page**: p. 102

## Iovi Victori, Iovi Libertati
- **Day**: 13 (Ides of April)
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Jupiter Victor; Jupiter Libertas; Jupiter Capitolinus (general Ides observance)
- **Ritual**: The Ides of each month were sacred to Jupiter and were feriae publicae. A white sheep (ovis idulis) was led along the Sacred Way through the Forum up to the temple of Jupiter Capitolinus and sacrificed by the flamen Dialis. Other temples of Jupiter received special attention on their anniversary days. Temple of Jupiter Victor on the Palatine (vowed by Fabius Rullianus at the battle of Sentinum, 295 BC). Temple of Jupiter Libertas on the Aventine (probably predates a temple of Libertas dedicated by Ti. Gracchus c. 238 BC).
- **Aition**: Jupiter Victor: temple vowed at battle of Sentinum, 295 BC.
- **Participants**: Flamen Dialis.
- **Page**: p. 102

## FORDICIDIA
- **Day**: 15
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Tellus (Terra Mater)
- **Ritual**: Sacrifice of pregnant cows (fordae / hordae) to Tellus to promote the fertility of land and flocks. One cow sacrificed on the Capitol by the pontiffs; one in each of the thirty curiae (the only other festival organized on the basis of the curiae was the Fornacalia). Attendants of the senior Vestal Virgin tore the unborn calves from their mothers' wombs and burnt them. Vestals kept the ashes for use at the Parilia on 21 April. By sympathetic magic the fertility of the cows was to pass into the corn growing in the womb of the earth.
- **Aition**: Not explicitly stated. Described as one of the earliest Roman festivals; no founding myth given.
- **Participants**: Pontiffs (Capitol sacrifice); thirty curiae (each with one cow); attendants of the senior Vestal Virgin; Vestal Virgins (kept ashes).
- **Page**: pp. 102–103

## CERIALIA
- **Day**: 19 (final day; Games ran 12–19)
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Ceres, Liber, Libera
- **Ritual**: Games in the Circus Maximus (ludi in Circo on the 19th). White robes required of all attendees. Most striking cult act: foxes with burning brands tied to their tails let loose in the Circus Maximus. Rural celebration: farmers offered spelt and salt (mola salsa?), incense on old hearths; sacrificial victim led three times around the young crops. Evening: plebeian families exchanged hospitality and dined together (mirroring the patrician Megalesia dinners). Temple of Ceres on the Aventine (dedicated 493 BC), a centre of plebeian interests, contained archives of plebeian aediles and copies of senatus consulta; decorated by Greek artists.
- **Aition**: Not explicitly stated for the Games (foundation before 202 BC). For Ceres' temple: dedicated 493 BC at a time of great tension between patricians and plebeians; cult possibly introduced from Campania or Sicily. For the fox rite: Ovid offers an explanation from Carseoli (a fox was ritually burnt as warning to vermin) but the original purpose is described as obscure.
- **Participants**: White-robed public (Games); flamen Cerialis (Ceres had her own priest); plebeian families (evening dinners); rural folk.
- **Page**: pp. 102–104

## PARILIA (Roma condita)
- **Day**: 21
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Pales (sex uncertain; possibly two Pales — Antiate calendar for 7 July refers to Palibus duobus)
- **Ritual**: Agricultural festival to purify and protect flocks. (a) Rural celebration (Ovid, F.4.735ff.): Sheep-fold decorated with leafy branches and a wreath; cleansed with water and swept at earliest dawn; sheep fumigated with sulphur; fire of olive and pine wood kindled with crackling laurel branches for good omen; offerings of millet cakes, food, pails of milk. Shepherd prayed to Pales facing east (four times), seeking protection for himself and flocks and forgiveness for inadvertent trespasses on sacred ground; Pales asked to ward off disease and wolves and grant prosperity. Shepherd washed hands in dew, drank milk and heated wine, then leapt through the burning bonfire; sheep may also have passed through the fires. (b) Urban celebration: ashes of the unborn calves from the Fordicidia and blood of the October Horse (sacrificed previous 15 October) were mixed together and thrown on burning bean-straws; worshippers leapt through the flames. These relics had been preserved by the Vestal Virgins. Bean-straw distributed freely; limited supply of relics. Official cult conducted by the Rex Sacrorum. General festivity throughout the city; merry-making and drunken crowd leaping over burning hay (Propertius 4.4.75f.). (c) Also: 21 April was Rome's birthday, the anniversary of her foundation in 753 BC. Special Games had been decreed to honour Caesar's victory at Munda (45 BC); the calendar note Feriae coronatis omnibus (Caer., Esq.) relates to garlands worn for Caesar, not to the original Parilia.
- **Aition**: No explicit founding myth given for the Parilia itself. Rome's foundation on this day (753 BC) is the aition for its status as Roma condita. Temple to Pales or two Pales dedicated by M. Atilius Regulus, 267 BC, probably on the Palatine.
- **Participants**: Shepherds and rural folk (private country celebration); Rex Sacrorum (official urban cult); Vestal Virgins (custodians of relics); all Romans (private urban celebrations).
- **Page**: pp. 103–106

## VINALIA PRIORA (Vinalia Erucinae)
- **Day**: 23
- **Fasti status**: F (Ant. mai.)
- **Deity**: Jupiter (primary, per Varro); Venus Erucina (per Antiate calendar and calendars; later association)
- **Ritual**: Wine of the previous year drawn and offered in libation to Jupiter; only thereafter could this wine (calpar) be tasted by men. A formal public ceremony of wine-tasting / wine-offering. Prostitutes sacrificed to Venus Erucina (temple outside the Colline Gate, dedicated 181 BC on 23 April). The Flamen Dialis officially commenced the vintage (auspicatur vindemiam) and sacrificed a lamb to Jupiter; then plucked a bunch of grapes himself — but this probably applies to the Vinalia Rustica on 19 August rather than 23 April. General feasting and drinking implied; farmers brought new wine to market.
- **Aition**: Explicitly stated by Ovid (F.4; also Praenestine calendar note): When Aeneas was at war with Turnus king of the Rutuli, the Etruscan king Mezentius offered support to Turnus in exchange for the wine of the next Latin vintage (or all first-fruits). The Latins vowed to give this tribute to Jupiter if he granted them victory. Mezentius was killed; Jupiter received his annual tribute of wine. "Hence the day is called the Vinalia; Jupiter claims it for his own and rejoices to be present at his own feast" (Ovid).
- **Participants**: General population (wine-offering and tasting); prostitutes (sacrifice to Venus Erucina); Flamen Dialis (official vintage commencement).
- **Page**: pp. 106–108

## ROBIGALIA
- **Day**: 25
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Robigus / Robigo (sex uncertain to the Romans; spirit of robigo: blight, red rust or mildew)
- **Ritual**: Procession to the grove of Robigus at the fifth milestone on the Via Claudia (lest mildew harm the corn). The flamen Quirinalis led the rite; he wore white robes, carried a napkin in his right hand, cast wine, incense, and the entrails of a dog and a sheep on to a sacrificial fire (Columella: the dog was a sucking puppy, lacteus catulus), and prayed to Robigus to protect the crops. Running Games (cursores maiores minoresque: men's and boys' events?) also held, confirmed in the Praenestine calendar note (Sacrificum et ludi cursoribus maioribus minoribusque fiunt). Festus records that red (rufae) dogs were sacrificed near the Porta Catularia during the Dogstar period for the same protective purpose; this sacrifice was not on a fixed day but may have coincided with the Robigalia.
- **Aition**: Institution attributed to Numa by Pliny (NH); Tertullian says Numa "instituted games for Mars and Robigus." The fifth-milestone location perhaps dates to when that boundary marked the edge of Roman territory.
- **Participants**: Flamen Quirinalis; white-robed procession; runners (Games).
- **Page**: pp. 108–110

## Ludi Florae / FLORALIA (start)
- **Day**: 27 (Julian 28), iv KAL. MAI.
- **Fasti status**: C
- **Deity**: Flora (ancient Italian goddess of flowers and vegetation in general; had her own flamen Floralis)
- **Ritual**: Temple of Flora on the lower slopes of the Aventine dedicated on this day. Theatrical performances (increasingly licentious; mime, strip-tease, naked performances by prostitutes). Circus Games with normal sports plus two special items: (1) Hares and goats let loose in the Circus (both regarded as salacious and fertile). (2) Vetches, beans and lupins scattered among the crowd (fertility symbols at a spring festival). Multi-coloured garments worn (contrast with the white required at the Cerialia). Night illumination (night-shows in theatres). Prostitutes claimed the Floralia as their feast. Plebeian aediles were responsible and provided money from fines for encroachments on public land. Under the Empire the Games lasted six days (theatrical performances ending with circus Games and a sacrifice to Flora); possibly extended to more than one day as early as Caesar's time (he added an extra day on 26 April when reforming the calendar in 45 BC).
- **Aition**: Scullard states the temple was built after a drought in 241 or 238 BC led the Romans to consult the Sibylline Books, which prescribed the building of a temple. Games not held annually at first; made annual in 173 BC due to frequent damage to crops. The Praenestine note reads: "Eodem die aedes Florae, quae rebus florescendis praeest, dedicata est propter sterilitatem frugum" ("because of the barrenness of crops"). This is the founding reason given.
- **Participants**: Plebeian aediles (responsible); prostitutes (claimed the festival as their feast); general public; Cicero as aedile in 69 BC was responsible for the Games.
- **Page**: pp. 110–111

## Feriae Latinae (Feriae Conceptivae — moveable feast)
- **Day**: Moveable; not fixed in the calendar. Normally held before the consuls departed for the campaign season in spring; sometimes a little later. Described under April as typical timing.
- **Fasti status**: Conceptivae (moveable); date fixed at start of each year by incoming consuls
- **Deity**: Jupiter Latiaris (on the Alban Mount)
- **Ritual**: Delegates from Latin cities (local magistrates) gathered on the summit of the Alban Mount (~13 miles south-east of Rome) to worship Jupiter Latiaris. (1) Main sacrifice: a pure white heifer that had never known the yoke, offered on behalf of the whole League. The Roman consuls conducted the sacrifice (after Rome assumed leadership of the Latin League). (2) Libation of milk (not wine) by the leader; other cities brought sheep or cheese and other agricultural produce. (3) Flesh of the victim divided among all delegates who zealously claimed their portions (carnem petere) to share in a common meal — a sacrament pledging loyalty. (4) Oscilla (little puppets fashioned like human beings) hung in the trees (ancient writers interpreted these as substitutes for earlier human sacrifice, but Scullard is sceptical). (5) Games (Ludi) lasting at least two days, held after the solemn day. (6) Chariot-race on the Capitol in Rome on the main day (Pliny; details disputed). (7) A fire-signal from the summit of the Alban Mount blazed forth at the end of the festival. (8) A praefectus urbi feriarum Latinarum causa was appointed to govern Rome during the consuls' absence. All senior Roman magistrates accompanied the consuls to the Alban Mount. The festival lasted over one thousand years (to at least 394 AD).
- **Aition**: Originally a festival of a group of Latin cities (possibly led by Alba Longa) worshipping Jupiter Latiaris as the god of their League. After King Tullus Hostilius destroyed Alba Longa (traditionally 7th century BC), Rome succeeded to its position. The Etruscan rulers of Rome may have reorganized the League. The Latin League was formally dissolved 338 BC and rearranged under Roman control.
- **Participants**: Roman consuls and all senior magistrates; delegates from all Latin cities (local magistrates); praefectus urbi (stayed in Rome); Roman People (Games and chariot-race in Rome).
- **Page**: pp. 111–115

## Summary

Scullard covers fifteen named occasions (including the Megalesia as both its start and its culmination separately, and the Cerialia as both a start-of-games entry and the final day):

| Day | Festival | Fasti | Deity |
|-----|----------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Veneralia | F | Venus Verticordia, Fortuna Virilis |
| 4–10 | Ludi Megalesiaci / Megalesia | C | Mater Magna (Cybele) |
| 5 | Fortunae Publicae Citeriori in Colle | N | Fortuna Publica |
| 10 | Matri deum Magnae Idaeae in Palatio | N | Mater Magna |
| 12–19 | Ludi Cereri (Cerialia, games span) | — | Ceres |
| 13 | Iovi Victori, Iovi Libertati (Ides) | NP | Jupiter Victor, Jupiter Libertas |

| 19 | Cerialia (final day) | NP | Ceres, Liber, Libera |

| 23 | Vinalia Priora | F | Jupiter (primary); Venus Erucina |
| 25 | Robigalia | NP | Robigus / Robigo |
| 27 | Ludi Florae / Floralia (start) | C | Flora |
| moveable | Feriae Latinae | conceptivae | Jupiter Latiaris |

**Explicit aitia given**: Veneralia (temple founded 114 BC for Vestal incest atonement; statue by Sulpicia per Sibylline Books); Megalesia (sacred stone arrived 4 April 204 BC, Hannibalic War); Vinalia (Aeneas–Turnus–Mezentius myth, per Ovid); Floralia (drought 241/238 BC, Sibylline Books, sterilitatem frugum — stated in Praenestine calendar note); Feriae Latinae (Latin League origin, destruction of Alba Longa).

**Key ritual details of note for the app**:
- Fordicidia ashes + October Horse blood → Parilia bonfires (cross-festival link across 15 April and 21 April, and 15 October).
- Cerialia's fox rite (burning brands on tails) is described as cruel and obscure; Scullard does not endorse any specific explanation.
- Robigalia dog sacrifice: entrails of dog + sheep by flamen Quirinalis; sucking puppy (Columella); not on a fixed day for the Porta Catularia version.
- Feriae Latinae is a moveable feast — not calendar-fixed; white heifer victim; milk libation (not wine); flesh divided among all delegates.
