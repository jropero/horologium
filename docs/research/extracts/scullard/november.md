## Ludi Victoriae Sullanae (culmination)
- **Day**: 1 (KAL. NOV.)
- **Fasti status**: F
- **Deity**: Victoria / Victoria Sullana
- **Ritual**: Culminating day (Circenses) of the Ludi Victoriae Sullanae, a multi-day games sequence running 26 October – 1 November. The Circenses on 1 November marked the anniversary of the Battle of the Colline Gate. Staged by praetors.
- **Aition**: Established 81 BC by Sulla to celebrate his victory over the Samnites at the Colline Gate. Originally called simply *Victoriae*; later renamed *Sullanae* to distinguish them from Caesar's Victory Games. First holding attested by a denarius of 59 BC: "Sex. Nonius praetor (in 81 BC) ludos Victoriae primus fecit."
- **Participants**: Praetors (organisers); general public (spectators).
- **Page**: p. 196

## Ludi Plebeii
- **Day**: 4–17 (PR. NON. NOV. – xiv [Julian xv] KAL. DEC.)
- **Fasti status**: not stated in this entry (games period)
- **Deity**: Jupiter (central Iovis epulum on the Ides; pompa circensis)
- **Ritual**: Second in importance only to the Ludi Romani. Days 4–12: scenic performances (ludi scaenici). Day 13: Feast of Jupiter (Iovis epulum) on the Ides divides scenic from circus days. Day 14: Equorum probatio. Days 15–17: Circus Games (ludi circenses), first in the Circus Flaminius, later transferred to the Circus Maximus. Pompa circensis with processional solemnity, ribaldry and excitement, closely paralleling the Romani procedure (Dionysius' description cited, p. 184).
- **Aition**: Probably established 220 BC when C. Flaminius was censor and built the Circus Flaminius. First explicitly mentioned 216 BC. Administered by plebeian aediles (contrast Ludi Romani, run by curule aediles), reflecting Flaminius' populist character.
- **Participants**: Plebeian aediles (administrators); senators (at the epulum); general public.
- **Page**: pp. 196–197

## Feriae Iovi / Iovis epulum
- **Day**: 13 (ID. NOV.)
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Jupiter
- **Ritual**: A solemn public feast (epulum) of Jupiter on the Ides of November, forming the centrepiece of the Ludi Plebeii — separating the nine scenic days from the three Circus days. Senators gathered for the solemn celebration (epulum Iovis).
- **Aition**: First explicitly mentioned 213 BC, when "the Plebeian Games were repeated (instaurati) for two days, and on account of the Games a banquet for Jupiter was held" (Livy 25.2.10). Scullard notes it may also have been a feature of the Ludi Romani on 13 September.
- **Participants**: Senators.
- **Page**: p. 197

## Feronia
- **Day**: 13 (ID. NOV.) — Fasti entry: "Feroniae in Campo" (Arv.); "Feroniae, Fortunae Primigeniae, [Pie]tati" (Ant. mai.)
- **Fasti status**: NP (part of the 13 November NP day)
- **Deity**: Feronia
- **Ritual**: Festival at Feronia's grove and temple in the Campus Martius. The grove/temple at Rome is attested only in the calendars. Her placement in the middle of the Plebeian Games suggests the cult preceded the games and may originate in a primitive fair (as at Lucus Feroniae). Freedwomen (libertinae) contributed money for a gift to Feronia; freewomen (matronae) contributed separately to Juno Regina. At Terracina, slaves could take sanctuary at her altar and receive the cap of freedom (pilleus) on their shaved heads on a seat inscribed "Bene meriti servi sedeant, surgant liberi."
- **Aition**: Not explicitly stated. Scullard notes her name may be Etruscan (Varro considered it Sabine; Varro also equated her with Libertas — "Feroniam quasi Fidoniam"). First mentioned at Rome 217 BC when the Sibylline Books ordered expiatory offerings. She may originally have been an agricultural goddess (received first-fruits at Lucus Feroniae) who later acquired special association with freedmen.
- **Participants**: Freedwomen (libertinae) with monetary contributions; general cult participants.
- **Page**: pp. 197–198

## [Pietati] (13 November)
- **Day**: 13 (ID. NOV.) — Fasti entry: "[Pie]tati" (Ant. mai.; supplement, not fully preserved)
- **Fasti status**: NP (part of the 13 November NP day)
- **Deity**: Pietas
- **Ritual**: Dies natalis of the temple of Pietas in the Forum Holitorium. Temple contained a gilded statue of M'. Acilius Glabrio — the first of its kind in Rome. The temple was destroyed 44 BC for the Theatre of Marcellus. Connected with the story of a daughter who suckled her imprisoned parent, and linked to the nearby Columna Lactaria where infants in need of milk were brought.
- **Aition**: Temple vowed by M'. Acilius Glabrio at Thermopylae (191 BC) and dedicated by his son ten years later. Personification of Pietas attested ca. that period in Plautus' Asinaria and Curculio. The name supplement "Pietati" is probable since Pietas is mentioned alongside Fortuna Primigenia in a 2nd-century AD inscription.
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 198

## Summary

November is the leanest festival month in the Roman calendar (Scullard's characterisation). Its religious content is dominated by two extended games periods:

| Day(s) | Name | Status | Deity/Focus |
|--------|------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Ludi Victoriae Sullanae (culmination) | F | Victoria |
| 4–17 | Ludi Plebeii | — | Jupiter (central epulum) |
| 8 | Mundus patet (3rd opening) | C, dies religiosus | chthonic |
| 13 | Feriae Iovi / Iovis epulum | NP | Jupiter |
| 13 | Feronia | NP | Feronia |
| 13 | Fortunae Primigeniae | NP | Fortuna Primigenia |
| 13 | [Pietati] | NP | Pietas |
| 14 | Equorum probatio | F, dies religiosus | — |
| 18–20 | Mercatus | — | — |

**Key observations:**
- The Ides of November (13th) cluster the most sacred content: the Iovis epulum as the pivot of the Ludi Plebeii, plus the festivals of Feronia, Fortuna Primigenia, and Pietas all sharing the NP day.
- Feronia is the most extensively discussed deity: her cult blended agricultural first-fruits with freedmen's liberation rites, and her Campus Martius temple likely predates the Plebeian Games.
- The mundus is opened three times a year (24 Aug., 5 Oct., 8 Nov.), each a dies religiosus; Scullard gives the full account at August and cross-references here.
- Rustic calendars prescribe wheat/barley sowing and tree trenching; the month is under the care of Diana (noted in the month introduction).
- Scullard does not record any major named festival unique to November beyond the games; the month's thinness is explicitly noted.
