# Augustus — Scullard (1981)
**PDF pages used**: 169–184
**Source**: H.H. Scullard, *Festivals and Ceremonies of the Roman Republic* (Thames & Hudson, 1981)

## Month introduction

The month (Mensis Sextilis) was under the protection of Ceres. Rustic calendars required the preparing of stakes, collecting harvest and wheat, burning the coarse part of the flax. Sacrifices were owing to Spes, Salus and Diana; it was the month of the Volcanalia. Many rites were performed near the Aventine, Circus Maximus and the bank of the Tiber.

## Spes in Foro Holitorio
- **Day**: 1
- **Fasti status**: F
- **Deity**: Spes (Hope)
- **Ritual**: Temple dedication day. A temple of Spes was built in the Forum Holitorium during the First Punic War by A. Atilius Calatinus. Probably identifiable with the middle of three temples now below S. Nicola in Carcere. An older temple of Spes Vetus existed on the Esquiline.
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 169–170

## Victoria / Victoria Virgo in Palatio
- **Day**: 1
- **Fasti status**: F (same day as Spes)
- **Deity**: Victoria, Victoria Virgo
- **Ritual**: Two temples of Victoria stood on the Palatine. One dedicated by L. Postumius Megellus in 294 BC from fines exacted as curule aedile, preceded by an ancient shrine of Victoria. The other, to Victoria Virgo, nearby, dedicated by M. Porcius Cato in 193 BC.
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 170

## Supplicia Canum
- **Day**: 3
- **Fasti status**: C
- **Deity**: (no named deity; associated with Iuventus and Summanus, temples flanking the procession route)
- **Ritual**: One or more dogs were crucified alive on a cross of elder-wood between the temples of Iuventus and Summanus and apparently carried round in procession. A goose (or geese), adorned with purple and gold, was carried in a litter to honour its wakeful predecessors. Since the two temples were near the Circus Maximus, the procession may have paraded inside the Circus.
- **Aition**: Explicitly stated — the temple dogs were asleep when the Gauls tried to assault the Capitol (geese gave the alarm). The dogs were punished annually for their ancestors' failure; the geese were honoured for their ancestors' vigilance.
- **Participants**: not stated (civic/public procession implied)
- **Page**: p. 170–171

## Saluti in Colle Quirinale sacrificium publicum
- **Day**: 5
- **Fasti status**: F
- **Deity**: Salus
- **Ritual**: Public sacrifice at the temple of Salus on the Quirinal. The temple was dedicated in 302 BC by C. Iunius Bubulcus when dictator; it contained famous paintings by C. Fabius Pictor. Part of the Quirinal was called collis Salutaris, indicating an early cult. Under the Empire Salus became Salus Publica populi Romani. Dies natalis of the colony of Brundisium and of this temple, as noted by Cicero (Att., 45 BC).
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: Public (sacrificium publicum)
- **Page**: p. 171

## Solis Indigitis in Colle Quirinale sacrificium publicum
- **Day**: 9
- **Fasti status**: C
- **Deity**: Sol Indiges
- **Ritual**: Public sacrifice to Sol Indiges on the Quirinal. King Tatius said to have dedicated altars to Sol and Luna. Quintilian refers to a Pulvinar Solis near the temple of Quirinus. Sol Indiges also had a shrine at Lavinium. The sun had another temple near the Circus Maximus. The major elevation of Sol's cult came only in the third century AD.
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: Public (sacrificium publicum)
- **Page**: p. 171

## Herculi Invicto ad Circum Maximum
- **Day**: 12
- **Fasti status**: C
- **Deity**: Hercules Invictus (and on 13th, Hercules Invictus ad Portam Trigeminam)
- **Ritual**: On 12 August the praetor urbanus sacrificed a heifer to Hercules and poured a libation from a special cup known as the skyphos of Hercules. Merchants paid tithes of profit; victorious generals offered tithes of booty; private individuals made offerings. The cult required no generalis invocatio (no reference to other gods during the sacrifice); dogs and women were excluded from the Ara Maxima. No part of the sacrificial animal might be removed from the precinct — what was not burnt on the altar was eaten on the spot. Hercules could receive all food and drink (Herculi autem omnia esculenta, poculenta). The tithes may have funded a free public feast. The original cult at the Ara Maxima was controlled by the Potitii and Pinarii gentes until the State took it over in 312 BC. Celebrated in the Greek manner, with head uncovered but crowned with laurel.
- **Aition**: Altar said to have been dedicated by Hercules himself, or by Evander after Hercules killed the legendary Cacus. (Explicitly stated as legendary foundation.)
- **Participants**: Praetor urbanus (official sacrifice); merchants, generals, private worshippers; women excluded from Ara Maxima; no dogs.
- **Page**: p. 171–173

## Veneri Victrici, Honori, Virtuti, Felicitati in theatro marmoreo
- **Day**: 12
- **Fasti status**: C (same entry)
- **Deity**: Venus Victrix, Honos, Virtus, Felicitas (and possibly Vesta or Valetudo — V uncertain)
- **Ritual**: Dies natalis of Pompey's stone theatre (55 BC). Pompey built a temple of Venus Victrix at the top of the central section of the theatre's seating so that the seats appeared as temple steps and the whole could be dedicated as a temple rather than a theatre. The other nearby temples or altars to Honos, Virtus, Felicitas and V are not precisely known.
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 173

## Dianae in Aventino
- **Day**: 13
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Diana
- **Ritual**: The most important festival of the day, involving a general holiday for all slaves, male and female, probably after the toil of the harvest. All Romans had to grant their slaves the day off; Diana's cult was one of the few that slaves could attend. Roman women made a particular practice of washing their hair on this day (Plutarch speculates this began with slave women and spread to free-born women). Women from Rome came in processions with burning torches from Aricia (Diana's sanctuary at Lake Nemi). The Aventine temple contained a wooden statue of Diana of Ephesus and bronze copies of old laws; one of the oldest sun-dials in Rome was on its walls. The Aventine was sometimes known as the Collis Dianae.
- **Aition**: Temple founded in the sixth century by King Servius Tullius, possibly as a political move to transfer or rival the headquarters of the Latin League centred near Aricia. The slave-holiday was falsely linked to a popular connection between servus ("slave") and King Servius. (Both stated as traditions, the political aition is scholarly inference.)
- **Participants**: All Romans (obliged to free their slaves); women (torchlit processions); slaves.
- **Page**: p. 173–175

## Vortumno in Aventino
- **Day**: 13
- **Fasti status**: NP (same day)
- **Deity**: Vortumnus (Vertumnus) — Etruscan god
- **Ritual**: Worship of Vortumnus at his temple in the Vicus Loreti Maioris on the north-west part of the Aventine (Loretum = ancient laurel-grove). The temple probably built by M. Fulvius Flaccus following his triumph in 264 BC (capture of Volsinii). The temple contained a portrait of Flaccus in triumphal robes. A statue of Vortumnus also stood in the Vicus Tuscus.
- **Aition**: The cult was brought from Volsinii (Etruscan city) captured by Fulvius Flaccus. The Romans connected the name with vertere ("to turn"), though the actual Etruscan etymology is uncertain.
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 175

## Fortunae Equestri
- **Day**: 13
- **Fasti status**: NP (same day; attested in Fasti Antiates alone)
- **Deity**: Fortuna Equestris
- **Ritual**: Dies natalis of a temple vowed by Q. Fulvius Flaccus in 180 BC during his campaign against the Celtiberians, dedicated seven years later to Fortuna in her relation to the Equites. Located "over against the stone theatre" (probably the theatre of Pompey in the Circus Flaminius). The temple did not survive the Republic.
- **Aition**: Vowed during the Celtiberian campaign.
- **Participants**: Knights (Equites)
- **Page**: p. 175

## PORTUNALIA
- **Day**: 17
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Portunus
- **Ritual**: Portunus had his own special flamen (flamen Portunalis), whose only recorded function was to anoint the armour of Quirinus with oil. Possibly on this day old wooden keys were hardened or purified in fire — the harvest was then filling barns and store-houses. The temple of Portunus is almost certainly the so-called Fortuna Virilis temple in the Forum Boarium. The area around the temple (Portunium) was frequented by flower-sellers.
- **Aition**: Originally Portunus was a god of doors (portus in early usage); alternatively he presided over a ferry across the Tiber. Later portus shifted to "harbour" and he became god of ports.
- **Participants**: Flamen Portunalis; public
- **Page**: p. 176–177

## Iano ad Theatrum Marcelli
- **Day**: 17
- **Fasti status**: NP (same day; later dies natalis shifted to 18 October after Augustan/Tiberian restoration)
- **Deity**: Janus
- **Ritual**: Temple of Janus built by C. Duilius after his defeat of the Carthaginians at Mylae in 260 BC, dedicated on the day of the Portunalia (17 August). After restoration begun by Augustus and completed by Tiberius in AD 17, its dedication day became 18 October. Located in the Forum Holitorium by the theatre of Marcellus.
- **Aition**: Vowed/built by Duilius after victory at Mylae (260 BC).
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 176–177

## VINALIA (Vinalia Rustica)
- **Day**: 19
- **Fasti status**: F
- **Deity**: Jupiter (primary); Venus Obsequens (associated)
- **Ritual**: The second festival of wine (the first being the Vinalia Priora of 23 April). The ceremonies were designed to gain protection of the growing wines. The flamen Dialis officially announced the vintage (auspicatur vindemoniam), a practice still current in Varro's day. A temple of Venus Obsequens near the Circus Maximus was also celebrated; it was built by Q. Fabius Maximus Gurges from fines imposed on women convicted of adultery (begun 295 BC, possibly as a thank-offering for his father's victory over the Samnites at Sentinum). Not recorded in Fasti Antiates under this date for Veneri.
- **Aition**: The Vinalia Rustica was the agricultural complement to the Vinalia Priora — the spring festival opened the previous harvest wine; the August festival sought protection for the growing season's wine.
- **Participants**: Flamen Dialis (announces vintage); public
- **Page**: p. 177

## Venus Libitina in Luco Libitinae
- **Day**: 19
- **Fasti status**: not stated (not in Fasti Antiates)
- **Deity**: Venus Libitina
- **Ritual**: A temple to Venus Libitina dedicated on 19 August in the Lucus Libitinae on the Esquiline. This area was the headquarters of undertakers (libitinarii) where lists of the dead were kept and funerals arranged. Plutarch speculates on the connection: perhaps a philosophical device (instituted by Numa) to eliminate repugnance of funerals, or a reminder that one goddess presides over births and deaths. Varro distinguishes Venus Libitina from Venus Libentina (goddess of sensual pleasure).
- **Aition**: not stated (Plutarch speculates only)
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 177

## CONSUALIA
- **Day**: 21
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Consus (god of the store-bin / harvested grain)
- **Ritual**: The flamen Quirinalis was in charge, with the Vestal Virgins in attendance (Consus had no flamen of his own). The soil was removed from the underground altar of Consus in the Circus Maximus. Consus was honoured with sacrifice and burnt offering of first-fruits. Horse and chariot races were held. Horses and asses were garlanded and allowed to rest. This festival celebrated the end of the harvest (a second Consualia on 15 December celebrated the end of the autumn sowing). A temple to Consus on the Aventine was vowed or built by L. Papirius Cursor, probably in 272 BC.
- **Aition**: Explicitly connected in tradition with the Rape of the Sabine Women — Romulus is said to have attracted the Sabines to Rome for the first celebration of the Consualia, so engrossing the men that the Romans could steal their women. However, Scullard notes this connection with the Sabine women "remains unexplained." Consus's connection with horses probably developed in the Etruscan period (Etruscans associated the underground altar with the dead; the horse was a funerary animal). The races later created a false link with Neptune Equestris.
- **Participants**: Flamen Quirinalis; Vestal Virgins; general public (horse and chariot races)
- **Page**: p. 177–178

## VOLCANALIA
- **Day**: 23
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Vulcan (primary); also Maia, Nymphs, Ops Opifera, Hora Quirini
- **Ritual**: Primary ceremonies at the Volcanal (Area Volcani) at the foot of the Capitol (or Lapis Niger area). The people (populus) "drive animals into a fire as substitutes for themselves (pro se)." Live fish were also offered to Vulcan as substitutes for human souls (fish being beyond the fire-god's reach while in the river). Vulcan received additional worship in the Circus Flaminius. Maia received sacrifice "supra Comitium" (i.e. in the Area Vulcani above the Comitium). A sacrifice to the Nymphs is recorded only in the Fasti Arvales. Ops Opifera also received worship (four days before her own Opiconsivia on 25 August). Hora, cult-partner of Vulcan, received worship "in Colle Volcano"; virtually nothing else is known about her.
- **Aition**: The antiquity of Vulcan is attested by the existence of a flamen Volcanalis. His name is not Latin; he is unlikely to have been Etruscan (the Etruscan fire-god was Sethlans); he has been compared with the Cretan deity Velchanos. Origin remains uncertain.
- **Participants**: Populus (individuals driving animals and fish into fire); public
- **Page**: p. 178–180

## Lunae in Graecostasi
- **Day**: 24
- **Fasti status**: C (also marked Dies religiosus)
- **Deity**: Luna
- **Ritual**: Sacrifice to Luna at the Graecostasis, a tribunal or platform between the Comitium and Forum. Luna's main temple was on the Aventine (dies natalis 31 March). The Graecostasis sacrifices are mentioned only in the Fasti Pinciani and may have been a late institution.
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 180–181

## Mundus patet
- **Day**: 24
- **Fasti status**: not in any calendar (no calendar includes "mundus patet"); the day is marked comitialis (C) in the fasti
- **Deity**: Dis and Proserpina (associated later); originally possibly Ceres
- **Ritual**: The mundus was a vaulted ritual pit divided into two parts, with a cover (possibly called lapis manalis) removed three times per year: 24 August, 5 October, 8 November. Removal of the lid (regarded as the Gate of Hell, ostium Orci) allowed the spirits (manes) of the underworld to emerge and roam the streets. The day was religiosus: no public business, no battle, no army levy, no ships to sail, no marriages. The mundus was originally probably a pit to store seed-corn at harvest (Cereris mundus), with the underworld associations being later Graeco-Etruscan accretions.
- **Aition**: Tradition that Romulus put first-fruits into a pit in the Comitium together with soil from each settler's homeland, possibly confused with the mundus Cereris. Whether there were two mundi remains uncertain.
- **Participants**: General population affected by the dies religiosus restrictions
- **Page**: p. 180–181

## OPICONSIVIA
- **Day**: 25
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Ops Consiva (Ops = Plenty; regarded as the Earth, terra)
- **Ritual**: At her sacrarium in the Regia, only the Vestal Virgins and the State-priest (wearing a white veil, the suffibulum) might enter. A special broad bronze vessel called a praefericulum was used. According to Festus she was worshipped in the Regia because she supplied the human race with all its resources (omnes opes); her sacrarium corresponds to the store-cupboard (penus) of the king, tended by the Vestal Virgins as symbolic "daughters." Ops was also commemorated at her later temple on the Capitol.
- **Aition**: not stated (the antiquity of the cult is noted but no founding legend given)
- **Participants**: Vestal Virgins; Rex Sacrorum (or State-priest with suffibulum); all others excluded
- **Page**: p. 181

## VOLTURNALIA
- **Day**: 27
- **Fasti status**: NP
- **Deity**: Volturnus (river or wind god; regarded as father of the fountain-deity Juturna)
- **Ritual**: Sacrifice to Volturnus. The cult was very ancient — he had his own flamen (flamen Volturnalis) — but Roman antiquarians knew little about him by the late Republic. He was identified with Eurus (the south-east wind) and shared his name with a river in Campania. As a wind he may have threatened the growing vines and needed propitiation.
- **Aition**: If the name derives from volvere ("to roll"), it could apply to a river or wind. Alternatively the name may be Etruscan (possible link with the family name Velthuma). Mommsen's suggestion that Volturnus was an early name of the Tiber is noted but not resolved.
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 181–182

---

## Solis et Lunae Circenses
- **Day**: 28
- **Fasti status**: C
- **Deity**: Sol and Luna
- **Ritual**: Dies natalis of the temple of Sol and Luna near the Circus Maximus. Tacitus refers to it as vetus aedem Solis ad Circum. Circus games (circenses) were associated with it but became important only in the later Empire. Tertullian says the Circus was primarily dedicated to the Sun; the statue of the Sun (probably driving a chariot) was on the top of the temple, since it was not thought right to honour the sky-god under a roof.
- **Aition**: not stated
- **Participants**: not stated
- **Page**: p. 182

## Summary

Scullard covers **14 dates** in August, with **20+ distinct cult entries** (several dates carry multiple simultaneous festivals). The month's character is strongly agricultural (harvest end, grain storage) and connected with fire-purification (Volcanalia) and water (Portunalia, Volturnalia). Key features:

- **NP days (dies nefasti publici, major public festivals)**: 13 (Diana/Ides), 17 (Portunalia), 21 (Consualia), 23 (Volcanalia), 25 (Opiconsivia), 27 (Volturnalia) — six NP days in one month, more than any other month in the Roman year.
- **Major religious spectacles**: Supplicia Canum (3 Aug, unique dog-crucifixion procession); Consualia horse-races (21 Aug); Volcanalia fire-offerings (23 Aug).
- **Slave holiday**: 13 August (Diana) was the one day all slaves were guaranteed rest.
- **Three harvest-storage deities**: Consus (grain store), Ops (plenty/earth), Volturnus (wind/river threatening crops).
- **Aitia explicitly stated by Scullard**: Supplicia Canum (Gauls/Capitol geese), Consualia (Rape of Sabines — but noted as unexplained connection), Hercules/Cacus at Ara Maxima, Diana/Servius Tullius founding.
- **Deities with no surviving ritual detail**: Camenae, Fortunae Equestri, Castor/Pollux in Circo Flaminio, Flora (13 Aug), Janus (17 Aug), Luna at Graecostasis.
- **Mundus patet (24 Aug)**: not in any calendar; parenthetical entry. Scullard treats it as a real practice with uncertain original function (agricultural pit vs. underworld gate).
