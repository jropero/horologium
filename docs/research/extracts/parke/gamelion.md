# Parke — Gamelion
Source: H. W. Parke, *Festivals of the Athenians* (1977), printed p. 104–106 / PDF p. 102–104
Extracted: 2026-10-25

---

## Chapter overview

Gamelion meant "the month of marriage". Weddings were the favourite human occasion; on the divine level the month was also the anniversary of the Sacred Marriage of Zeus and Hera (celebrated on the 26th). Parke notes the chapter covers only two festivals: the Hieros Gamos / Gamelia and the Lenaia.

---

### Hieros Gamos / Gamelia

**Day**: 26th of Gamelion (Parke: "celebrated on the 26th of the month as the anniversary")
**Deity**: Zeus and Hera (Sacred Marriage)
**Ritual**: none preserved
**Aition**: The occurrence of the festival on the 26th is presented as the anniversary of the divine marriage; which came first — the prevalence of human weddings in Gamelion making the month seem appropriate, or the festival date inducing people to choose it as auspicious — Parke says is impossible to tell.
**Participants**: unknown
**Sources**: none cited explicitly for this festival
**Notes**: "No details of the festival of the *Hieros Gamos* (Sacred Wedding) or the *Gamelia* are preserved." (fn. 122) The connection between human weddings and the divine model is noted but left unresolved.
**Parke**: printed p. 104 / PDF p. 102

### Lenaia

**Day**: 12th of Gamelion (procession and start of dramatic contests; "lasting several days starting from the 12th")
**Deity**: Dionysus Lenaios (sanctuary of Dionysus Lenaios, the Lenaion, within Athens)
**Ritual**:
1. Procession organised by the Basileus (King archon); by the 4th century at least, also overseen by the overseers of the Eleusinian Mysteries.
2. At the Lenaion, the Daiduchos ("Torch-bearer") holds a torch and calls "Call on the god"; the audience shouts the response: "Son of Semele, Iacchos, giver of wealth."
3. Dramatic contests of comedy and tragedy lasting several days.
4. Probable midnight revel of women devoted to Dionysus (Parke: "we would have to suppose that part of the festival was a midnight revel of women devoted to Dionysus" — flagged as reconstructed from vase-painting, not from literary sources).

**Aition**: none explicitly discussed by Parke.
**Participants**:
- Athenian citizens only. Foreigners were excluded; even the resident aliens (Metoikoi) were excluded — making this "a more native and intimate occasion" (fn. 123).
- The procession included people riding on carts and singing abusive songs (attested in ancient commentaries; Parke notes this feature is too common to the genre to be distinctive, fn. 124).
- Women devotees of Dionysus possibly participated in a nocturnal revel (reconstructed from red-figure vase evidence, c. 480 BC; Parke accepts this with caution).

**Sources**:
- Hesychius — s.v. the phrase from Aristophanes, explaining the Lenaion as "a place in the city of Athens with an extensive enclosure and in it the sanctuary of Dionysus Lenaios in which the dramatic contests of the *Lenaia* were held before the (Dionysiac) theatre was built."
- Aristophanes, *Acharnians* — performed at this festival; provides the evidence that no foreigners were present (fn. 123).
- Plutarch — Delphic women (Thyiades) going up to Parnassus in winter, evidence for maenadic activity in January/February.
- Red-figure vases, c. 480 BC (Naples and others) — scenes of maenads with thyrsos, castanets, tambourines, double flute, torches; Parke follows the identification of Frickenhaus (original) and Deubner that these are Lenaia scenes (fn. 126).

**Notes**:
- **Organisation**: The primitive original organisation by the Basileus (the religious and civic chief) is taken as evidence of the festival's antiquity. The later joining of the overseers of the Eleusinian Mysteries may reflect administrative creep or an attempt by the Eleusinian priesthood "to get a footing in a festival with which they cannot have had any basic connection."
- **Iacchos invocation**: Parke states the Daiduchos's invocation of Iacchos as "Son of Semele, giver of wealth" *cannot* have been an early piece of ritual. Iacchos was a deity invented to personify the triumphant shout of Eleusinian initiates; he was equated with Bacchus due to name resemblance. The identification of Iacchos as son of Semele (the Theban mother of Dionysus) is Eleusinian overlay, as is "giver of wealth" (an epithet of Demeter).
- **Etymology debate**: Parke presents two views — (a) from *lenos* ("wine-vat"), appropriate for Dionysus but awkward for January when grape-treading is long past; (b) from *Lenai*, a name for maenads or infatuated female worshippers of Dionysus, which Parke finds "quite appropriate." Neither is settled.
- **Primitive origins**: The Lenaion is described as "the earliest, and certainly had preceded the introduction of Dionysus Eleuthereus in the mid-sixth century and the establishment of the *City Dionysia*." The Lenaia is therefore characterised as the primitive Attic counterpart of the *Country Dionysia*.
- **Women's midnight revel**: Parke flags this as reconstruction from vase-painting only: "our literary sources do not describe" these ceremonies. The identification with Lenaia scenes is Deubner's (following Frickenhaus), and Parke accepts it as "the best identification" while acknowledging it requires the inference of a midnight revel.
- Deubner attempted further conjectures about the Lenaia which "our literary sources do not describe" — Parke does not endorse them beyond what evidence supports.
