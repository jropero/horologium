### Lenaia

**Month/Day**: Gamelion 12 (Deubner: "höchstwahrscheinlich am 12."; dramatic performances occupied several further days)

**Deity**: Dionysos Lenaios

**Official name**: Διονύσια τὰ ἐπὶ Ληναίω (or Ἐπιληναία Διονύσια)

**Venue**: The Lenaion — a large enclosed precinct (τρίβολον) in the city of Athens; its exact location unknown. Deubner rejects the identification of Dionysos Limnaios with Dionysos Lenaios, and rejects the "field" interpretation derived from a confused scholion on Aristoph. Ach. 202. He places the Lenaion in or near the agora.

**Ritual**:
1. Pompe (procession) within or from the Lenaion precinct, organized jointly by the Basileus and the Epimeletes (Aristot. Ath. Pol. 57.1; law of Euegoros: Demosth. 21.10)
2. Before or during the agon, the eleusinian Dadouchos holding a torch addressed the assembly: "Invoke the god!" (Καλεῖτε θεόν); the crowd responded: "Σεμέλης Ἴακχε πλουτοδότα" (Schol. Aristoph. Frö. 479)
3. Sacrifice by the ἐπισκόποι Ἐλευσινόθεν (IG² 1672, 182), preceded by an ἀπαρχή to Demeter, Kore, and Pluton
4. Dramatic agon organized by the Basileus alone (Aristot. Ath. Pol. 57.1): tragedy and comedy
5. Womens' orgiastikon in the Lenaion: maenadic ceremony represented on the "Lenaia vases" — women (Ληναί / λῆναι) before a columnar Dionysos idol (mask and draped clothes on a pillar), wine mixed in stamnoi and ladled into cups, ecstatic dancing with thyrsos, torches, tympana, and Kastagnetten; some carry a fawn-skin or fawn-calf (Lenaia vases, Frickenhaus 1912)
6. Skōmmata (mockeries) thrown from wagons, carried over from Choen to Lenaia (Phot./Suid.; Schol. Aristoph. Ritt. 547)
7. Schirmvasen (parasol vases, Buschor) also assigned to Lenaia by Deubner: komos with parasols, dress exchange, male participants; the skyphos is the characteristic drinking vessel of the Lenaia vases, the chous of the Choen — a fundamental distinction.

**Aition**: None discussed by Deubner for the Lenaia itself. The Dadouchos' invocation of Iakchos as son of Semele connects to the Theban Dionysos myth, reflecting the orgiastic tradition's Boeotian origin (Frickenhaus, followed cautiously by Deubner).

**Participants**:
- Basileus (oversaw agon)
- Epimeletes (jointly organized pompe)
- Eleusinian Dadouchos (ritual invocation)
- Eleusinian ἐπίσκοποι (sacrifice)
- Women only in the orgiastic rite (Lenaia vases: no men visible)
- Male participants in the komos/Schirmvasen phase
- Ion. Mykonos parallels: 10 Lenaion — Demeter, Kore, Zeus Buleus; 11 Lenaion — Semele; 12 Lenaion — Dionysos Leneus (Prott, Fasti sacri Nr. 4 = Dittenberger Syll.³ 1024)

**Sources**:
- Aristotle, Ath. Pol. 57.1 (basileus organized pompe and agon with epimeletes)
- Demosth. 21.10 (law of Euegoros: pompe epi Lenaio, tragedians, comedians)
- Hesych. ἐπὶ Ληναίω ἀγών (Lenaion as large precinct with shrine of Dionysos Lenaios)
- Schol. Aristoph. Ach. 202; Steph. Byz. Λήναιον (erroneous "en agrois" location — rejected)
- Schol. Aristoph. Frö. 479 (Dadouchos invocation)
- IG² 1672, 182 (aparche to eleusinian triad, sacrifice at Epilenaia Dionysia)
- IG² 1496 Ab 105 (333/2 BCE, hide-money list); IG² 2130, 57 ff.
- Phot./Suid.: skōmmata at Lenaia transferred from Choen
- Aristoph. Ach. 504; Schol. Aristoph. Hes. Erga 502 (Gamelion month)
- Prott, Fasti sacri S. 17, 44 Nr. 22 A 2; Ath. Mitt. 23, 1898, 222 f.
- Inscr. de Délos 372 A 102; Milet 3, 163 Nr. 31, 3 (comparable festivals in Delos, Miletos)
- Inschr. v. Magnesia 117, 5 (Dionysos mysteries in Lenaion month)
- Frickenhaus, Lenäenvasen (72. Winckelmannsprogramm, Berlin 1912)
- Phanodemos at Athen. 465b (for Anthesteria, used here as a counter-argument)
- Archilochos frg. 119 (lobakchoi with mention of Demeter and Kore feast)

**Notes**:
- Central dispute: Nilsson attributed the Lenaia vases to the Choen (Anthesteria), Deubner to the Lenaia. Deubner's main arguments: (a) characteristic vessel is the skyphos, not the chous; (b) only women visible, consistent with Ληναί; (c) ecstatic dancing and thyrsos are not mere artistic invention. Nilsson's followers included Beazley, Wrede, Buschor, Altheim, Wilamowitz.
- The name Lenaion derives from Ληναί (maenads), not from ληνός (wine-press; Ribbeck, followed by Deubner and Nilsson). The "wine-pressing" interpretation of Lenaia is rejected.
- Relationship to Eleusis: Deubner acknowledges the eleusinian elements (dadouchos, epimeletes) but notes they are relatively recent, linked to the young figure of Iakchos.
- Dionysos Lenaios = the thracian orgiastic god, brought via Thebes; contrasted with the kleinasian spring god of the Anthesteria.
- Ionic Lenaia: the festival is common-Ionic, like the Anthesteria; not younger than the Anthesteria.

### Ländliche Dionysia (Rural Dionysia)

**Month/Day**: Posideon; for the demos Myrrinus, assembly about the festival was on the 19th Posideon (IG² 1183, 36), which Deubner (following Wilhelm) places *after* the festival, so Myrrinus celebrated *before* the 19th. Other demes not necessarily on the same day.

**Deity**: Dionysos (Phales as companion: Aristoph. Ach. 263)

**Ritual**:
1. Pompe (procession), described from Aristoph. Ach. 247 ff.:
   - Daughter of Dikaiopolis as Kanephore at the front
   - Two slaves holding the phallus erect
   - Dikaiopolis at the rear singing the Phallikon
   - Contents of the kanoun (basket) constitute the unbloody offering
2. Askoliasmos: jumping on an inflated greased wineskin (ἀσκός); entertainment, not a proper ritual
3. Komoi: dionysiac revel-processions from different groups (possibly different demes collaborating), competing for prizes in song and costume; κωμάρχοι led each group; these komoi are significant for the origin of comedy
4. Possibly also: amphora of wine, a vine-branch, a goat as sacrificial animal, a basket of figs, and the phallus (Plut. de cup. div. 527d — Deubner applies this to the Ländliche Dionysia, with the Dionysiac elements superimposed on an older phallic layer)
5. Slaves participated (Plut. non posse suav. vivi 1098b)

**Aition**: None discussed by Deubner.

**Participants**:
- All free men including slaves
- Kanephore (prominent family daughter)
- κωμάρχοι (leaders of komoi groups)
- Slaves

**Sources**:
- Aristoph. Ach. 247–279 (pompe description)
- Plut. de cup. div. 527d (traditional pompe content)
- Plut. non posse suav. vivi 1098b (slaves' participation)
- Cornutus, De natura deorum 30 (askoliasmos, rural youth)
- Verg. Georg. 2.380 ff. (askoliasmos in rural context)
- Schol. Aristoph. Plut. 1129; Poll. 9.121; Suid. (askoliasmos)
- IG² 1183, 36 (Myrrinus, 19 Posideon)
- Wilhelm, Urk. dram. Auff. 238 f. (assembly after festival)
- Documented in demes: Acharnai (IG² 1206; Aristoph. Ach. 247); Aixone (Polemon 1, 1929, 162; IG² 1202); Ikaria (IG I² 187; IG² 1178); Kollytos (Aeschin. 1.157; Demosth. 18.180); Myrrinus (IG² 1183); Phlya (Isaeus 8.15, 35); Eleusis (IG² 1186, 1189, etc.); Piräus (IG² 1496; Demosth. 21.10; Aristot. Ath. Pol. 54.8 — state-level festival with Archon-Demarch and state funding)

**Notes**:
- The phallus was not a symbol of Dionysos but the older autonomous phallic fertility god absorbed into the Dionysiac cult. The Ländliche Dionysia were originally a pure phallic celebration.
- Deubner rejects a separate festival "Askolia" as a grammaticians' invention.
- The Piräus festival (Διονύσια τὰ ἐν Πειραιεῖ) had state-level status equal to the Lenaia and Städtische Dionysia; epheboi participated in pompe and sacrifice.
- Komoi at the Ländliche Dionysia are relevant to the pre-literary origins of comedy (Koerte, Wilamowitz).
- Calendar frieze of Hag. Eleutherios: Posideon section shows judges behind a prize-laden table, two cocks attacking each other (representing agon).

**Deubner**: printed p. 133–137 / PDF approx. 143–148

### Städtische Dionysia (Great Dionysia / City Dionysia)

**Month/Day**: Elaphebolion; Proagon on 8th; Asklepieia on 8th (preceding Dionysia, per hautgelderrechnung IG² 1496); eisagoge (ritual entrance of image) evening of 8th; main festival 9th–13th:
- 9th morning: Pompe (great sacrificial procession)
- 9th day: choirs of παῖδες and ἄνδρες
- 9th evening: Komos
- 10th: Comedies
- 11th–13th: Tragedies
- 14th: Assembly (Thuk. 4.118.12; Demosth. 21.10)

**Deity**: Dionysos Eleuthereus

**Ritual**:
1. **Proagon** (8 Elaphebolion): poets and actors presented themselves to the public (Rohde, Kl. Schr. 2, 381 ff.; IG² 780.15)
2. **Asklepieia** (8 Elaphebolion): sacrifice to Asklepios in the neighboring sanctuary (Aeschin. 3.67)
3. **Eisagoge** (evening of 8th): Epheboi escorted the image of Dionysos Eleuthereus by torchlight from the Eschara (a small altar near the Academy chapel) to the theater precinct (IG² 1006, 12; 1008, 14; 1011, 11). A preliminary sacrifice was made at the Academy chapel before departure (Deubner: relic of the original appeasement sacrifice in Eleutherai when the god was taken away). Before the procession there was a general banquet (Menander / Alkiphr. 4.18.16; Philostr. v. soph. 2.1.3)
4. **Pompe** (morning of 9th): great sacrificial procession; the great display of state wealth. Phalloi carried (one per colony, and Athens itself supplied phalloi); kanephores preceded them (IG² 896.8; Schol. Aristoph. Ach. 242). The procession went to the old temple of Dionysos in the theater precinct. Reigentänze to the Twelve Gods and other deities (Xen. hipparch. 3.2).
5. **Dithyrambic choruses** (day of 9th): boys' and men's choruses (Demosth. 21.10; order in Dionysiac fasti: Wilhelm, Urk. dram. Auff. 18)
6. **Komos** (evening of 9th): dionysiac carnivalesque revel-procession, in contrast to the hieratic pompe (mockeries, jokes)
7. **Dramatic competitions**: comedies (10th), tragedies (11th–13th)

**Aition**: Deubner does not discuss an aition per se, but notes that the annual eisagoge *re-enacts* the original transfer of the Eleuthereus image from Eleutherai to Athens (Paus. 1.38.8; 1.29.2), hence the small chapel near the Academy (a temporary stop, representing Eleutherai). The festival was (probably) founded by Peisistratos.

**Participants**:
- Archon Eponymos (oversaw festival — not the Basileus; this marks it as a newer foundation)
- Epheboi (eisagoge, sacrifice, pompe with bull: IG² 1028, 1029)
- Epimeletes of mysteries (also involved)
- Colonies: sent phalloi
- State-invited foreign ambassadors (assigned seats: IG² 456.32)
- Iobakchen: celebrated 10 Elaphebolion with sponde (IG² 1368.117)

**Sources**:
- Aristot. Ath. Pol. 56.3 ff. (Archon Eponymos overseeing)
- Demosth. 21.10 (law of Euegoros: pompe, boy-choirs, [men's choirs], komos, comedians, tragedians)
- Paus. 1.20.3; 1.29.2; 1.38.8 (eisagoge, Academy chapel, Eleutherai origin)
- IG² 1006, 12; 1008, 14 ff.; 1011, 11 (ephebos eisagoge)
- IG² 1496 Aa 78 ff. (hautgelderrechnung: Asklepieia before Dionysia)
- IG I² 45, 12 (Brea decree: colonies send phalloi)
- IG² 1368, 117 ff. (Iobakchen, 10 Elaphebolion sacrifice)
- IG² 2046 (2nd c. CE: basileus also sacrificed at the pompe in imperial era)
- Alkiphr. 4.18.16 (Menander wishing to hymn Dionysos at the Eschara annually)
- Philostr. v. soph. 2.1.3 (Herodes Atticus banquet in Kerameikos)
- Wilhelm, Urk. dram. Auff. 12, 18 (Dionysiac fasti order)
- Thuk. 4.118.12 (assembly on 14th)

**Notes**:
- Three processional acts are distinct (Deubner argues firmly against scholars who conflated them): (1) eisagoge (preparatory, not part of the proper festival), (2) pompe (the proper sacrificial procession), (3) komos (carnivalesque appendage). Robert, Pfuhl, Bethe, and Buschor had variously confused or equated these; Deubner insists the epigraphic evidence (ephebe inscriptions and Euegoros law) clearly separates them.
- The festival is modeled on the Ländliche Dionysia (hence the phallos in the pompe); it is younger than the rural version, younger than the Lenaia.
- The Basileus was not the organizer (unlike Lenaia and Anthesteria); the Archon Eponymos was — a sign of the festival's relatively recent origin.
- IG² 2046 (2nd c. CE) shows the Basileus making a sacrifice at the pompe, suggesting later involvement.
- Calendar frieze of Hag. Eleutherios: Elaphebolion section shows a comic actor pulling a goat.

**Deubner**: printed p. 138–141 / PDF approx. 149–153

### Oschophoria

**Month/Day**: Pyanopsion; precise day uncertain. The aition associates the festival with Theseus' return from Crete on 7th or 8th Pyanopsion (Plut. Thes. 22.4; 36.4). The 7th is occupied by the Pyanopsia; the 9th begins the Thesmophoria cycle (Stenia). Deubner suggests the 8th as most probable, but notes it must not coincide with the well-attested Theseia (also Pyanopsion).

**Deity**: Dionysos (departure point of procession, recipient of the vine-branches); Athena Skiras (destination)

**Ritual**:
1. **Procession** (ὦσχοφορία / ὠσχοφορία) from a Dionysos sanctuary in the city (probably the Limnaion) to the precinct of Athena Skiras in Phaleron:
   - Led by two youths (νεανίαι) dressed in women's clothing, carrying vine-branches heavy with grapes (ὤσχοι; ὦσχος = vine-branch with fruit still attached)
   - The two leaders were selected from noble and wealthy families (Istor, apud Harpokr.; Hesych)
   - A herald attended, with a wreathed staff (not the herald himself wreathed: Plut. Thes. 22.4)
   - Chorus sang ὠσχοφορικά songs throughout
   - Epheboi from each phyle also participated (running competition afterward)
   - Deipnophoroi (food-carrier women) accompanied the procession with provisions
2. **At the Oschophorion** (τόπος, an informal precinct in Phaleron, where Athena Skiras also had her temple):
   - Sacrifice (Plut. Thes. 23.4, via Demon)
   - Libations/sponde with the ritual cries ἐλελεῦ (joy) and ἰοὺ ἰού (lamentation), juxtaposed — Deubner: ecstatic character natural for a wine-god festival; the psychic differentiation (joy/grief) in Plut. Thes. 22.4 is purely aetiological, not original
   - Deipnophoroi participated in the sacrifice and told μῦθοι during the communal meal
   - Special dances (Athen. 631b)
3. **Agon** (foot-race, Proklos Chrest. via Phot. Bibl. 322.13 Bekk.):
   - Each phyle sent epheboi to race
   - The winner (πρότερος sc. ἢ οἱ ἄλλοι εἰς κέρμα ἀφικόμενος) drank from the πενταπλόα cup: a mixture of ὄλαιον, οἶνος, μέλι, τυρός, ἄλφιτα
   - Deubner emends the confused passage in Aristodemos (Athen. 495f) and Schol. Nik. Alex. 109: they wrongly attribute vine-branches to all runners and confuse the Oschophoria with the Skira; Proklos' account is the most reliable
4. **Komos** back to Athens (inferred from Pfuhl, De pompis 49; Aristodemos in Athen. 495f)

**Aition**: Deubner notes that the bräuche were interpreted through the Theseus-Crete myth (probably not before the 6th century BCE: Robert). The vine-branches were said to have been offered to Dionysos and Ariadne. The women's dress of the two leaders was interpreted as Theseus disguising his youths. Deubner treats the aition as secondary and irrelevant to the actual ritual.

**Participants**:
- Two leading νεανίαι: noble and wealthy families, dressed as women
- Chorus (singing oschophorika)
- Epheboi from all ten phylai (race)
- Deipnophoroi (food-carrier women — a distinctive office at this festival)
- Herald with wreathed staff
- Broader Athenian public joining the procession

**Sources**:
- Proklos Chrest. in Phot. Bibl. S. 322.13 Bekk. (procession, the two leaders, race, pentaploa)
- Plut. Thes. 22.4; 23.2–4 (via Demon: deipnophoroi, sacrifice; ritual cries; aetiological connection to Theseus)
- Harpokr. ὠσχοφόροι; Istor, apud Harpokr. (noble families)
- Hesych ὠσχοφόρια; ὠσχοφόριον (place in Phaleron)
- Schol. Nik. Alex. 109 (race with vine-branches — confused account)
- Aristodemos in Athen. 495f (race and komos — confused account with Skira elements)
- Athen. 631b (dances at Oschophorion)
- Bekk. Anecd. 1, 318.23; 1, 285.29 ff. (ὠσχοφόρος)
- Phot. ὠσχοφορεῖν; Suid. ὠσχοφόρος
- Paus. 1.36.4 (Athena Skiras sanctuary in Phaleron)
- Pfuhl, De pompis 48 ff. (Deubner frequently cites but also corrects)
- IG I² 187; IG² 1178 (for deme Ikaria Dionysia, context)

**Notes**:
- The purpose of the festival: thanksgiving/blessing for the completed grape harvest (galathe). The procession went to Phaleron (the sea) because Dionysos was believed to have landed there when he brought the vine to Attica — Pfuhl's explanation, accepted by Deubner.
- The Oschophorion: a τόπος (informal locality), not a proper temenos. Athena Skiras settled there from Salamis at some point, her temple then marking the endpoint of the procession; she is not the mistress of the festival.
- The vine-branches were offerings for Dionysos, not Athena Skiras; "Ὠσχοφόρια" should not be called the chief festival of Athena Skiras (against Bekk. Anecd. and Suid. Ὠσχοφόρια: Σκιράδος Ἀθηνᾶς ἑορτή).
- Women's dress of the leaders: Deubner follows Lobeck's derivation from the preservation of the old Ionic chiton in ceremonial dress (festival costume conservatism), not from any initiation rite.
- Rejected interpretation: Van der Loeff (Mnemos. 43, 1915, 404 ff.) proposed derivation from ὄσχος (scrotum) and a male-initiation ceremony behind the female dress and the deipnophoroi-as-nurses motif. Deubner rejects this entirely: the better manuscript tradition has Ὠσχοφόρια (vine-branch, not scrotum), and the parallel of Daphnephoria and Eiresione confirms it; analogy of Thesmophoria, Skirophoria shows the name denotes carrying of objects, not body parts.
- Dispute on date: Mommsen, Pfuhl, Robert all discussed; Deubner opts for 8th Pyanopsion by exclusion but leaves uncertainty on record.
- The ἀμφιθαλής requirement: applies to the two leading youths (both parents living), not to all runners — Deubner corrects the Nikanderscholion.

#### Gamelion Sacrifice (Dionysos, last third of Gamelion)

**Month/Day**: Gamelion, last third (φθίνοντος Γαμηλιῶνος)

**Deity**: Dionysos

**Ritual**: Sacrifice of an auserlesener Bock (ἐρίφος κριτός) to Dionysos

#### Klematis (month unknown)

**Month/Day**: Unknown; linked to the Elaphebolion pompe in one inscription

**Deity**: Dionysos (implied)

**Ritual**: Procession involving a vine-branch (κλῆμα); epheboi participated in both the Klematis and the Elaphebolion pompe (IG² 1043, 31). Deubner compares it to the procession in Plutarch (de cup. div. 527d) where a vine-branch was carried.

**Sources**: IG² 1043, 31; Plut. de cup. div. 527d (earlier reference); Koehler to IG 2, 482

**Notes**: Deubner follows the analogy of χόες / χύτροι (two-word name from the object carried) to suggest the vine-branch was a specific cult object, not merely a reference to vine-pruning.

**Deubner**: printed p. 147 / PDF approx. 157

#### Iobakcheia

**Month/Day**: Uncertain (Deubner: probably connected to Anthesteria period)

**Deity**: Dionysos

**Participants/Context**: Named in the oath of the Geraeren ([Demosth.] 59.78, the Neaira speech) alongside Theoinia: the Geraeren swore to celebrate both festivals "according to ancestral custom at the proper time" (κατὰ τὰ πάτρια καὶ κατὰ τὸν καιρόν)

**Sources**: [Demosth.] 59.78; IG² 1368 (Iobakchen inscription, 2nd c. CE — the cult association "cannot be separated" from the older festival)

**Notes**:
- Nothing else known about the festival's actual ritual.
- Its connection to the Anthesteria-Limnaion area is confirmed by the fact that the Iobakchen association (IG² 1368) settled on the site of the old Limnaion in the 2nd c. CE, and by a month "Iobakchios" on Astypalaia apparently equated with Anthesterion (Bischoff, De fastis 374, 377).
- Robert identified the Iobakcheia with the festival of the Lenaia vases; Buschor assigned it to the wine-libation at the Choen; Prott made Ambrosia, Klematis, and Iobakcheia three separate days of the Lenaia — Deubner calls all of these "mere speculation."

**Deubner**: printed p. 147–148 / PDF approx. 157–158

#### Theoinia

**Month/Day**: Uncertain (celebrated in the demes, i.e., in the countryside)

**Deity**: Dionysos Theoinos (= "wine-god"; ἐπίκλησις)

**Ritual**: Rural celebrations in individual demes; specific gennētai groups added a supplementary sacrifice (ἐπέθυον). The festival and its cult place (Theoinia) were named from the epithet Theoinos.

**Participants**: General deme population plus specific gennētai groups, including (probably) Krokonidai or Koironidai — eleusinian gennē (Harpokr. Θεοίνια, citing Lykourgos, Δικαδασία Κροκωνιδῶν πρὸς Κοιρωνίδας)

**Sources**:
- Harpokr. Θεοίνια (Lykourgos reference; Aeschylus frg. 382 N. and Dionysios Skymnaios in Schol. Lykophr. 1247 for the epithet Θεοίνης/Θεοινοῦς in connection with maenads)
- Hesych. Θεοίνια
- Phot. Θεοίνιον (sanctuary of Dionysos; brief excerpt from a source mentioning a genos)
- Bekk. Anecd. 1, 264.6; Etym. m. 446.40

**Notes**:
- Deubner distinguishes Theoinia firmly from the Ländliche Dionysia (following Nilsson, De Dion. att. 104 ff.): different cult epithet, different character.
- Orgiasticism: two Tragiker fragments (Aeschylus frg. 382; Dionysios Skymnaios apud Schol. Lykophr. 1247) mention Theoinos alongside maenads, but Deubner judges any conclusion about orgiasticism "highly doubtful" (äußerst zweifelhaft).
- Only one "Theoinia" is unconfirmed; there may have been multiple such cult places.
- Buschor assigned the Theoinia to the Hieros Gamos of the Anthesteria; Prott made it one day of the Lenaia. Deubner calls both "floating in the air" (in der Luft schweben).

**Deubner**: printed p. 147–148 / PDF approx. 157–158

#### Iobakchen cult-calendar (IG² 1368, 2nd century CE)

This is not a civic Athenian festival but the calendar of the private Iobakchen association, treated by Deubner (pp. 148–151) as evidence for survival of Dionysiac traditions.

**Festivals of the association**:

1. **Monthly (ἔνατος of each month)**: because the 9th Elaphebolion was the first day of the Great Dionysia (Deubner)
2. **Annual ἐμφιετηρίς** (anniversary feast of the association)
3. **Βακχεῖα**: not a single festival but the totality of Dionysiac festivals (Ziehen); includes the Stibas as the principal one
4. **Στιβάς** (from στιβάς, "litter of leaves/straw"):
   - The main festival of the association, organized by the priest
   - Named for the custom of strewing leaves/straw (attested nowhere else among Iobakchen festivals)
   - Deubner connects the Stibas with the Katagogia and the Anthesteria (citing the litter-laden wagons in the Massilian Floralia connected to the Anthesteria: Deubner, Jahrb. 42, 1927)
   - A libation (σπονδή) for the Katagogia was poured at the Stibas
   - The priest Nikomachos had introduced a theological discourse (θεολογία) at the Stibas
   - Mimetic drama (sacred play) at the Stibas: ten roles distributed by lot (IG² 1368, 121 ff.):
     - Five human: ἱερεύς (implicit), ἀνθιερεύς, ἀρχίβακχος, ταμίας, βουκολικός
     - Five divine: Διονυσοκόρη, Παλαίμων, Ἀφροδίτη, Πρωτεύρυθμος (otherwise unknown divine figure, probably of dance)
     - Meal portions (μέρη) distributed to role-holders
5. **10 Elaphebolion sacrifice** (ἀρχίβακχος): the archebakkos sacrificed on the 10th Elaphebolion (second day of Great Dionysia) — IG² 1368, 117

**Sources**: IG² 1368 (Iobakchen inscription, 2nd c. CE; with commentaries by Dittenberger, Syll.³ 1109 and Ziehen, Leg. sacr. 132 ff.); Philostr. v. Apollon. 4.21 (Anthesteria still celebrated 2nd c. CE)

**Notes**:
- The σπονδή for Katagogia at the Stibas supports Deubner's earlier identification of Katagogia with the Anthesteria (p. 104), since the Iobakchen settled on the Limnaion site.
- The five divine roles paired with five human roles may not be coincidental; the five divine are: Kore (= Dionysokore), Palaimon (in Dionysiac connection elsewhere), Aphrodite, Proteurythmos (dance daemon), and implicitly Dionysos himself (played by the priest?).
- The 10th Elaphebolion sacrifice by the archebakkos (2nd day of Great Dionysia): seemingly in conflict with the Stibas-context for the drama, suggesting later editorial insertion into the inscription.
- "Stibas" name could only be given if leaf-strewing was unique to this festival among the association's celebrations.
