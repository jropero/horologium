# Deubner — Demeter & Kore, Part 1 (Skira, Thesmophoria)
Source: L. Deubner, *Attische Feste* (1932), printed p. 40–59 / PDF p. 39–58
Extracted: 2026-10-25

---

## Overview

Deubner treats the Skira and Thesmophoria as two closely connected festivals of Demeter and Kore. The central ritual link: at the Skira, piglets and other objects are cast into underground pits (Megara) belonging to Demeter and Kore; at the Thesmophoria, the decomposed remains are retrieved and mixed with seed-grain. The chapter is largely a polemic against Gjerstad (ARW 27, 1929) who had assigned the Skira to Athena alone and attempted to identify Thesmophoria/Skirophoria/Arretophoria as one festival — Deubner refutes both positions in detail.

---

### Skira (= Skirophoria)

**Month/Day**: 12 Skirophorion
**Deity**: Demeter and Kore (primary); Athena Polias and Poseidon-Erechtheus (secondary, via procession)
**Ritual**:
- Women threw piglets alive into underground chambers (Megara) sacred to Demeter and Kore
- They also cast in mysterious ritual breads (ἄρρητα) shaped like snakes and phalli, and pine branches
- At the same festival, the priestess of Athena Polias and the priest of Poseidon-Erechtheus processed from the Acropolis under a large white canopy (σκίρον, megas skiasdeion), carried by members of the Eteobutadai clan, to a place called Skiron on the sacred road to Eleusis, near the Kephisos
- At Skiron, sacrifices were performed — Deubner thinks not to Athena alone but involving the Demeter/Kore sanctuary nearby (Paus. 1, 37, 2: sanctuary of Demeter, Kore, Athena, and Poseidon together)
- Sexual abstinence was prescribed for participants (Philochoros); garlic was eaten as a precaution
- Participants in the Skiron procession used the so-called Dios kodion (a magical fleece, not originally Zeusian); Deubner suggests it served a post-harvest fertility rite on the Skiron field
- In Marathon: day before the Skira (pro Skira), sacrifices to Kurotrophos, heroes Hyttenios and Galios, Tritopatreis, and Akamantes — all linked to earth and fertility religion (IG 2² 1358b)
- Myrtle garlands were used at the Skira (Polyaen 3, 10, 4); myrtle is attested as Demeter's plant via the Eleusinian Mysteries

**Aition**: Deubner explicitly discusses one, drawn from the Lukianscholion (Schol. Luk. dial. meretr., p. 275–276 Rabe): when Kore was abducted by Pluto, a swineherd named Eubouleus was present with his pigs; both Eubouleus and his herd were swallowed into the earth (συγκατεπόθησαν). In honour of Eubouleus, piglets are thrown into the Megara (πίττεσθαι τοὺς εἰς τὰ χοιάσματα τῆς Δήμητρος καὶ τῆς Κόρης). Deubner assigns this aition specifically to the Skira, not the Thesmophoria (contra Gjerstad and Clement of Alexandria).

**Participants**: Women (primary ritual actors for the Megara rite); the Eteobutadai (carrying the canopy); priestess of Athena Polias; priest of Poseidon-Erechtheus; priest of Helios (Deubner thinks Helios joined late, perhaps under Stoic influence)

**Sources**:
- Main: Schol. Lukian dial. meretr. p. 275–276 Rabe (key scholion)
- Schol. Aristoph. Ekkl. 18 (names and date)
- Aristoph. Thesm. 834; Ekkl. 18, 59; Menand. Epitr. p. 36, 14 Jensen
- Harpokr. s.v. σκίρον (Lysimachides on the procession and canopy)
- Steph. Byz. s.v. Σκίρος; Schol. Aristoph. Thesm. 834
- IG 2² 1177 (Piraeus Thesmophorion, 4th c. BCE — lists Skira among Demeter festivals)
- IG 2² 1358b (Marathon calendar, pro Skira offerings)
- Paus. 1, 36, 4 (location: Skiron on sacred road near Kephisos); 1, 37, 2 (sanctuary)
- Plut. coniug. praec. 144a (three sacred ploughings: at Skiron, at Rharia, below the Acropolis)
- Phot. s.v. τρωπιλίη (garlic and sexual abstinence: Philochoros cited)
- Suid. s.v. Διὸς κῴδιον (magical fleece, via Polemon)
- Polyaen 3, 10, 4 (myrtle garlands)

**Notes**:
- Major scholarly controversy: Gjerstad argued the Skira was an Athena Polias festival, not a Demeter festival, and treated all three names (Thesmophoria, Skirophoria, Arretophoria) as one festival. Deubner strongly refutes both positions, arguing from internal coherence of the Lukianscholion. Robert (Herrn. 20, 1885) had already correctly seen the scholion distinguishes three separate festivals.
- Deubner rejects Buschor's "parasol-festival" interpretation: the Vasenbilder showing cross-dressed figures with parasols are not depictions of the Skira; elsewhere σκίρον never means a handheld parasol (always σκιάδειον), and the Lysimachides testimony refers specifically to a large baldachin (μέγα σκιάδειον).
- The meaning of σκίρα remains uncertain: Robert suggested a connection to the appearance of the objects; Deubner notes it may parallel how Xόες and Χύτροι are named after objects used.
- Whether the Helios priest's participation in the procession is original or a late (Stoic-influenced) addition is uncertain; Deubner leans toward late addition.
- Connection between the Skiron procession and the three sacred ploughings (at Skiron, at Eleusis/Rharia, and below the Akropolis) shows a compromise between the old agrarian Athena cult and the newly incorporated Eleusinian goddesses (Robert's interpretation, accepted by Deubner).

### Stenia

**Month/Day**: 9 Pyanopsion (two days before the Thesmophoria proper)
**Deity**: Demeter and Kore
**Ritual**:
- Night festival (vigil)
- Women mocked and abused each other (aischrologia, ritual insult), described as σκώπτουσι καὶ λοιδοροῦσιν (Hesych)
- The Prytanes sacrificed to Demeter and Kore on behalf of the Boule and Demos (IG 2² 674)
- A parallel Stenia celebration apparently took place in Eleusis involving the hierophant and priestesses (IG 2² 1363)

**Participants**: Women of Athens (citizen status uncertain for the Stenia, unlike the Thesmophoria where citizen wives were required)

**Sources**:
- Hesych s.v. Στήνια; s.v. στηνιάσαι
- Phot. s.v. Στήνια (also citing Eubulos: the Anodos of Demeter was thought to occur at the Stenia)
- IG 2² 674, 6 (Prytanes' sacrifice)
- Schol. Aristoph. Thesm. 834 (Stenia distinct from Thesmophoria: "both are women's festivals, but the Stenia two days before the Thesmophoria in Pyanopsion")
- IG 2² 1363 (Eleusinian counterpart)
- Aeneas Tact. 4, 8 (night celebration of Thesmophoria in Eleusis — Deubner thinks this refers to the Stenia)

**Notes**: The exact content is sparse. Whether the Stenia required women of impeccable civic birth is unknown (unlike the Thesmophoria, where it is certain).

**Deubner**: printed p. 52 / PDF p. 51

### Thesmophoria — Halimus preliminary festival

**Month/Day**: 10 Pyanopsion (later addition to the cycle; originally the Anodos was day 1)
**Deity**: Demeter (of Halimus)
**Ritual**:
- The most distinguished women from Athens (πρῶται γυναῖκες) went to the deme Halimus (at Cape Kolias, east end of Phaleron Bay) for local Thesmophoria
- They sacrificed and danced
- The festival had a secret character (μυστήρια: Clem. Protr. 2, 34)
- Its inclusion in the city's festival cycle was probably for political reasons (strengthening ties with the deme)

**Participants**: The most distinguished women of Athens (Plut. Sol. 8, 4–5)

**Sources**:
- Schol. Aristoph. Thesm. 80 (δεκάτῃ ἐν Ἁλιμοῦντι Θεσμοφόρια ἄγεται)
- Phot. s.v. Θεσμοφορίων ἡμέραι (confirms day 10 in Halimus)
- Plut. Sol. 8, 4–5
- Clem. Protr. 2, 34, 2 (τὰ ἐν Ἁλιμοῦντι τῆς Ἀττικῆς μυστήρια)

**Notes**: Deubner argues the three original days (Anodos, Nesteia, Kalligeneia) received their names before the Halimus day was added; the Nesteia therefore counted as the third day from Halimus onwards, which is how Aristoph. Thesm. 80 (ἡμέρα τρίτη τῶν Θεσμοφορίων, ἡ Μέση) makes sense — "Mese" was the old name of the Nesteia when it was the middle day.

**Deubner**: printed p. 51–52 / PDF p. 50–51

### Thesmophoria — Anodos (Day 1 of the main festival)

**Month/Day**: 11 Pyanopsion
**Deity**: Demeter and Kore (Θεσμοφόρoι)
**Ritual**:
- Solemn procession (πομπή) of women up to the elevated Thesmophorion (on the Pnyx)
- Sacrifice in the sanctuary upon arrival
- Women erected booths (σκηναί/Laubhütten) and remained in the Thesmophorion for all three days
- The "Anodos" (Ascent) gives this day its name: ἄνοδον τὴν εἰς τὸ Θεσμοφόριον ἄφιξιν λέγουσιν

**Participants**: Citizen wives (εὐγενεῖς γυναῖκες per Aristoph. Thesm. 330) of good birth; men excluded entirely

**Sources**:
- Hesych s.v. Ἄνοδος (= 11th of Pyanopsion, when women ascend to the Thesmophorion)
- Schol. Aristoph. Thesm. 585 (Ἄνοδος: τὴν εἰς τὸ Θεσμοφόριον ἄφιξιν; ἐπὶ ὑψηλοῦ γὰρ κεῖται τὸ Θεσμοφόριον)
- Phot. s.v. Θεσμοφορίων ἡμέραι (ἑνδεκάτῃ Κάθοδος — variant name)
- Alkiphr. 2, 37, 1 Schepers (on the Anodos as first day)
- Isaeus 6, 50 (pompē form; slave woman intruding on the procession)
- IG 2² 1177, 23–24 (Piraeus: ἀναβάσει τοῦ Θεσμοφορίου — the elevated location)
- Aristoph. Thesm. 281, 584, 623, 657, 893 (language of ἀνέρχεσθαι throughout)

**Notes**: An alternative name for the first day was "Kathodos" (seen in some sources — Deubner notes the variant), perhaps referring to the return from Halimus. Deubner argues it cannot mean "descent" in the main festival since the sanctuary was elevated; "Kathodos" probably reflects the name after the Halimus addition.

**Deubner**: printed p. 53–54 / PDF p. 52–53

### Thesmophoria — Nesteia (Day 2)

**Month/Day**: 12 Pyanopsion
**Deity**: Demeter and Kore
**Ritual**:
- The day of fasting (νηστεία): the women fasted throughout, the darkest and most solemn day of the festival (Plut. Demosth. 30, 5)
- Women sat on the ground (χαμαί) on beds of lygos (chaste-tree, Vitex agnus-castus)
- Sexual abstinence required (same purpose as fasting: openness to the fertility forces expected to flow on day 3)
- No flower garlands permitted
- Women held a mock citizens' assembly (parodied by Aristoph. Thesm. as the Thesmophoriazusen): the female herald invoked Thesmophoroi (Demeter and Kore), Ploutos, Kalligeneia, Kurotrophos, Hermes, Charites
- Cultically a "rest day" between the sacrifices of day 1 and day 3

**Participants**: Citizen wives; the antlētriai (Schöpfweiber) had to have observed sexual abstinence for three days before performing their office

**Sources**:
- Plut. Demosth. 30, 5 (ἐν ᾗ τὴν σκυθρωποτάτην τῶν Θεσμοφορίων ἡμέραν ἄγουσαι παρὰ τῇ θεῷ νηστεύουσιν αἱ γυναῖκες)
- Aristoph. Thesm. 372, 984 (νηστεύομεν δὲ πάντως)
- Aristoph. Vög. 1519 (ἀλλ' ὥσπερ Θεσμοφορίοις νηστεύομεν)
- Athen. 307f
- Cornut. 28 p. 55, 7 Lang (fasting in honor of Demeter, as they celebrate at sowing time)
- Plut. de Is. 378e (women at Thesmophoria fast and sit on the ground)
- Schol. Soph. OC 681 (no flower garlands for women celebrating the Thesmophoria)
- Lukianscholion p. 276, 5 (antlētriai must have been chaste for three days beforehand)
- Fehrle, Kultische Keuschheit 139ff. (lygos attested as bedding)
- Steph. Byz. s.v. Μίλητος (pine branches used at Milesian Thesmophoria, with fertility meaning)

**Notes**:
- The lygos (chaste-tree) was used as bedding; later tradition rationalized its use as aphrodisiac-suppressing, but Deubner follows Nilsson in seeing this as a secondary reinterpretation: the original function was to promote female fertility (hence pine branches at Milesian Thesmophoria had same fertility meaning, not chastity meaning).
- Sitting on the ground had no deep symbolic meaning (Deubner against symbolic over-reading); it was simply the natural result of sleeping on lygos-strew in booths.
- The Nesteia in Aristophanes' play was a day of rest between ritual sacrifices, which is why it was suitable for a women's assembly.

**Deubner**: printed p. 54–56 / PDF p. 53–55

### Thesmophoria — Kalligeneia (Day 3)

**Month/Day**: 13 Pyanopsion
**Deity**: Kalligeneia (a deity derived from the festival's purpose); Demeter and Kore
**Ritual**:
- Name means "beautiful/good offspring": καλός taken in the broadest sense
- Sacrifices (blood and cake offering); the antlētriai retrieved the decomposed remains (σαπέντα) from the Megara
- The retrieved remains (decayed piglets, breads) were placed on the altars; worshippers took portions and mixed them with seed-grain for a fertile harvest
- Women made ritual noise (crashing sounds, rattle) as they descended into the underground chambers to scare away snakes believed to guard the Megara
- At descent, they also deposited fresh objects (new flatbreads shaped as snakes and phalli, new pine branches) in the Megara as substitutes
- Ritual mockery/aischrologia by women (Apollodoros: γυναῖκας σκώπτειν)
- Use of ritual cunni/plastic objects (πλάσματα, γυναικεῖα μόρια) — Theodoret. gr. aff. cur. 3, 84
- Eating pomegranate seeds that had not fallen on the ground (Clem. Protr. 2, 19, 3: women at the Thesmophoria carefully avoided eating seeds that had fallen to the ground)
- Two ἄρχουσαι from each deme were elected to manage the offerings and hospitality; they provided supplies to the priestess: unmilled and milled barley and wheat, dried figs, wine, oil, honey, sesame, poppy seeds, cheese, garlic, a torch, and 400 drachmae silver (Cholargos decree, 334/3 BCE, IG 2² 1184)
- Schol. Aristoph. Ran. 338: κρεοφαγεῖν and χοιροσφαγεῖν at the Thesmophoria (uncertain whether the ἱερομνήμονες of the inscription handled blood sacrifice)

**Aition**: No separate aition for the Kalligeneia is discussed by Deubner; see Skira for the Eubouleus aition which connects the two festivals structurally.

**Participants**: Citizen wives of each deme; two elected ἄρχουσαι per deme; the ἀντλήτριαι (Schöpfweiber) who retrieved the remains (must be pure for 3 days beforehand)

**Sources**:
- Aristoph. Thesm. 298 (Kalligeneia invoked in the herald's address — also attested as a deity name: Usener, Götternamen 122ff.)
- Schol. Aristoph. Thesm. 80 (three days: Anodos, Nesteia, Kalligeneia); Phot. s.v. Θεσμοφορίων ἡμέραι
- Alkiphr. 2, 37, 1 Schepers (Kalligeneia on the day after Nesteia)
- Isaeus 8, 19 and 3, 80 (ἄρχειν εἰς τὰ Θεσμοφόρια as technical term; the elected archousai)
- IG 2² 1184 (Cholargos deme decree, 334/3 BCE: list of supplies for the Thesmophoria)
- Schol. Aristoph. Ran. 338 (meat-eating and pig-slaughter)
- Apollod. 1, 30: ἐν τοῖς Θεσμοφορίοις τὰς γυναῖκας σκώπτειν λέγουσιν
- Kleomedes (mocking language of Epikouros compared to brothels and women celebrating the Thesmophoria)
- Theodoret. gr. aff. cur. 3, 84 (ritual cunni honored at the Thesmophoria)
- Clem. Protr. 2, 19, 3 (pomegranate seeds: αἱ θεσμοφοριάζουσαι τῆς ῥοιᾶς τοὺς κόκκους παραφυλάττουσιν μηδένα τῶν ἀποπεπτωκότων κόκκων ἐσθίειν)
- Lukianscholion p. 276, 3–7 (the antlētriai and the snakes in the megara)

**Notes**:
- Deubner links the antlētriai (Schöpfweiber) to the Thesmophoria, not the Skira: it is at the Thesmophoria that the decomposed remains are retrieved; they were thrown in at the Skira months earlier.
- The Kalligeneia as a deity emerged from the name of the festival function (Usener: naming-from-function). She is invoked specifically on the third day.
- Deubner is uncertain whether blood sacrifice (pig-slaughter, κρεοφαγεῖν) was a standard feature or only attested in Scholia.
- The pomegranate kernel taboo (only those not fallen on the ground may be eaten) is often misquoted in modern literature by omitting the last three words of the Clemens passage, which reverses the sense.

## Cross-cutting Notes

### Relationship between Skira and Thesmophoria

Deubner establishes the two festivals as formally linked:
1. At the Skira (12 Skirophorion): piglets, bread-shapes, and pine branches are cast into the Megara
2. At the Thesmophoria (Pyanopsion): the decomposed remains are retrieved and mixed with seed-grain

The interval is approximately four months (Skirophorion to Pyanopsion). The Thesmophoria falls at the Attic sowing season (Plutarch: Pyanopsion = μὴν σπόριμος). The decayed material, having absorbed earth-powers, was used as a fertility charm for the new sowing. This interpretation connects the three sacred ploughings of Attica (Skiron, Rharion at Eleusis, below the Akropolis) to the Skirophoria/Thesmophoria and the Arretophoria respectively.

### Name of the Thesmophoria

Deubner explains the name: θεσμός means "what is deposited" (cf. Anakreon Frg. 24; Hesych). The θεσμοί carried by the θεσμοφόροι are the decomposed flesh and breads that are "deposited" (deponiert) in the Megara. Similarly σκίρα may have been the name for the ritual objects (appeta) carried/deposited, though their exact appearance is uncertain.

### Scholarly Disputes Deubner Engages

- **Gjerstad (ARW 27, 1929)**: Argued Skira = Athena Polias festival; Thesmophoria/Skirophoria/Arretophoria are one festival. Deubner systematically refutes this from the Lukianscholion's own structure and from epigraphy (IG 2² 1177).
- **Buschor (Jahrb. 38/39, 1923/4)**: Argued the Skira was a "Schirmfest" (parasol festival); Wilamowitz accepted this. Deubner rejects it: the vase paintings showing cross-dressed figures with parasols cannot be assigned to the Skira; σκίρον never means an ordinary handheld parasol elsewhere.
- **Robert (Herrn. 20, 1885)**: Deubner follows Robert on the structure of the Lukianscholion and on the Skiron procession as a compromise between the old agrarian Athena cult and the new Eleusinian goddesses.
- **Nilsson**: Deubner follows Nilsson on the lygos reinterpretation (Gr. Feste 318).
- On the meaning of fasting: Deubner distinguishes fasting as preparation for receiving fertility forces (not merely abstention), citing Deubner, *De incubatione* 14ff.
