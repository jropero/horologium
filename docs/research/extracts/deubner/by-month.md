## Hekatombaion

- **Day 7**: Apollon Hekatombaios sacrifice — Apollon Hekatombaios — inferred festival named after the month's sacrifice to Apollon; Prott's Mykonos calendar confirms the 7th as Apollon's day in the equivalent month. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 12**: Kronia — Kronos + Rhea — masters of households dine with their slaves after the completion of the harvest, with general loud merriment; the month was formerly called Kronion. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Day 16**: Synoikia — Athena + Eirene (from 374 BCE) — public festival (δημοτελής) commemorating the Synoikismos of Theseus; the deme Skambonidai sacrificed a full-grown animal on the Akropolis; a sacrifice to Eirene with an unbloody altar was added after Timotheos' naval peace of 374 BCE. — *Source: athena.md*
- **Night 27/28**: Panathenaia pannychis — Athena — all-night celebration on the Akropolis with young men and maidens dancing and singing, a paian, ololyge by the women, and a torch-race whose winning flame lit the sacrificial pyre. — *Source: athena.md*
- **Day 28**: Panathenaia — Athena Polias / Hygieia / Nike — great procession with kanephoroi, thallophoroi (handsome old men with olive branches), and metoikoi bearing trays and water-jars; double sacrifice (old temple offering + hekatomb); a Peplos hung from a ship-cart and gymnastic, hippic, and musical agons at the Great (penteteris) Panathenaia; instituted by Erichthonios, reorganized 566/5 BCE. — *Source: athena.md*
- **Early to mid month (approximate)**: Herakleia Marathonic — Herakles — penteteris (quadrennial) with agones and an Athenian theoria sent to Marathon; the deme-assembly discussing it met four days before the end of Skirophorion, placing the festival in early Hekatombaion. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Month (approximate, soon after new year)**: Aphrodisia — Aphrodite Pandemos — pompe; the Astynomoi were charged with purifying the sanctuary with dove's blood, whitewashing altars, and washing the statues; hetairai and the Tetradist association also participated. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*

## Metageitnion

- **Month (day unknown)**: Metageitnia — Apollon Metageitnios — sacrifice to Apollon Metageitnios; the month is named after the festival; Deubner connects it to neighborhood associations (metageitnion = change of neighbours). — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Month, in Metageitnion (calendar frieze)**: Herakleia Kynosargic (Diomeian) — Herakles — celebrated at the Kynosarges sanctuary in deme Diomeia; a decree of Alkibiades required monthly sacrifices by the priest together with parasitoi of illegitimate birth (νόθοι), matching Herakles' status; shown in Metageitnion on the Hagios Eleutherios calendar frieze. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Month (Metageitnion most probable; start of Boedromion also possible)**: Eleusinia — Demeter + Kore — agonistic festival (athletic and musical) distinct from the Mysteries; a pompe with kanephore; sacrifices to Hermes, Poseidon, Artemis, Triptolemos, and the Two Goddesses; prizes of sacred barley from the Rarian Plain; held as both a penteteris and a trieteris. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*

## Boedromion

- **Day 5**: Genesia — the Dead + Ge — state public festival (δημοτελής) for the commemoration of the dead, with sacrifice also to Ge (Earth); private family commemorations coincided on the same day; Deubner derives the name from γενήτης = "festival of the ancestors." — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Day 6**: Artemis Agroptera sacrifice — Artemis Agroptera + Enyalios — the Polemarchos sacrificed 500 goats annually, fulfilling a vow made before Marathon; epheboi participated in full armour; the 6th was chosen because Artemis already received a sacrifice on this day. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 7 (probable)**: Boedromia — Apollon Boedromios — sacrifice to Apollon Boedromios ("Helper in Battle-Cry") with a pompe; the month is named after the festival. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 13**: Eleusinian Mysteries: escort of ἱερά — Demeter + Kore — epheboi went to Eleusis and escorted the sacred objects (cists) back to the urban Eleusinion below the Akropolis. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 14**: Eleusinian Mysteries: ἱερά arrive — Demeter + Kore — the sacred objects arrived and were deposited in the urban Eleusinion. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 15**: Eleusinian Mysteries — ἀγυρμός + πρόρρησις — Demeter + Kore — assembly of the mystai; Hierophant and Daduch formally excluded barbarians and murderers from the Stoa Poikile. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 16**: Eleusinian Mysteries — ἅλαδε μύσται — Demeter + Kore — initiates and their sacrificial pigs bathed in the sea at the Kantharos harbour; the pigs were subsequently sacrificed. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 17**: Eleusinian Mysteries — sacrifice — Demeter + Kore — sacrifice for Demeter and Kore at the urban Eleusinion (attested in a private Attic calendar). — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 18**: Epidauria — Asklepios — Asklepios festival inserted when his cult was admitted to the Mysteries; pompe organized by the Basileus; mystai stayed home. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 19**: Eleusinian Mysteries — great procession — Demeter + Kore + Iakchos — the Iakchos image joined the sacred objects retrieved from the Eleusinion; the procession moved along the Sacred Road to Eleusis with sacrifices, libations, hymns, and dances at stations along the way. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Night 19/20**: Eleusinian Mysteries — night festival at Eleusis — Demeter + Kore + Iakchos — torch-lit dances and songs at the Kallichoron well; Iakchos received solemnly by the Epimeletes. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Days following 20**: Eleusinian Mysteries — Myesis + Telety — Demeter + Kore — initiation ceremony (kykeon-drinking, handling of ciste-objects, dromena: Koremythos drama, Hieros Gamos, birth proclamation of Brimon, "Ue Küe" formula, reaped ear of grain shown to Epoptai); Epopteia (second grade) for those initiated the previous year; closing plēmochoai ceremony. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Month (day unknown)**: Heros Egretes festival — Heros Egretes — the Orgeones held a communal banquet in a booth with kitchen and dining couches; month is Boedromion per Deubner. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*

## Pyanepsion

- **Day 5**: Proërosia — Demeter + Kore — "the sacrifices before the plowing"; hierophant and herald announced the festival; public ox-sacrifices with epheboi; connected to the sacred plowing on the Rarian Plain; alternative name Proarkturia attested. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 7**: Pyanopsia — Apollon (+ Artemis, Helios, Horai secondarily) — a bean-dish of legumes and wheat offered to Apollon (possibly in lectisternium form); Eiresione procession: amphithalēs boys carried an olive or laurel branch hung with fruits and honeycombs to the temple of Apollon and to individual house doors, renewed annually. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 8 (Deubner's preferred date; 7th also possible)**: Oschophoria — Dionysos + Athena Skiras — procession from a Dionysos sanctuary to Athena Skiras at Phaleron, led by two noble youths in women's clothing carrying grape-clusters; a chorus sang; epheboi from each phyle raced, the winner drinking from a pentaploa cup; a komos returned to Athens. The 7th is occupied by the Pyanopsia; the 9th begins the Stenia; so the 8th is most probable but unconfirmed. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 8**: Theseia — Theseus (hero) — state sacrifice with pompe; libations to Theseus; public feast; torch-race and multiple agons; epheboi in armed parade; day before: ram sacrifice to Konnidas (Theseus's tutor) and sacrifice to the Amazons; enlarged after Kimon brought Theseus's bones from Skyros (475 BCE). — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Day 9**: Stenia — Demeter + Kore — night festival two days before the Thesmophoria proper; women mocked and ritually abused each other (aischrologia); the Prytanes sacrificed to Demeter and Kore on behalf of the Boule and Demos. — *Source: demeter-pt1.md*
- **Day 10**: Thesmophoria (Halimus preliminary) — Demeter of Halimus — the most distinguished women from Athens went to the deme Halimus (Cape Kolias) for local Thesmophoria with sacrifice, dancing, and secret rites (μυστήρια); probably politically incorporated to strengthen ties with the deme. — *Source: demeter-pt1.md*
- **Days 11–13**: *(Skip Thesmophoria — already reviewed/corrected)* — *Source: demeter-pt1.md*
- **Pyanopsion (day unknown)**: Arretophoria (autumn rite) — Athena Polias — Deubner's reconstruction: two Arrephoren descended by an underground passage near Aphrodite en Kepois, left the covered sacred objects they had carried, brought back others, and were then dismissed and replaced; the summer rite (Skirophorion) deposited the objects, this autumn rite retrieved them. Pausanias describes this phase; no day explicitly named. — *Source: athena.md*
- **Three consecutive days in Pyanopsion (day not fixed)**: Apaturia — Zeus Phratrios + Athena Phratria — Day 1 Dorpia: phratores dined communally; Day 2 Anarrhysis: sacrifice to Zeus Phratrios and Athena; Day 3 Kureotis: registration of newborns (meion sacrifice), adolescents entering ephebeia (koureion sacrifice), and new brides (gamelia sacrifice) into phratries; solemn Hephaistos torchlight at the end; possibly a fourth day (Epibda). — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Last day (eny kai nea = 29th or 30th)**: Chalkeia — Athena Ergane (+ Hephaistos secondary) — sacrifice to Athena Ergane on the Akropolis by the prytaneis; originally a festival of the whole people, later of craftsmen; the official start of weaving the Panathenaic Peplos, with two Arrephoren setting up the warp. — *Source: athena.md*

## Maimakterion

- **Month (day unknown)**: Maimakteria — Zeus Maimaktes ("the raging/stormy one") — the month is named after this festival; no ritual content is preserved; Deubner supposes it concerned averting damage from the onset of winter storms. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Last third of month (φθίνοντος)**: Pompaia — Zeus Meilichios (secondary) — a procession in which the diòn kōdion (fleece from a Zeus Meilichios sacrifice), a kērykeion (herald's staff), and possibly a cauldron of Hermes were carried; Deubner interprets it as an apotropaic circumambulation of the newly sown fields; purificatory material was cast at crossroads. Private calendar entry connects it to Maimakterion 20. — *Source: zeus.md*

## Poseideon

- **Day 8 (probable)**: Posidea — Poseidon — state festival named for the month; largely lost; probably on the 8th (Poseidon's special day for Athenians); private calendar attests a sacrifice to Poseidon Khamaizelios with wineless libations; a penteteris at Sunion with Athenian theoria and regatta. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Posideon, before 19th (Myrrinus assembly date)**: Rural Dionysia (Ländliche Dionysia) — Dionysos — a pompe with kanephore daughter, slaves carrying the phallus, and the Phallikon song; askoliasmos (jumping on inflated greased wineskin); komoi led by kōmarchoi competing for prizes in song and costume; slaves participated; Piraeus festival had state-level status. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 26**: Haloa — Demeter + Kore — exclusively a women's festival at Eleusis; terracotta phalloi erected in the fields; women handled obscene objects and engaged in aischrologia; a great banquet of everything the earth and sea provide (except certain forbidden foods: pomegranate, apple, domestic fowl, eggs, mullet, etc.); priestesses whispered incitement to illicit love; hetairai participated prominently; a pompé for Poseidon also connected. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*

## Gamelion

- **Day 12**: Lenaia — Dionysos Lenaios — a pompe organized by the Basileus and Epimeletes; the Eleusinian Dadouchos invoked the god ("Kalete theon!"); Eleusinian ἐπίσκοποι sacrificed; a dramatic agon (tragedy and comedy) organized by the Basileus; women performed an orgiastic rite (Ληναί) before a columnar Dionysos mask with ecstatic dancing, thyrsos, torches, and tympana; skōmmata (ritual mockeries) were thrown from wagons. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 19**: Kittoseies Dionysou — Dionysos — ivy-crowning of Dionysos images (attested in a private sacrifice calendar of imperial date, IG² 1367.21). — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Last third of month (φθίνοντος)**: Goat sacrifice to Dionysos — Dionysos — sacrifice of a select goat (ἐρίφος κριτός) to Dionysos; attested in an old sacrifice calendar (IG I² 840.16); the calendar frieze of Hagios Eleutherios shows Dionysos riding a goat in the Gamelion panel. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **ca. Day 24**: Theogamia / Gamelia — Zeus + Hera — the sacred marriage (ἱερὸς γάμος) of Zeus and Hera; a sacrifice to Zeus Ἥραιος on the Akropolis (a piglet, IG I² 840.20); the month Gamelion is named from the associated feast (Gamelia); Athenians favoured the days approaching the new moon as auspicious for human marriages. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Month (uncertain attribution)**: Ambrosia — Dionysos τῶν ληνῶν — a festival called Ambrosia celebrated for Dionysos of the Lenai in Gamelion; attested by Plutarch in a scholion on Hesiod but its Athenian attribution is uncertain per Deubner. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*

## Anthesterion

- **Day 11**: Pithoigia — Dionysos ἐν Λίμναις — people brought pithoi and drinking vessels to the Limnaion; before drinking they poured a libation and prayed for the use of the wine to be "harmless and wholesome"; they drank, danced, and sang invoking Dionysos; slaves were permitted to participate. — *Source: dionysos-pt1.md*
- **Day 12**: Choes — Dionysos ἐν Λίμναις + Basilinna — **(a)** city-wide drinking contest with each participant given his own chous; the winner received a wineskin from the Basileus; garlands brought to the priestess at the Limnaion afterward; **(b)** Hieros Gamos: a ship-wagon procession brought Dionysos to the Limnaion, where the Basilinna and 14 Geraeren performed secret rites (ἀπόρρητα ἱερά) at 14 altars; the Basilinna was "married" to Dionysos, with the consummation at the Bukoleion; **(c)** dead souls roamed the city: people chewed whitethorn, smeared doors with pitch, temples were cordoned off; a ἡμέρα μιαρά; **(d)** children in their third year were crowned with flower-wreaths and received gift-choes. — *Source: dionysos-pt1.md*
- **Day 13**: Chytroi — Hermes Chthonios — pots containing a mixture of all seeds (παμπέρματα) were set out for chthonic Hermes; prayer for the dead; no one could eat from the offering; Hydrophoria (water-libations into the chasm near the Olympeion) for victims of the flood; the festival ended with the expulsion-cry "θύραζε, κῆρες, οὐκέτ' Ἀνθεστήρια"; comedy contests also attested. — *Source: dionysos-pt1.md*
- **Day 23**: Diasia — Zeus Meilichios — celebrated outside the city (probably near the Ilissos); holocausts of sacrificial animals; the common people made unbloody offerings (cakes in animal shapes) entirely burnt; private banquets also held; children received gifts (toy carts); gloomy (σκυθρωπός) character combined with private jollity. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Month (Anthesterion)**: Kleine Mysterien (Agrai) — Demeter + Kore — obligatory preliminary initiation for the Great Mysteries; purification bath of the mystai in the Ilissos river at Agrai; sacrifice by the Epimeletes; pantomimic performances with Dionysiac content; celebrated twice in years that also had the Eleusinia. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Month Hieros (= Anthesterion)**: Delia — Apollon on Delos — annual Athenian theoria (sacred embassy) to Delos for the Apollonia; choruses organized by the Archon Eponymos; a penteteris was established 426/5 BCE. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Month (disputed: Anthesterion per Pringsheim from Marathonian calendar, or Thargelion 6 per Philochoros; or listed after Haloa in the Eleusinian inscription)**: Chloia — Demeter Chloe + Kore — a joyful festival celebrated at Eleusis in spring when the young grain is green; sacrifice by the Eleusinian demarch; Deubner cannot fix the month precisely. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*

## Elaphebolion

- **Day 8**: Proagon — Dionysos Eleuthereus — poets and actors presented themselves publicly before the dramatic competitions. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 8**: Asklepieia — Asklepios — sacrifice in the Asklepios sanctuary neighbouring the theater (just before the Dionysia, per hide-money accounts). — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Evening of Day 8**: Eisagoge — Dionysos Eleuthereus — epheboi escorted the image of Dionysos Eleuthereus by torchlight from the Eschara (chapel near the Academy) to the theater precinct, re-enacting the original transfer from Eleutherai; a general banquet preceded the procession. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 9 (morning)**: City Dionysia (Great Dionysia) — pompe — Dionysos Eleuthereus — great sacrificial procession to the temple of Dionysos; phalloi carried (one per colony); kanephoroi preceded; dancing before the Twelve Gods; organized by the Archon Eponymos. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 9 (day)**: City Dionysia — dithyrambic choruses — boys' and men's choral competitions. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 9 (evening)**: City Dionysia — komos — Dionysos — carnivalesque revel-procession (mockeries and jokes), distinct from the hieratic pompe. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Day 10**: City Dionysia — comedies — Dionysos Eleuthereus — comic dramatic competitions. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Days 11–13**: City Dionysia — tragedies — Dionysos Eleuthereus — tragic dramatic competitions over three days. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **A few days after the Dionysia (Wilamowitz suggests full moon)**: Pandia — Zeus — a festival of Zeus; the day after: a mandatory popular assembly in the theater where complaints about the preceding festival could be lodged; phyle Pandionis celebrated specially. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Month (day unknown)**: Elaphebolia — Artemis Elaphebolos — originally deer were sacrificed to Artemis; later substitute cakes (plakountes) shaped like deer made of wheat-dough, honey, and sesame; the month is named after the festival. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Month (probable; inferred from Delian month Galaxion = Attic Elaphebolion)**: Galaxia — Meter Theon — a barley-and-milk porridge (galaxia) named the festival; epheboi sacrificed and dedicated a phiale to the goddess. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*

## Munichion

- **Day 4**: Eros festival — Eros — festival for Eros at his sanctuary on the north slope of the Akropolis; details not preserved; rock inscription reads "for Eros the festival on the 4th of Munichion." — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Day 6**: Delphinia — Apollon (+ Artemis) — a procession of suppliant maidens carrying hikétériai (branches wound with white wool) to the Delphinion; aetiologically connected to Theseus carrying a supplicant branch before sailing to Crete. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 16**: Munichia — Artemis Munichia (+ Hekate at crossroads privately) — solemn procession to the Artemis Munichia sanctuary; round amphiphon cakes with burning candles were brought; epheboi sacrificed and held a regatta around the Munichia peninsula; the day also liturgically commemorated the Salamis victory. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 19**: Olympieia — Zeus Olympios — a pompe in which the cavalry participated; a hippic agon; agonistic Olympia in the imperial period (none older than Hadrian). — *Source: zeus.md*

## Thargelion

- **Day 6**: Thargelia day 1 (purification) — Apollon Pythios — two pharmakoi (social outcasts) served as scapegoats: led through the city, beaten with fig-branches and squill, struck seven times on the genitals to concentrate and neutralize accumulated pollution, then expelled from the city; no ancient source attests killing in Attica. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Day 6**: Note — Philochoros (ap. Schol. Aristoph. Lys. 835) places an offering to Demeter Chloe on this day on the Akropolis; Deubner cites this as one of the conflicting pieces of evidence for the date of the Chloia (see also Anthesterion). — *Source: apollon-artemis.md / demeter-pt2.md*
- **Day 7**: Thargelia day 2 (firstfruits) — Apollon Pythios — firstfruits of the not-yet-ripe grain were cooked in a pot (thargelia); a loaf baked from the new grain was also called thargelos; a solemn pompe conveyed both to the deity organized by the Archon Eponymos; choral competitions (men's and boys' choirs with choregoi). — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Shortly before day 25 (Deubner rejects Photios' 19th)**: Kallynteria — Athena Polias — sweeping and cleaning of the Athena Polias temple; the annual renewal of the oil in the golden eternal lamp (Kallimachos' lamp) probably took place on this day; no ritual details survive. — *Source: athena.md*
- **Day 19 (extending into the night of the 20th)**: Bendideia — Bendis (Thracian) — double pompe in the Piraeus: one processional group of Athenian epichoroi, one of Thracian residents, both moving to the Bendis sanctuary near Munichia; crowns and a morning meal provided; at night a horseback torch-race by the Thracians; pannychis; officially recognized by 429/8 BCE. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Day 25**: Plynteria — Athena Polias — the Praxiergidai secretly removed the clothing and ornament from the old wooden image of Athena Polias and covered it; a procession led by the Basilinna took the image to Phaleron where two πλυντρίδες washed it in the sea; epheboi escorted; all other sanctuaries were roped off; the day was an ἡμέρα ἀποφράς (inauspicious). Date from Plutarch (Alkib. 34.1; 6th from month's end); Photios' 29th is rejected. — *Source: athena.md*

## Skiraphorion

- *(Skip Skira, Day 12 — already reviewed/corrected)*
- **Day 14**: Dipolieia / Buphonia — Zeus Polieus (+ Athena Polias) — barley and wheat laid on the altar on the Akropolis; an ox that ate from the grain was struck by the Thaulonidai clan's βουφόνος, who then fled; the ox-hide was stuffed with straw and set back on its feet; a formal mock-trial shifted blame down a chain until the knife was convicted and thrown into the sea; the ox was eaten. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Before day 20 (decrees honouring officials dated 20th and 28th)**: Diisoteria — Zeus Soter + Athena Soteira — main festival in the Piraeus: pompe with epheboi and sacrificial bull; rich animal sacrifices; sacrifice also to Asklepios and Hygieia; ephebe regatta to close; the Piraeus Diosoteriön was the location. Distinct from the modest city sacrifice below. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Day 30**: Eisiteria (city festival) — Zeus Soter + Athena Soteira — a modest state sacrifice by the priest of Zeus Soter on the Agora before the Stoa of Zeus "for the health of the Boule," placing the incoming new year under divine protection; never called "Diisoteria"; entirely separate from the Piraeus festival above. — *Source: zeus.md*
- **Skirophorion (no day named)**: Arretophoria (summer rite) — Athena Polias — the rite of carrying the ἄρρητα ἱερά (secret objects, reportedly shaped in dough as snakes and phalli, with pine branches); the two Arrephoren carried covered objects down via an underground passage near Aphrodite en Kepois, left them, and brought back something else; the month Skirophorion is explicitly named by the Etym. magnum. Deubner treats this as the "depositing" phase, with the autumn (Pyanopsion) rite as the "retrieval" phase. — *Source: athena.md*

## Unplaced / Date Unknown

- **Procharisteria** — Athena — "at the end of winter, when the grain began to sprout": all state officials sacrificed to Athena; described as the oldest sacrifice, connected later to the return of Kore. No Attic month or day is named by Deubner. — *Source: athena.md*
- **Kalamaia** — Demeter — spring or early summer, when grain stalks (κάλαμοι) reached a certain stage; sacrifice in the old manner with a pompe organized by the Hierophant and priestesses; also celebrated at Piraeus; date cannot be fixed. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Plerosia** — Zeus (in deme Myrrinus) / Demeter (in Piraeus inscription) — probably Posideon but uncertain (IG² 1183 text is damaged); listed among Demeter festivals in the Piraeus inscription between Thesmophoria and Kalamaia/Skira. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Balkytis / Ballētys** — Demeter (in honor of Demophon, son of Keleos) — annual festival at Eleusis involving a throwing ceremony (possibly stones) and mock-battles among the boys of Eleusis; date not specified. — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Epikleidia** — Demeter — festival of Demeter at Athens; nothing transmitted beyond the name (Hesych). — *Source: demeter-pt2.md*
- **Daphnephoria** — Apollon — dressing of the offering-basket, hymns, and laurel-branch carrying; Lydus says the 7th (Apollon's birthday), but Deubner marks the whole entry with a large question mark as poorly attested. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Pythais** — Apollon at Delphi — an irregular theoria sent only when Zeus sent lightning over the Harma ridge of Mount Parnes (observed by Pythaistai over three consecutive days of three consecutive months); when the sign appeared, Athens sent sacrificial animals, firstfruits, and choruses to Delphi; probably in the last months of the Attic year. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Brauronia** — Artemis Brauronia (at Brauron) — annual celebration; penteteris organized by hieropoioi was the main festival; girls aged 5–10 (arktoi) in saffron robes served the goddess; goat sacrifice; procession with kanephore; rhapsodic performances at the penteteris; a women's festival primarily. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Tauropolia** — Artemis Tauropolos (at Halai Araphenides) — night celebration with music and dancing (women and girls); a man had his throat scratched with a sword until blood flowed (symbolic remnant of human sacrifice); attested by Menander (Epitrepontes) and Euripides (IT 1449ff.). — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Amarysia** — Artemis Amarysia (at deme Athmonon) — festival with an agon reportedly rivalling the Euboean Artemisia in scale; cult imported from Amarynthos in Euboea. — *Source: apollon-artemis.md*
- **Promethia** — Prometheus — torch-race (λαμπαδηδρομία) starting from the altar of Prometheus in the Academy toward the city; the winning torch lit the great Panathenaia pyre; men's and boys' choirs; associated with Panathenaia but independent. Date uncertain. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Hephaistia** — Hephaistos (+ Athena) — torch-race identical to the Promethia; pompe; trumpet signal for each ox; 200 citizens lifted oxen to the altar; large animal sacrifice with raw meat distribution to metoikoi; annual and penteteris (from 329/8 BCE); pre-dates the reorganization decree of 421/0 BCE. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Eumenides / Semnai Theai festival** — Eumenides — pompe by the genos Hesychidai to the Areopag sanctuary; ram sacrificed to Hesychos; wineless libations and milk in clay vessels; Eupatrids explicitly excluded; epheboi baked the cakes. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Adonia** — Adonis — women on rooftops mourned with laid-out images of the dead Adonis; Adonis gardens (fast-sprouting seeds in sherds) were then thrown into water as rain-magic; fruit offerings before the lament; celebrated by women and hetairai and by Cypriot and Syrian Piraeus associations; Deubner infers an autumn date from grape and ripe-fruit imagery, but no fixed month is given. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Attideia** — Attis + Kybele — two-day or double festival of the Orgeones of the Great Mother; a κλίνη set up for the dead Attis; procession with two thrones; collection taken; taurobolia attested; a relatively late Hellenistic import (2nd century BCE and later). — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Anakia** — Dioskouroi — festival for the twin sea-gods; maritime character confirmed by naval terminology in an inscription; also celebrated in deme Plotheia. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Charites festival** — Three Charites — a festival of secret character performed before the gates of the Akropolis; Pausanias is the only source; date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Hermaia** — Hermes (of the palaestrae) — athletic/gymnasium festival in which the boys themselves sacrificed; Salaminian kleruchs held an agonistic version with male kanephoroi. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Museia** — Muses — school festival in which pupils demonstrated the results of their training; parallel to the Hermaia of the palaestrae. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Hekalos festival** — Zeus Hekalos + Hekale — the demos Hekale and surrounding demes sacrificed to Zeus Hekalos and honored Hekale; attributed as a Theseus foundation by Philochoros. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Enyalios at Skiradion (Salamis)** — Enyalios — a ritual re-enactment of Solon's capture of Salamis: an armed man leapt from a ship, another came from land, converging on the cape at Skiradion; Solon founded the sanctuary as a thank-offering for the victory. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Aianteia** — Aias (hero of Salamis) — festival on Salamis island; lectisternium with Aias's image in full armor; pompe; sacrifices to Aias and Asklepios; sea regatta, foot race, and torch-race; epheboi sailed to Salamis to sacrifice to Zeus Tropaios at the battle monument. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Nemeseia** — Nemesis (in relation to the dead) — a panegyris with ritual obligations performed for the deceased; possibly apotropaic (appeasing the wrath of the dead); attested by Demosthenes and lexicographers. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Epitaphia** — the war dead (+ Harmodios and Aristogeiton) — annual memorial in the Kerameikos; the Polemarch organized a funerary agon and chthonic libations (enagismata) for the war dead; funeral oration; epheboi's armed foot race and torch-race; Kosmet led epheboi to the Marathon polyandreion; winter timing inferred from Thucydides 2.34.1. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Amphiaraia** — Amphiaraos (at Oropos) — established after 338 BCE; pompe, sacrifices, and agons; both penteteris and annual/triennial celebration; grandly expanded after Sulla's gifts; an extra-Attic sanctuary but Athens sent its own theoria and maintained the cult. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Nemesia (at Rhamnus)** — Nemesis (at Rhamnus) — games/festival at the famous Nemesis sanctuary in north-east Attica; a Basileus donated cattle for sacrifice (inscription 236/5 BCE); distinct from the Athenian Nemeseia above. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Kabeiroi festival** — Megistoi Theoi / Kabeiroi — a pompe; epheboi sacrificed; a regatta in the Piraeus harbour. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Charmosyna** — Isis + Osiris — a "joy-festival" on the occasion of the finding/resurrection of Osiris (parallel to Roman Hilaria); attested as Attic by Hesychios; parallels at Kios and Tomi. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Kotyttia** — Kotys / Kotytto (Thracian) — private cult circles; initiation involving bathing; effeminate dances; tambourines and harps; mocked by Eupolis in his comedy *Baptai*; not a state festival. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Sabazios mysteries** — Sabazios — private night ceremonies with purification by clay and bran, the formula "Ἔφυγον κακόν, εὗρον ἄμεινον," and a sacramental drink poured from a mixing-vessel; known almost only from Demosthenes' hostile portrait (18.259). Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Theoinia** — Dionysos Theoinos — rural celebrations in individual demes; specific gennētai groups added a supplementary sacrifice; named from the epithet Theoinos (wine-god). Date unknown. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Iobakcheia** — Dionysos — named in the oath of the Geraeren ([Demosth.] 59.78) alongside the Theoinia; probably connected to the Anthesteria/Limnaion area; nothing else known of the actual ritual; distinct from the Imperial-era Iobakchen association. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Klematis** — Dionysos — a procession involving a vine-branch (κλῆμα); epheboi participated in both this festival and the Elaphebolion pompe (IG² 1043.31); possibly linked to Elaphebolion but this is uncertain. — *Source: dionysos-pt2.md*
- **Heroa (at the urban Asklepion)** — Heroes — the priest of Asklepios sacrificed a cattle-victim at the Heroa beside the city Asklepion; a pannychis was connected. Date unknown. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
- **Pluton lectisternium (Eleusis)** — Pluton — Pluton received a lectisternium at Eleusis; attested by IG² 1933.1; no date specified. — *Source: various-heroes-apaturia.md*
