## VERSCHIEDENE GÖTTER

### Promethia
**Month/Day**: uncertain (associated with Panathenaia; also at Hephaistia)
**Deity**: Prometheus (old Attic fire-god)
**Ritual**:
- Torches were lit at the altar of Prometheus in the Academy
- The torch-race (λαμπαδηδρομία) started from that altar and moved through the Kerameikos toward the city
- The winner's torch was used to light the pyre at the great Panathenaia
- Men's and boys' choirs performed
- All torch-races organized by the Basileus
**Aition**: The race's purpose was to replace fire polluted through long use with fresh, sacred fire from the fire-god's altar; speed preserved its sanctity. (Deubner: this is the originary sense, not aition in the mythological sense.)
**Participants**: Torch-runners (lampadarchai), choirs; Basileus organized
**Sources**: Harpokr. s.v. λαμπάς; Schol. Aristoph. Frö. 131; Paus. 1.30.2; IG 1² 84; IG 2² 1138; [Xen.] resp. Ath. 3.4
**Notes**: Deubner discusses whether the Panathenaia used the altar of Eros (Pisistratan addition) or Prometheus as the starting point; he concludes Prometheus was original for the Promethia, and Eros for the Panathenaia specifically. L. Weber's contrary view is rejected.
**Deubner**: printed p. 209–211 / PDF p. ~235–237

### Hephaistia
**Month/Day**: uncertain (date uncertain; penteteris from 329/8 BCE under Kephisophon)
**Deity**: Hephaistos (with Athena)
**Ritual**:
- Torch-race identical in structure to the Promethia (see above)
- Pompe (procession) escorting sacrificial animals to the sanctuary
- A trumpet signal announced each ox being led to the altar
- 200 Athenians lifted the cattle and carried them to the altar
- Large animal sacrifice (at least 3 oxen); raw meat distributed to Metoikoi
- Choral/musical performances
- A penteteris (quadrennial) festival alongside the annual celebration, from 329/8 BCE
**Participants**: 20 Hieropoioi organized the festival; Metoikoi received meat; 200 citizens lifted oxen; choirs
**Sources**: IG 1² 84 (reorganization decree, 421/0 BCE); Herodot 8.98 (implies pre-421/0 existence); Aristot. Ath.pol. 57.1; 54.7; IG 2² 1138; Reisch, Öst. Jahresh. 1.1898.55ff.; Stengel, RE 8.290
**Notes**: Deubner argues the Hephaistia predated the 421/0 inscription (a reorganization, not a foundation), using Herodot's reference to the torch-race for Hephaistos (who died ca. 425 BCE). Stengel held the opposite; Deubner refutes this. The inscription also mentions the great Panathenaia in the same context. Hephaistos's role at the Apaturia is noted as connected (see Apaturia below).
**Deubner**: printed p. 211–213 / PDF p. ~237–239

### Eumeniden (cult festival)
**Month/Day**: uncertain
**Deity**: Eumenides / Semnai Theai (at the Areopag)
**Ritual**:
- The genos Hesychidai organized a Pompe to the sanctuary of the Eumenides on the Areopag
- Before arriving at the Areopag, a ram was sacrificed to Hesychos (a guardian of the underworld's entrance)
- Offerings to the Eumenides: cakes and wine-free libations (νηφάλια) in clay vessels; milk in clay vessels
- The women of the genos were specifically responsible for the libations
- Burnt offerings (σφάγια — animals completely destroyed) and torches added (reconstructed from the end of Aeschylus's Eumenides, which Deubner reads as a ritual imitation)
- The Eupatrids were explicitly excluded from the rite (because of their genealogical connection to Orestes the matricide)
- No slaves permitted; only free persons of good repute
- Cakes baked specifically by chosen epheboi
**Participants**: Genos Hesychidai (both men and women); free persons only; epheboi baked the cakes; Eupatrids excluded
**Sources**: Polemon in Schol. Soph. O.C. 489; Kallim. Frg. 123 Schneider; Philo, *quod omnis probus liber sit* 140 (6.39.21 Cohn); Aeschyl. Eum. 1005–1042 (reconstructed); Toepffer, Att. Gen. 170–177
**Notes**: No specific date in the sources; Deubner notes the striking exclusion of the noble Eupatrids (normally the elite of cultic life) as a reflection of the Orestean pollution mythology. The nephaelia libations are characteristic of chthonic cult throughout.
**Deubner**: printed p. 213–214 / PDF p. ~239–240

### Posidea
**Month/Day**: Posideon, probably 8th day (the eighth was Poseidon's special day for Athenians generally)
**Deity**: Poseidon (also Poseidon Khamaizelios in a private calendar)
**Ritual**:
- State festival (details largely lost); the month Posideon was named after it
- Private sacrifice calendar: on 8th Posideon, sacrifice of cakes and a wine-free libation to Poseidon Khamaizelios (epithet probably expressing his old chthonic character)
- Penteteris at Sunion with a Theoria sent from Athens and a regatta
- Agon of kyklical choirs in the Piraeus
**Participants**: State; private worshippers; Athenian Theoria to Sunion
**Sources**: Theophr. char. 28.4; Plut. Thes. 36.6; Nilsson, Griech. Feste 79–82; IG 2² 1367.16ff.; IG 1² 190.8; Herod. 6.87; Lys. 21.5
**Notes**: Deubner calls the festival "verschollen" (lost/obscured) for the state level; he reconstructs the date from Poseidon's general eighth-day association among Athenians (Plut. Thes. 36.6). Poseidon also participated in the Haloa (see Demeter section). The Khamaizelios epithet, meaning roughly "he who presses the earth," is taken as a relic of his chthonic aspect.
**Deubner**: printed p. 213–215 / PDF p. ~239–241

### Aphrodisia (Aphrodite Pandemos)
**Month/Day**: probably shortly after the start of the Attic year (Hekatombaion area); exact date uncertain
**Deity**: Aphrodite Pandemos
**Ritual**:
- A Pompe (procession)
- A decree of 287/6 BCE (IG 2² 659) specifies preparatory duties assigned to the Astynomoi:
  - Acquire a dove to purify the sanctuary (its blood sprinkled on the precinct)
  - Whitewash the altars
- Tar the roofs
  - Wash the statues
  - Obtain purple paint for repainting the statues
- The Tetradists (a thiasic association meeting on the 4th of each month) also venerated Aphrodite Pandemos
- The hetairai celebrated their own Aphrodisia (visiting the sanctuary and holding private banquets)
- The demos Plotheia celebrated Aphrodisia
**Participants**: Astynomoi (administrators); Tetradists; hetairai; private worshippers
**Sources**: IG 2² 659; Athen. 659d; Hesych s.v. Τετραδισταί; Alkiphr. 4.16.3 Schepers; IG 2² 1172.5; Plautus, Poenulus 190
**Notes**: Deubner finds the cult "relatively old" from the phrase κατὰ τὰ πάτρια in the decree. The dove-blood purification parallels the Kallynteria (purification of the Polias temple). Deubner is uncertain whether the Aphrodisia and the Pandemos Pompe were the same event.
**Deubner**: printed p. 215–216 / PDF p. ~241–242

### Hekalos / Hekale (local demo festival)
**Month/Day**: uncertain (demo festival, not polis-level)
**Deity**: Zeus Hekalos + Hekale (also called Hekaline)
**Ritual**:
- Sacrifice offered by the demos Hekale and neighboring demes to Zeus Hekalos and Hekale
- The cult was attributed as a foundation of Theseus: on his return from slaying the Marathonian bull, he honored the old woman Hekale who had sheltered him, finding her dead
**Aition**: Philochoros: surrounding demes gathered to sacrifice to Zeus Hekalos and honored Hekale, calling her Hekaline (diminutive of affection), in memory of Theseus's hospitality with her (Plut. Thes. 14.2–3).
**Participants**: Demo Hekale and surrounding demes
**Sources**: Philoch. bei Plut. Thes. 14.2–3; Etym. m. 319.46 (also from Philochoros); Steph. Byz. s.v. Ἑκάλη; Hesych s.v. Ἑκάλειος Ζεύς; IG 2² 772.9ff.
**Notes**: Deubner argues that "Zeus Hekalos" is not an original epithet but shows the juxtaposition of two independent divine names (as with Zeus Keraunos → Zeus Keraunios). The original deity was called Hekalos, with Hekale as feminine counterpart (gender-differentiation of a single divine concept, as in Hekatos/Hekate). The new Plutarch edition reads "Ἑκαλείῳ" (a later form) instead of the Meursius reading "Ἑκάλῳ" which Deubner prefers. Deubner notes the political motivation: connecting local cults with Theseus integrated them into the Athenian state-cult system.
**Deubner**: printed p. 216–217 / PDF p. ~242–243

### Enyalios (ritual at Skiradion, Salamis)
**Month/Day**: uncertain
**Deity**: Enyalios (war god)
**Ritual**:
- A ritual re-enacting the capture of Salamis under Solon
- A fully armed man leaped from a ship with war-cries; another man came from land; both converged on the Skiradion (cape on Salamis)
- This re-enacted the two-pronged attack Plutarch describes: naval detachment + Solon's land force
- Solon founded a sanctuary of Enyalios at the Skiradion to commemorate the victory
**Aition**: Deubner believes Solon founded the sanctuary and the ritual simultaneously as a thank-offering for the victory over the Megarians; he prefers this over interpreting the ritual as an old rite that influenced the narrative. Petersen's identification of a red-figure cup (Taf. 24) with this ritual is accepted by Deubner.
**Participants**: Armed men re-enacting the capture
**Sources**: Plut. Sol. 9; red-figure cup (Taf. 24); Toepffer, Beitr. zur griech. Altertumswiss. 14ff.; Petersen, Jahrb. 32.1917.145ff.
**Notes**: Deubner rejects connecting the ritual with the cult of Aias (despite Wettläufe occurring there) and with Athena Skiras (proposed by L. Weber). The Skiradion/Skiras connection he sees as purely toponymic.
**Deubner**: printed p. 218–219 / PDF p. ~244–245

### Bendideia
**Month/Day**: 19th Thargelion (also given as 20th, since the festival extended into the night)
**Deity**: Bendis (Thracian goddess)
**Ritual**:
- Day: a double Pompe in the Piraeus — one by the epichoroi (Athenian association), one by the Thracians — setting out separately but joining for one procession
- Both processional groups ultimately moved from the hearth of the Prytaneion to the sanctuary of Bendis in the Piraeus (near the sanctuary of Artemis Munichia)
- Representatives of the Piraeus Thracians arranged washing facilities at the Nymphaion for the arriving procession
- Crowns and a morning meal (ἄριστον) were provided to all participants at the sanctuary
- Priest and priestess prayed for all members (including the city-based association)
- Evening / Night: horseback torch-race (ἀφιππολαμπάς), performed by the Thracians
- Pannychis (all-night festival)
**Participants**: Two sacred associations: (1) epichoroi Athenians, (2) Thracian residents of Piraeus (with an additional urban Athenian Thracian group that had split off)
**Sources**: Plat. Rep. 327a, 328a; Proklos ad Plat. Tim. 9b (S. 26.13 Diehl); Proklos ad Tim. 27a (S. 85.28); Schol. Plat. Rep. 327a; IG 1² 310.208; IG 2² 1255, 1283, 1324, 1496 Aa 86, b 117
**Notes**: Cult was officially recognized by the state by 429/8 BCE. Deubner infers the reason for placing the festival in Thargelion from the Thracian practice of women bringing wheat-ears to Bendis at the offering (harvest connection); the Thargelia immediately before were also characterized by first-fruits offerings (ἀπαρχαί). The Piraeus Thracian association may have been founded ca. the dramatic date of Plato's Republic (Deubner tentatively). Date: two ancient sources disagree on 19th vs. 20th Thargelion; Deubner explains: the festival extended into the night and thus crossed into the next civil day.
**Deubner**: printed p. 218–220 / PDF p. ~244–246

### Adonia
**Month/Day**: probably autumn (Deubner's inference from the grape imagery and Theokrit's mention of ripe fruit)
**Deity**: Adonis
**Ritual**:
- Celebrated by the women of Athens on the rooftops of their houses
- Two main elements:
  1. **Mourning rites**: Images (εἴδωλα) of Adonis were laid out in the manner of corpses being borne out for burial; women beat themselves, enacted the burial ceremony, sang laments (θρῆνοι); dancing and drinking could also accompany the lament (Aristoph.)
  2. **Adonis gardens (κῆποι)**: Seeds of fast-sprouting plants (fennel, lettuce) were sown in sherds or any containers filled with earth; these "gardens" were carried onto the rooftops; at the end of the festival they were thrown into water
- Fruit (grapes, tree-fruits) offered to Adonis before the lament ceremony (Theokrit)
- Incense burned
**Aition**: The mourning represents Adonis's death; the Adonis gardens are a sympathetic magic for fertility: the swift sprouting guarantees abundant vegetation in the coming spring. The casting into water = rain-magic to ensure the vegetation's growth. Deubner rejects Mannhardt's identification of Adonis as a general vegetation daemon; he follows Frazer's practical interpretation (food/hunger was the motivation) while modifying the explanation of the gardens (not the dying = the sprouting is what matters).
**Participants**: Women of Athens (and hetairai); also an association of Cypriot Salaminians (IG 2² 1290) and a thiasos of the Syrian Aphrodite (IG 2² 1261) in the Piraeus
**Sources**: Plut. Alkib. 18.5; Plut. Nikias 13.11; Aristoph. Lys. 389–396; Theokrit. 15.112; Hesych s.v. Ἀδώνιδος κῆποι; Suid. s.v. Ἀδώνιδος κῆποι; Zenob. 1.49; IG 2² 1290.10, 1261.9; attic vases (Taf. 25)
**Notes**: Deubner dates the festival to autumn based on the grape imagery (grapes can be kept until spring but are iconographically autumnal) and Theokrit's mention of ripe tree-fruits. He rejects Plutarch's association of the Adonia with the Sicilian expedition (summer 415 BCE) as "romantic fiction." The festival's Syrian/Cypriot origin is evident. Hetairai favored it. No fixed Attic month is given by Deubner — he treats the timing as approximate.
**Deubner**: printed p. 220–221 / PDF p. ~246–247

### Attideia
**Month/Day**: double festival (possibly two separate days or a two-day feast)
**Deity**: Attis (and Kybele)
**Ritual**:
- Orgeones of the Great Mother celebrated the Attideia
- A priestess set up a κλίνη (ceremonial couch), presumably for the dead Attis (mourning rite)
- A procession with two thrones (carrying images of Kybele and Attis)
- During the procession, a collection was taken
- Women carrying phialai (bowls) and those surrounding the goddess's image wore silver ornaments
- Taurobolia (bull-blood initiations) also attested at Athens
**Sources**: IG 2² 1315.9; IG 2² 1328.9, 1329.14; IG 3.172–173; Foucart, Assoc. relig. 92–93; Cumont, Rel. Orient.⁴ 63f.
**Notes**: Deubner notes the two Attideia may be two separate festivals per year (like Roman parallel) or a two-day celebration. The Attideia are a relatively late Hellenistic import; these inscriptions are 2nd century BCE and later.
**Deubner**: printed p. 221–222 / PDF p. ~247–248

### Sabazios mysteries
**Month/Day**: uncertain
**Deity**: Sabazios
**Ritual**:
- Night ceremonies organized (at least in one documented case) by the mother of Aeschines
- Aeschines' role: dressed in a fawn-skin (νεβρίς), read liturgical texts to the initiates, poured the sacramental drink from a mixing-vessel
- Purification ritual: the initiates were rubbed with clay and bran; then they rose and spoke the formula: "Ἔφυγον κακόν, εὗρον ἄμεινον" ("I escaped evil, I found the better")
- Daytime street-behavior of the worshippers is noted separately as non-ritual
**Sources**: Demosth. 18.259
**Notes**: Our only detailed source is Demosthenes' hostile portrait. These were private mysteries, not a state festival. Deubner includes it in the survey of foreign cults finding acceptance in private Athenian circles.
**Deubner**: printed p. 222–223 / PDF p. ~248–249

## HEROEN UND TOTE

### Theseia
**Month/Day**: 8th Pyanopsion
**Deity**: Theseus (hero)
**Ritual**:
- State sacrifice with a Pompe (procession), confirmed from 2nd-century BCE inscriptions
- Libations (χοαί / νεκύσια) to Theseus — Deubner infers from a confused scholion that erroneously associates the 8th Pyanopsion with Choes
- Public feast for the demos from the sacrificial meat (large skin-money expenditure attested)
- Ἀθάρη (milk porridge / atharē) specially eaten at this festival
- Lampas (torch-race) and multiple other agones organized by an Agonothetes
- Epheboi appeared in armed parade
- Day before: a ram sacrifice to Konnidas (Theseus's tutor/guardian)
- Day before: sacrifice to the Amazons
- Kybernesia (separate festival): for Nausithoos and Phaiax (Theseus's legendary helmsmen), celebrated in Phaleron near the sanctuary of Skiros
**Aition**: The grandly enlarged celebration from 475 BCE followed Kimon's recovery of Theseus's bones from Skyros. Deubner argues older genos cults (Phytalidai, and families tracing descent from children of the Cretan tribute) preceded the state cult. Philochoros claimed there were four separate Theseia.
**Participants**: State; Athenian demos (fed from the sacrifices); epheboi in armed parade; Agonothetes (2nd century BCE); Phytalidai (earlier)
**Sources**: Plut. Thes. 36.4; Hesych s.v. Θήσεια; Schol. Aristoph. Plut. 627–628; Aristoph. Plut. 627f. with Schol.; Aristot. Ath.pol. 15.4; Philoch. bei Plut. Thes. 17.6–7; 35.3; IG 2² 956ff.; IG 2² 1496 Ac 134f.; Plut. Thes. 4 (Konnidas), 27.7 (Amazons), 17.7 (Kybernesia)
**Notes**: Deubner notes that the Theseia's connection with the Choes is likely a scribal confusion in the scholion. He argues that Konnidas was likely the progenitor of the genos Koneidai, linked to Theseus secondarily (perhaps through a convenient festival date). The Kybernesia similarly predate their association with Theseus; Nausithoos and Phaiax as Phaeakian figures (Odyssey 7.62) were originally sea-helpers, later mythologized into Theseus's crew.
**Deubner**: printed p. 223–225 / PDF p. ~249–251

### Herakleia
**Month/Day**: Metageitnion (Kynosargic); probably early Hekatombaion (Marathonic)
**Deity**: Herakles
**Ritual**:
**A. Kynosargic Herakleia (ἐν Κυνοσάργει / Diomeian Herakleia):**
- Celebrated in the Kynosarges sanctuary in the demos Diomeia
- A psephisma of Alkibiades specified that monthly sacrifices be performed by the priest together with the παράσιτοι (table-companions of the hero)
- The parasitoi had to be of illegitimate birth (νόθοι), matching Herakles' own heroic status
- Refusal to serve as parasiton was actionable before a court
- Depicted on the calendar frieze of Hag. Eleutherios in Metageitnion

**B. Marathonic Herakleia:**
- Penteteris (quadrennial) with agones
- Athen sent a special Theoria (festival delegation)
- Philochoros mentions parasitoi here too (Marathon was part of the Tetrapolis)
- Festival probably in early Hekatombaion (the assembly discussing it met four days before the end of Skirophorion)

**C. Herakles Alexikakos and Melon (Melite):**
- Sacrifice: an apple (μῆλον) with four sticks inserted at the bottom (= legs) and two at the top (= horns), making it resemble an ox
- This substitution arose because the Melitans did not sacrifice cattle themselves
- Deubner explains: Herakles absorbed an old apple-god; the apple-offering was the original; the animal-disguise was added later to give the hero's offering a more satisfying appearance

**D. Mesogeioi Herakles festival:**
- Organized by the cult-league of the Mesogeioi
- Events: Pompe, lectisternium, meat-distribution, wine-drinking
- Personnel: priest of Diomos, a pyrophoros (fire-carrier), and a girl-leader (koragogos, probably for kanephoroi)
**Sources**: Harpokr. s.v. Ἡράκλεια; Demosth. 19.125; Aristoph. Frö. 651; Polemon bei Athen. 6.234e; Apollodor Frg. 115 (FGrHist 2.1075); Hesych s.v. Μήλων Ἡρακλεῖ; Suid. s.v. Μήλειος Ἡρακλῆς; Aristot. Ath.pol. 54.7; IG 2² 1245, 1247, 1244; Judeich, Topogr. v. Athen⁴ 169f., 422f.
**Notes**: Deubner stresses that of the many Attic Herakleia, Harpokration says only the Marathonic and Kynosargic were especially prestigious. The parasitos institution (table-fellowship with the hero) is attested for Herakles more broadly. The apple-sacrifice at Melite receives a full comparative analysis; Poll. 1.30f. reports a Theban parallel with a ram represented by an apple.
**Deubner**: printed p. 225–227 / PDF p. ~251–253

### Aianteia
**Month/Day**: uncertain
**Deity**: Aias (hero of Salamis)
**Ritual**:
- The main festival was on Salamis island
- The Athenians set up a lectisternium for Aias: his image was laid on a kline in full armor (the armor may have been placed on the couch, or the image was decorated with it)
- After the Battle of Salamis (480 BCE), the old hero-festival was elaborately expanded
- Pompe (procession)
- Sacrifices to Aias and also to Asklepios
- Agones: regatta on the sea, foot race (long and short), torch-race (Lampas)
- Prelude: Epheboi sailed to Salamis and sacrificed to Zeus Tropaios at the Battle of Salamis monument (Paus. 1.36.1) — at least once immediately before the Aianteia, and Deubner takes this as standard
**Participants**: Athenian state; epheboi (sailing to Salamis); agonists
**Sources**: Hesych s.v. Αἰαντία; Schol. Pind. Nem. 2.19; Herod. 8.64; IG 2² 1006.28ff., 30f., 72f.; IG 2² 1008.17ff., 22f.; IG 2² 1011.16ff.; IG 2² 1028.24f., 27; IG 2² 1029.14f.; IG 2² 1227.32; v. d. Mühll, *Der große Aias* 23; Toepffer, RE 1.928
**Notes**: Whether the kline was set up in Athens or Salamis is uncertain (v. d. Mühll assumes Salamis). The date is unknown.
**Deubner**: printed p. 227–228 / PDF p. ~253–254

### Amphiaraia
**Month/Day**: uncertain (Oropos festival)
**Deity**: Amphiaraos (hero/seer)
**Ritual**:
- Established after 338 BCE when Athens recaptured Oropos
- Two tiers: (1) penteteris (grand, quadrennial) and (2) annual or triennial celebration
- Both with Pompe, sacrifices, and agones
- After Sulla's gifts to the sanctuary: the festival was grandly expanded and renamed τὰ Ἀμφιάραα καὶ Ῥωμαῖα
- Still celebrated in the Imperial period as Ἀμφιάραα
**Sources**: IG 7.414; 4253.13f.; 4254.15ff.19.36; Klee, *Zur Gesch. d. gymnischen Agone* 58f.; Preuner, Herrn. 57.1922.80ff.
**Notes**: Technically a non-Attic sanctuary (at Oropos in Boeotia/disputed territory), but Athens sent its own festival delegation and maintained the cult.
**Deubner**: printed p. 228–229 / PDF p. ~254–255

### Genesia
**Month/Day**: 5th Boedromion (state festival)
**Deity**: The Dead (chthonic offering also to Ge)
**Ritual**:
- Public state festival (δημοτελής) for the commemoration of the dead
- Sacrifice also to Ge (Earth), who holds the dead in her bosom
- Private commemorations were also held annually (probably on the same day)
**Aition**: No mythological aition; Deubner discusses the etymology: Γενέσια relates to γενήτης and means "a festival of the ancestors" (paralleling Roman Parentalia), not a birth festival. Kern's interpretation (connecting death and life through Earth) is rejected. Deubner follows W. Schmidt's etymology.
**Participants**: Entire Athenian demos (state-level); private families
**Sources**: Bekker, Anecd. 1.86.20 (with Philochoros and Solon as sources for the 5th Boedromion); Hesych s.v. Γενέσια; Herod. 4.26; Ammon. S.36 Valck.; Schol. Plat. Alkib. I 21c; Etym. m. 225.29f.; Poll. 3.102; Phryn. ecl. S.103 Lob.
**Notes**: Deubner rejects Ammonios's claim that private Genesia fell on the individual's death anniversary (he says both public and private celebrations coincided on the 5th Boedromion). The alternative name Nekysia is not an official name but a generic term for the rite type. The autumn Genesia is described as the counterpart to the spring Anthesteria soul-festival.
**Deubner**: printed p. 228–230 / PDF p. ~254–256

### Epitaphia
**Month/Day**: winter (inferred from Thukydides 2.34.1; not the 8th Pyanopsion)
**Deity**: War dead (state heroes); also Harmodios and Aristogeiton
**Ritual**:
- Annual memorial in the Kerameikos for the war dead buried in the state cemetery (δημόσιον σῆμα)
- New burials of fallen warriors were combined with this annual ceremony
- The Polemarch organized: (1) the funerary agon (ἐπιτάφιος ἀγών) and (2) enagismata (chthonic libations) for the war dead and for Harmodios and Aristogeiton
- Funeral oration (from the 5th century, attested to Lekythos depictions)
- Epheboi: armed foot race and Lampas (torch race) against visiting epheboi from other cities
- The Kosmet also led epheboi to the Marathon polyandreion (mass grave), where they laid crowns and offered Totenopfer to those fallen for freedom
**Participants**: Polemarch (organizer); epheboi; Kosmet; citizens; funeral speakers
**Sources**: Aristot. Ath.pol. 58.1; Thukyd. 2.34.1; IG 2² 1006.22.69; IG 2² 1011.9; IG 3.106.108.110; Lekythoi (Taf. 26–27, Brückner, Ath. Mitt. 35.1910.200ff.); Dittenberger, Syll.³ 717 Note 10; Hesych s.v. ἐπ' Εὐρυγύῃ (= Melēsagoras tradition)
**Notes**: Deubner notes that a connection with the Theseia (which others proposed) is "ganz unsicher" — the winter date from Thukydides argues against the 8th Pyanopsion. The fake Atthis attributed to Amelesagoras claimed the agon was founded in honor of Eurygyes (= Androgeos son of Minos); Deubner is skeptical but leaves it open whether an old hero cult provided the kernel.
**Deubner**: printed p. 229–230 / PDF p. ~255–256

## APATURIA

### Apaturia
**Month/Day**: Pyanopsion, three consecutive days (Day 1: Dorpia; Day 2: Anarrhysis; Day 3: Kureotis); some sources add a fourth day (Epibda)
**Deity**: Zeus Phratrios + Athena Phratria (main); Dionysos Melanaigis (secondary role, unclear); Hephaistos (solemn torchlight offering)
**Ritual**:
**Day 1 — Dorpia ("Supper Day"):**
- The phratores (members of a phratry) came together and ate an evening meal (δόρπον) communally
- The state contributed a subvention (δημοτελής contribution)
- Five days before the Dorpia, the priest announced the place of celebration (Demotionidai inscription)

**Day 2 — Anarrhysis ("Sacrifice Day"):**
- The name derives from ἀνερύειν/ἀνερρύειν = to bend back the animal's head before slitting the throat (the standard Greek sacrificial gesture)
- Sacrifice to Zeus Phratrios and Athena (Phratria)

**Day 3 — Kureotis ("Registration Day"):**
- The formal registration of new phratry members
- Three categories of sacrifice:
  1. **Meion** ("the small offering"): sacrifice for infants/young children born in the previous year, providing their preliminary registration; the priest received 3 obols and a share of the meat
  2. **Koureion** (or Koureiōtis sacrifice): sacrifice for adolescents entering ephebeia (associated with a haircut — the boys' hair was cut and the hair-offering made); the priest received 1 drachma + bread + wine; Deubner argues this was at the point of entering the ephebate (not at age 3–4 as Busolt proposed)
  3. **Gamelia**: sacrifice offered by a new husband when introducing his young wife into his phratry; equivalent to the koureion for males

- **Wine libation (oinisteria)**: offered when children were introduced, mentioned by Pollux

- **Solemn Hephaistos offering**: phratores put on their finest garments, lit torches from the hearth fire, and sang hymns to Hephaistos while holding the torches

- **Dionysos Melanaigis**: plays a role at the Apaturia that Deubner cannot more precisely define

- **Olive branch (κοροθάλη)**: placed at the house door at the occasion of the koureion and gamelia (apotropaic)

- **Four life-events marked at the Kureotis**: birth, Choes-feast (= confirmatio of the child's status), ephebeia, marriage — all requiring phratry registration
**Aition**: Deubner briefly notes the ancient popular etymology connecting "Apaturia" with ὁμοπατόρια (same-father / common-father) as given in the scholia. He also notes that the etymology via ἀπάτη (deception) is in the same scholion. The linguistic derivation is uncertain (Wackernagel, cited by Deubner, finds difficulties with the ου of Ἀπατούρια on either etymology). Deubner does not discuss the Ionian aition myth at length.
**Participants**: All Ionian phratries throughout Attica; state subsidized; priests of each phratry; women members of phratries (the gamelia concerns them); adolescent boys (koureion); newborn children and their fathers (meion)
**Sources**: Schol. Aristoph. Ach. 146 (fullest account); Herod. 1.147 (pan-Ionian character); Theophr. char. 3.3; Harpokr. s.v. Ἀπατούρια; IG 2² 1237 (Demotionidai inscription, with priest's payments and timing of announcement); Dittenberger, Syll.³ 921 Notes 5, 31; Plat. Euthyd. 302d; Poll. 8.107; Poll. 3.52; Isaeus 3.76.79; 8.18–20; Etym. m. 533.37–51; 118.55; 119.15f.; 98.56ff.; Hesych s.v. Κουρεῶτις, s.v. oinistēria; Suid. s.v. Κουρεῶτις; Bekker, Anecd. 1.416–417; Proklos ad Plat. Tim. 21b (S. 88.11 Diehl); Schol. ad Tim. (S. 364 Herrn.); Konon 39; Philyllios Frg. 8 (CAF 1.784); Schol. Aristoph. Fried. 890; IG 2² 1299.29f. (Panaktos, confirming widespread Attic celebration); Harpokr. s.v. λαμπάς (for the Hephaistos torchlight); Iobakchen inscription (for the four life-events)
**Notes**:
- Deubner resolves the confusion between meion and koureion by reading Pollux's statement that the koureion applies to "children of advanced age" (alongside the gamelia for girls). He concludes: meion = infant registration (born in the preceding year); koureion = ephebic registration (first shaving/haircut moment). This contradicts Busolt (who put the koureion at age 3–4).
- The Demotionidai inscription shows the priest receives double at the koureion vs. meion, confirming the koureion's greater solemnity.
- The precise role of Dionysos Melanaigis is "nicht näher kenntlich" — Deubner quotes the sources (scholia to Aristoph. Ach. 146 Z. 35f.; Frieden 890 Z. 54ff.; Proklos; Bekker) but does not attempt a reconstruction.
- The Hephaistos torchlight is quoted verbatim from Istros (in Harpokr.): those wearing their finest garments take lit torches from the hearth and sing to Hephaistos.
- Deubner notes that the four occasions marked at the Kureotis (birth, Choes, ephebeia, wedding) correspond exactly to the four occasions for wine-gifts among the Iobakchen (γεννήσεως, χοῶν, ἐφηβείας, γάμου).
**Deubner**: printed p. 230–234 / PDF p. ~256–260
