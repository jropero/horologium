## Overview

The Egyptian Book of the Dead (*Reu nu pert em hru*, "Chapters of Coming Forth by Day") is not a single text but a collection of ~190 spells compiled from the Middle Kingdom through the Late Period. Several spells explicitly name a civil calendar date or festival in their title or rubric, making them directly relevant to the app's Egyptian calendar and festival data. The most actionable content follows.

---

## 1. Festival Connections

### Chapter CXXX — Birthday of Osiris (Epagomenal Day 1)

**Full title:** "Another Chapter of Making Perfect the Khu, which is to be Recited on the Birthday of Osiris, and of Making to Live the Soul for ever." (Papyrus of Nu, BM 10,477, sheet 17.)

**Civil calendar date:** The Birthday of Osiris is the 1st of the five Epagomenal Days (Heriu-renpet, "days upon the year"), which falls outside the 360-day calendar immediately after the last day of month 12 (Mesore). In the civil calendar this is day 361.

**Spell excerpt:**
> "The heavens are opened, the earth is opened, the West is opened, the East is opened, the southern half of heaven is opened, the northern half of heaven is opened, the doors are opened, and the gates are thrown wide open to Rā as he cometh forth from the horizon. The Sektet boat openeth for him the double doors and the Mātet boat bursteth open the gates; he breatheth, and the god Shu cometh into being, and he createth the goddess Tefnut."

**Rubric (pp. 211–212):** "This chapter shall be recited over a boat of the God Rā which hath been painted in colours in a pure place... upon the Birthday of Osiris. If these ceremonies be performed his soul shall have existence, and shall live for ever, and shall not die a second time."

**App relevance:** Direct festival-date link for Epagomenal Day 1. The spell is associated with the solar bark and Osiris' rebirth — suitable as literary source material for the epagomenal days info panel. The rubric explicitly names the occasion, making this the best-attested BoD / calendar-day link.

### Chapter CXXXIII — Day of the Month (New Moon / Monthly Festival)

**Full title:** "The Book of Making Perfect the Khu which is to be Recited on the Day of the Month." (Papyrus of Nu, BM 10,477, sheet 16.)

**Civil calendar date:** "Day of the Month" (*pesdjentiu* or the monthly lunar festival). This is the day of the new crescent moon — the beginning of a new lunar month, which coincides with the opening of the civil month in the ideal synchronised calendar.

**Spell excerpt:**
> "Rā riseth in his horizon, and his company of the gods follow after him. The god cometh forth out of his hidden habitations, and food falleth out of the eastern horizon of heaven at the word of the goddess Nut who maketh plain the paths of Rā... Behold, thou art that Image of gold, and thou dost possess the splendours of the disks of heaven and art terrible; thou comest, being renewed each day."

**Rubric (pp. 215–216):** "This Chapter shall be recited over a boat four cubits in its length and made of green porcelain on which have been painted the divine sovereign chiefs of the cities; and a heaven with its stars shall also be made... Let not the eye of any man whatsoever look upon it with the exception of thine own self, or thy father, or thy son... and the gods shall look upon him as a divine being like unto themselves; and mankind and the dead shall look upon him and shall fall down upon their faces, and he shall be seen in the underworld in the form of the radiance of Rā."

**App relevance:** New moon festival text. Reinforces the app's existing lunar calendar mechanics. Could appear as a Fonte Antiqua quote on the first day of each lunar month.

### Chapter CXXXV — Moon Renewing Itself (Monthly New Moon)

**Full title:** "Another Chapter to be Recited when the Moon Reneweth Itself on the Day of the Month." (From Lepsius, *Todtenbuch*, Bl. 55.)

**Civil calendar date:** The new moon, first visibility of the lunar crescent.

**Spell excerpt:**
> "Osiris unfettereth, or, as others say, openeth the storm cloud in the body of heaven, and is unfettered himself; Horus is made strong happily each day. He whose transformations are great hath offerings made unto him at the moment, and he hath made an end of the storm which is in the face of the Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant. Verily he cometh, and he is Rā in his journeying, and he is the four celestial gods in the heavens above."

**Rubric:** "If this chapter be known by the deceased he shall become a perfect Khu in the underworld, and he shall not die therein a second time, and he shall eat his food side by side with Osiris. If this chapter be known by him upon earth he shall be like unto Thoth, and he shall be adored by the living ones."

**App relevance:** Short, poetic month-renewal spell. Suitable as a Fonte Antiqua quote on the first day of each new lunar month in the Egyptian calendar display.

### Chapter CXXXVI A (second version) — Festival of Six

**Full title:** "Another Chapter of Making Perfect the Khu; [it shall be recited] on the festival of Six." (Papyrus of Nu, BM 10,477, sheet 16.)

**Civil calendar date:** The "Festival of Six" (*Shenet* or *Senet*) was a 6-day festival associated with Osiris and the dead. It was celebrated in multiple months but principally near the end of Khoiak (Month 4 of Akhet), making it one of the major festivals of the Osirian cycle. The number six echoes the six days of mourning for Osiris.

**Spell excerpt:**
> "Behold now, O ye luminaries in Ânnu (Heliopolis), ye people in Kher-aba, the god Kha hath been born; his cordage hath been completed, and the instrument wherewith he maketh his way hath he grasped firmly; and the Osiris Nu is strong with them to direct the implement of the gods. The Osiris Nu hath delivered the boat of the sun therewith... and he cometh forth into heaven. The Osiris Nu saileth round about in heaven, he travelleth therein unto Nut, he journeyeth along with Rā."

**Rubric (p. 221):** "This Chapter shall be said over a figure of the deceased which shall be placed in a model of the Boat of the Sun... Every Khu for whom such things are done shall have an existence among the living ones, and he shall never perish, and he shall have a being like unto that of the holy God; no evil thing whatsoever shall attack him."

**App relevance:** Directly links a named civil calendar festival ("Festival of Six") to a BoD spell. Suitable for the Khoiak festival entry in `egyptianFestivalsData.ts`.

### Chapter CXL — Last Day of II Peret (End of Month 6 / Mekhir)

**Full title:** "The Book which is to be Recited on the Last Day of the Second Month of the Season Pert when the Utchat is Full in the Second Month of the Season Pert." (From Lepsius, *Todtenbuch*, Bl. 57.)

**Civil calendar date:** The last day of Month 6 (II Peret = Mekhir, the sixth month of the Egyptian civil year; Coptic Μεχίρ). This is also mentioned in Chapter CXXV (see §2 below): "on the day when the Eye (Utchat) of Rā is full in Ânnu (Heliopolis) at the end of the second month of the season Pert." The full Eye of Ra (= the full moon, or the Eye/Utchat amulet charged with solar power) on this specific date was a significant occasion.

**Spell excerpt:**
> "The divine Power hath risen and shineth in the horizon, and the god Tem hath risen out of the odour of that which floweth from him. The Khus shine in heaven and Het-benbenet rejoiceth, for there is among them a form which is like unto themselves; and there are shouts and cries of gladness within the shrine, and the sounds of those who rejoice go round about through the underworld, and homage is paid unto him at the decree of Tem and Heru-khuti."

**Rubric (pp. 231–232):** "This Chapter shall be recited over an Utchat of real lapis-lazuli or of mak stone plated with gold, before which shall be offered every kind of fair and pure oblation when Rā sheweth himself on the last day of the second month of the season Pert. And thou shalt make another Utchat of jasper and place it upon such part of the dead man's body as thou pleasest... whilst this chapter is being recited four altars shall be lighted for Rā-Tem, and four for the Utchat, and four for the gods who have been mentioned."

**App relevance:** Gives a precise civil calendar date: last day of Month 6 (II Peret = day 180 of the civil year). This is a moon/solar conjunction day. Could be added to `egyptianFestivalsData.ts` for Month 6 day 30.

### Chapters CXLI–CXLII — Festivals of Amentet (9th Day)

**Full title:** "Here beginneth the Book which a Man shall Recite for his Father or for his Son during the Festivals of Amentet, whereby he shall Make him Perfect with Rā and with the Gods, and whereby he shall have his Existence with them; it shall be Recited on the Ninth Day of the Festival." (Papyrus of Nu, BM 10,477, sheet 15.)

**Civil calendar date:** The "Festivals of Amentet" most likely refer to the Beautiful Feast of the Valley (*Heb Nefer en Ipt*), one of the most important Theban festivals, celebrated in the 10th month (II Shomu = Paoni/Epiphi). The spell is specifically for the 9th day of the festival.

**Chapter CXLI contains offerings to 64 named entities including:** Osiris (governor of Amentet), Ra-Heru-khuti, Nu, Maat, the Boat of Ra, Temu, the Great and Little companies of gods, Horus, Shu, Tefnut, Seb, Nut, Isis, Nephthys, the four Sons of Horus (Mesthi, Hapi, Tuamautef, Qebhsennuf), Thoth, the Gods of South/North/West/East, and the Gods of the horizon.

**Chapter CXLII (the second section)** expands with 124 invocations to Osiris in all his local forms — Osiris-Un-nefer, Osiris-the-living-one, Osiris-lord-of-life, through regional epithets like Osiris-in-Re-stau, Osiris-in-Abydos, Osiris-Orion (Sah), Osiris-Ptah-lord-of-life, Osiris-in-Tanenet, Osiris-in-heaven, etc.

**App relevance:** Rich list of Osiris epithets (suitable for the deity info modal). The "9th day of the Festival of Amentet" is a dateable civil calendar event for II Shomu.

### Chapter XVIII — Festival Night References (embedded, no specific date)

Chapter XVIII and its parallels contain ritual narration of mythological festival nights, including:
- "the night of the 'things of the night' festival" in Sekhem (Letopolis)
- "the festival of the Haker" (a major Osirian festival, known from other sources to fall in month III or IV Peret)
- "the festival of the ploughing and turning up of the earth in Tattu [Busiris]"
- "the night of the battle and overthrow of the Sebau-fiend in Tattu"
- "the night when Isis lay down to make lamentation for her brother Osiris"
- "the night of the judgment of those who are to be annihilated"

These festival names are referenced but no civil date is given within the chapter text. Cross-reference needed with `egyptianFestivalsData.ts` for the Haker festival.

## 2. Day/Date References within Spell Texts

### Chapter CXXV — Calendar Date Embedded in the Negative Confession Text

Within the **Introduction** to Chapter CXXV (Papyrus of Nu, sheet 22), text page 192, appears this passage:

> "My purity is the purity of that great Bennu which is in the city of Suten-henen (Heracleopolis), for, behold, I am the nose of the God of the winds, who maketh all mankind to live **on the day when the Eye (Utchat) of Rā is full in Ânnu (Heliopolis) at the end of the second month of the season Pert** (i.e., the season of growing) [in the presence of the divine lord of this earth]. I have seen the Eye of Rā when it was full in Ânnu, and therefore let not evil befall me in this land and in this Hall of double Maāti."

**Civil calendar date:** End of Month 6 (II Peret = Mekhir), day 180. This is the same date as Chapter CXL — strong attestation of an important calendar occasion (the "fullness of the Utchat in Heliopolis"). Budge's footnote confirms: "the month called by the Copts Mekhir, the sixth month of the Egyptian year."

## 4. Hemerology Overlaps

No spells in this Budge edition are explicitly marked "auspicious" or "inauspicious" in the hemerological sense used in the Egyptian Calendar of Lucky and Unlucky Days (Cairo Papyrus 86637, Sallier Papyrus IV). The BoD rubrics use different terminology: spells are effective (*ur* "great"), and their performance averts evil (*nen-neferu* "no evil shall come") rather than labelling days themselves.

**Cross-reference opportunity:** The hemerological notes in `egyptianHemerologyData.ts` mention days when Osiris, Horus, Sokar and Ma'at are propitiated. The BoD chapters linked to specific dates (CXXX, CXL, CXLII) share deity overlap with the hemerology. In particular:
- End of Month 6 (day 180, II Peret last day) is marked "good" (*nfr*) in several hemerology sources.
- The Birthday of Osiris (Epagomenal Day 1) is consistently a major ritual day.

## 5. Epagomenal Days — Five Gods

The five Epagomenal Days (*Heriu-renpet*, "days upon the year") are the birthdays of Osiris (day 1), Horus-the-Elder (day 2), Set (day 3), Isis (day 4), and Nephthys (day 5). The BoD connection:

- **Osiris (Epagomenal Day 1):** Chapter CXXX — explicitly titled "to be recited on the Birthday of Osiris." The strongest direct link.
- **Horus, Set, Isis, Nephthys:** No Chapter explicitly titled for their birthdays found in this edition. However:
  - Isis and Nephthys appear as protective mourners in dozens of spells (Chapters 1, 17, 125, 137–139) without a specific birthday rubric.
  - Set appears as the adversary overcome by Horus in Chapters XVII–XX.
  - The "birth" of all five gods is narrated mythologically across Chapters XVII, XIX and CXXIX (Hymn to Osiris).

**Note:** The myth of Nut giving birth to the five gods over the five days (*Heriu-renpet*) is Herodotus II.4 and Plutarch's *Isis and Osiris*, not a BoD text. This Budge edition does not reproduce that narrative. For the app's epagomenal days content, Chapter CXXX is the primary BoD source for Osiris; the other four days require non-BoD sources (Plutarch, Dendera texts).

## App Integration Ideas

### 1. Epagomenal Days info panel — Chapter CXXX (high priority)
Add Chapter CXXX (Birthday of Osiris) as a sourced literary quote to the Epagomenal Day 1 entry in `egyptianFestivalsData.ts`. This is the only BoD spell with an explicit epagomenal birthday rubric. The opening lines ("The heavens are opened...") are poetic and translatable.

### 2. Month 6 festival entry — Chapters CXL + CXXV cross-reference (high priority)
The "fullness of the Utchat on the last day of II Peret (Month 6 = Mekhir, day 30)" is confirmed by two independent spell rubrics (CXL and CXXV). This warrants a festival entry for Month 6 day 30 in `egyptianFestivalsData.ts` — call it *Meh Utchat* (Filling of the Eye) or similar. Both spells refer to offerings before a lapis-lazuli Eye amulet, a specific ritual that can be noted.

The two new-moon spells could supply a rotating Fonte Antiqua quote for the first day of each Egyptian lunar month in the app. The quote from CXXXIII ("thou comest, being renewed each day") is short enough for the date display.

### 4. Hall of Maāti reference panel — Chapter CXXV 42 assessors (medium priority)
The complete assessor list is suitable for an expandable "Hall of Maāti" modal or info panel within the Egyptian calendar section. Each assessor's city and denied sin could display as a readable list. App context: any festival linked to Osirian judgment (Khoiak, Sokar Festival, Epagomenal days).

## Edition Note and Cautions

- **Budge (1898) is dated.** His translations contain known errors and archaic diction. For app-facing text, cross-check key passages against:
  - R. O. Faulkner, *The Ancient Egyptian Book of the Dead* (1972; revised ed. 1985) — standard modern English translation.
  - T. G. Allen, *The Book of the Dead or Going Forth by Day* (SAOC 37, 1974).
  - E. Hornung, *Das Totenbuch der Ägypter* (1979; German) — authoritative for rubrics.
- **Spell numbering:** The chapter numbers used here follow Budge/Lepsius. They match Faulkner's spell numbers (which are now standard) only approximately. Budge's Chapter CXXX = Faulkner Spell 130, etc.
- **Theban Recension:** This edition reproduces the New Kingdom Theban Recension, which is the richest in calendar-specific rubrics. The Saïte Recension (26th Dynasty onward) reorganises some chapters and has different rubric language.
- **"Birthday of Osiris" vs. "Sokar Festival":** Some modern sources link Spell 130 to the Sokar Festival (IV Akhet 26) rather than the Birthday of Osiris (Epagomenal Day 1). The Budge rubric clearly says "Birthday of Osiris." The Sokar Festival link comes from other papyri and the Sokar Funerary Papyri (not BoD). Do not conflate.
