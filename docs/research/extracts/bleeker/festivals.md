PDF: 82 pages (two-up scan, ca. 160 printed pages). Chapters I–II are theoretical; Chapters III–VI provide festival data.

## SOKARIS / KHOIAK CYCLE (IV Akhet = app month 3)

### General date range
The great festival calendar of Ramses III gives a festival cycle of **10 days for Khoiak, running from 21 to 30 Khoiak** (i.e. days 21–30 of IV Akhet). (p. 70/84 in Bleeker's page numbering)

### Earlier festival: 14 Khoiak
From the **Middle Kingdom**, at least two Sokaris festivals are attested:
- One on **14 Khoiak** (IV Akhet day 14)
- One on **16 Mechir** (II Peret day 16) — in the New Kingdom this shifted to 27 Mechir

Both are pre-New Kingdom attestations. The source gives these in the chapter "Data" on the festival of Sokaris.

### The Denderah Khoiak text (Ptolemaic, Loret edition)
The Ptolemaic temple text at Denderah describes **Osiris festivals from 12–30 Khoiak**:
- **12 Khoiak**: ritual of breaking open the ground (ḫbf tꜣ) — Bleeker argues this is the authentic original date for this ritual (see below under 22 Khoiak)
- **30 Khoiak**: interment of Osiris took place (also interpreted as another instance of ḫbf tꜣ in a secondary sense)

The Denderah text also mentions prominently the "mould of Sokaris" in which an image symbolising the god (restored after Seth's attack) was fashioned.

### The 10-day cycle (Festival Calendar of Ramses III, Medinet Habu)
Per note 4 on p. 74 of Bleeker, the specific ceremonies within the 10-day cycle are:

| Day (IV Akhet) | Ritual |
|---|---|
| **21 Khoiak** | Day of opening the window in the sanctuary of Sokaris |
| **22 Khoiak** | Day of the festival of breaking open the ground (ḫbf tꜣ) |
| **23 Khoiak** | Day of holding a procession in the sanctuary of Sokaris |
| **24 Khoiak** | Day of the placing of Sokaris "in their midst" (of the dead?) |
| **25 Khoiak** | The Nḏrj.t festival — garlands of onions were woven, apparently to be worn at the Sokaris festival on day 26 |
| **26 Khoiak** | **The festival of Sokaris** — the principal festival; the solemn circuit with the divine boat (ḥnw) was celebrated |
| **27–29 Khoiak** | Offerings to Ptah-Sokaris-Osiris and his ennead; no special Sokaris ritual mentioned in the calendar |
| **30 Khoiak** | **Erection of the djed-pillar** (ḏd-pillar). The interment of Osiris also belongs here (Denderah text). |

Source: JEOL 7, pp. 336/7; OIP Vol. LI: IV Pl. 226; Loret, *Les Fetes d'Osiris au mois de Choiak*

### Primary deity: Sokaris (= Sokar)
- A chthonic, funerary god, "god of death who encompasses potential life" — Bleeker's characterisation
- His sacred boat is the **ḥnw** (meaning unknown), the oldest of the divine boats
- Usually depicted as a falcon; later identified as Ptah-Sokaris-Osiris
- Home sanctuary: pr ḥnw ("house of the ḥnw"), located at Memphis
- His domain = Rꜣ-štꜣw ("the secret way"), associated with 4th and 5th hours of the Amduat

### The boat procession on 26 Khoiak (Medinet Habu reliefs)
The Medinet Habu reliefs depict the following acts at the festival of 26 Khoiak:
1. King offers products of the field to Sokaris (enthroned, with "great ennead" of nine gods)
2. King makes offering of incense to Khnum, Ḥr-ꜣwj, and Shesmu
3. Boat of Sokaris is in a chapel; king offers to Sokaris in his many qualities
4. The ḥnw boat is drawn forth; king follows with eight pairs of priests, princes, and courtiers holding a chord
5. Emblem of Nefertem carried horizontally on a litter
6. Horus falcon on a tall standard with double royal crown
7. Double row of priests carrying emblems and offerings
8. Five smaller ships carried on poles at the head of the procession
9. Group prostrating or kneeling at the head

The festival almost certainly lasted longer than one day (nocturnal elements, multiple costume changes of the king, multiple subsidiary rituals).

### Ritual of breaking open the ground (ḫbf tꜣ)
- Performed in connection with Sokaris (and later Osiris)
- Two functions: (1) founding of temples — king demarcates site with pickaxe; (2) commencement of field work
- Per the Book of the Dead (1:21–22): "I cut open the earth on the day of working the land in Herakleopolis" — combined with the episode of placing Sokaris' boat on its sledge
- Per festival calendar of Ramses III: held on **22 Khoiak** (IV Akhet day 22)
- Per Denderah text (Loret): took place on **12 Khoiak** (and in a secondary sense on 30 Khoiak)
- Mythological basis: earth is drenched with blood of Seth and his consorts (victory of Osiris); Book of the Dead 18:25 seq.
- Bleeker's conclusion: the ritual was *originally* connected with Sokaris, not Osiris; it formed the prelude to Sokaris' own festival

### The Nḏrj.t festival (25 Khoiak)
- Garlands of onions were woven at this festival
- Onion = symbol of fertility (Kees)
- Connection to Sokaris suggests Sokaris was expected to promote fecundity
- Meaning and etymology of the name are obscure (Bleeker)

### Connexion to the winter solstice
In the Ptolemaic (Alexandrian calendar), 26 Khoiak coincided with the winter solstice. Bleeker notes this "reveals nothing about the original nature of the festival" — it is a calendar coincidence, not the origin of the festival.

### 30 Khoiak and 1 Tybi
Bleeker discusses the theory (Moret, Sethe) that Menes celebrated the unification of Egypt with the festivals of 26 Khoiak, and that on **1 Tybi** he ascended the throne. Hence 1 Tybi (= first day of I Peret, app month 4) was considered the ideal day for coronation. Bleeker is sceptical of this particular theory but confirms that 1 Tybi "marked a new beginning" and "the time of flowering began" (p. 113).

## WAGY FESTIVAL / Wꜣg (I Akhet = app month 0)

### Date
- **Eve: 17 Thoth** (I Akhet day 17) — "the eve of the wꜣg festival"
- **Festival itself: 18 Thoth** (I Akhet day 18)

Source: Bleeker p. 132 (ch. VI) — cross-confirmed by the Hepzefi contracts from Siut (Middle Kingdom).

### Character
- One of the few festivals specifically for the dead (wꜣg festival = festival of the dead par excellence)
- Also dedicated to Osiris (as god of the dead)
- Cattle were sacrificed
- Model boats were placed in graves (wishing the dead a successful voyage to Abydos)
- Sowing and harvesting were preparatory measures to the wꜣg festival (pyramid texts)

### Hepzefi Contracts (Middle Kingdom, Siut)
The most detailed picture of the Wagy/New Year festival complex comes from ten contracts between Hepzefi (count of Siut) and local priests for his funerary cult. The festivals on which ceremonies were to be performed included:
1. First intercalary day — Upwaut visits Anubis; loaves offered
2. **New Year's eve** — lights kindled in temple of Upwaut; torch ritual
3. **New Year's morning** — loaves offered; torch kindled
4. **New Year's day (1 Thoth)** — loaves, torches, procession
5. **Night of the wꜣg festival (17 Thoth)** — lay priests offer bread to image of Hepzefi; procession with lights to lowest terrace of grave; beer offered; torches
6. **Wꜣg festival (18 Thoth)** — loaves offered; keeper of wardrobe gives torch to funerary priest; procession with lights and "glorification" of the deceased
7. **Each procession day** — meat and beer offered

The ritual of kindling torches (fire = dispels darkness, grants joy) recurs throughout. The priests "glorify" (fꜣḫ) the deceased — the causative of "to make (the deceased) a ꜣḫ (being of light)".

Patron gods of the Wagy ritual at Siut: **Upwaut** ("opener of the ways") and **Anubis** (mummification; guide of the dead).

### The Feast of Thoth (19 Thoth)
Bleeker's Ahmose I inscription lists in sequence: "the feast of Wag, the feast of Thoth" — suggesting the Feast of Thoth follows immediately after the Wagy festival. This is consistent with 19 Thoth as the Feast of Thoth (I Akhet day 19), though Bleeker does not elaborate on this festival separately.

## MIN FESTIVAL (I Shomu = app month 8)

### Date
- **11 Pachon** (I Shomu, the 9th civil month = app month 8)
- Celebrated at harvest time

Source: Bleeker pp. 112, 121, 165 — and his monograph *Die Geburt eines Gottes* (1956).

### Deity
- Min, god of Koptos — "a typical god of vegetation" (Bleeker), of sexuality and fertility
- Min manifests his "power of resurrection" through the harvest

### Character and rituals
A royal festival with three highlights:
1. **Harvest rite**: the king ceremoniously strikes the sickle into the corn — the first reaper, enacting a mythical act magically. The king walked around "the field" whilst reciting spells seven times.
2. **Renewal of royal dignity**: the king orders release of four birds to proclaim to South, North, West, East that "just as Horus, son of Isis and Osiris, has received the white and the red crown, so has the king received the white and the red crown" — an annual renewal of kingship.
3. **Raising / "birth" of Min**: the mfw.t (birth) of the god — the conviction that Min possesses divine life and continuously renews himself.

The festival is depicted on the Northern and Eastern walls of the second court of Medinet Habu.

The min festival has the "programme text" (a continuous text describing the course) that the Sokaris festival lacks.

### Position in the year cycle (Bleeker's scheme)
Bleeker (pp. 113–114) reconstructs the annual festival scheme:
- **1 Thoth (I Akhet 1)**: New Year — very great celebration, beginning of Nile floods
- **Khoiak festivals (IV Akhet 21–30)**: Sokaris festivals — preparation of soil, sowing of seed
- **1 Tybi (I Peret 1)**: beginning of flowering season — ideal date for coronation
- **11 Pachon (I Shomu 11)**: Min festival — harvest, confirmation of the pharaoh's ability to rule

## BEAUTIFUL FEAST OF THE VALLEY (II Shomu = app month 9)

### Date
- **Payni** (II Shomu = 10th civil month = app month 9), between the harvest and the Nile floods
- No specific day-number is given by Bleeker for this festival

### Deity
- Amon-Re (principal); Hathor acts in the background as patroness of festive joy and the dead

### Character
A visit by the **divine boat of Amon-Re** from his temple at Karnak to the necropolis on the west bank of Thebes.
- Boat drawn on sledge to Nile bank, embarked on a ship, transported across the Nile and through canals to the mortuary temple
- King directed the proceedings; his ship led the way
- Sacred boat of Amon followed by a flotilla of smaller boats

Funerary function: the dead were commemorated at their tomb, which became "a house of the joy of the heart." The mortuary image of the deceased was apparently taken in Amon's procession and returned to the grave; a family festival followed. A funerary banquet was held.

Text quoted: "Emerge from the earth, behold Re and follow Amon in his beautiful festival of the desert valley."

The ritual included: choirs, sistra, necklaces, songs, "glorification" (fꜣḫ) of the deceased, a flower bouquet (symbolising renewed life), a festive banquet.

Foucart's interpretation: the boat journey "exprimer par image la totalité ou la moitié de la vie active du soleil." The boat also journeys to Luxor on New Year's Day. By the Valley festival, the deceased is wished the power of the sun-god who rises each day from the underworld.

Source reference: G. Foucart, *La belle fête de la vallée* (1930); S. Schott, *Das schöne Fest vom Wüstentale* (1953).

## OPET FESTIVAL (II Akhet = app month 1)

### Character (summary from Bleeker, no specific date given)
- Held in honour of Amon-Re
- Amon visits his ꜥip.t ("harem") in the Luxor temple to celebrate what appears to be the hieros gamos (sacred marriage)
- The three boats of Amon, Mut, and Khonsu appear together at this festival
- The boats are depicted in the colonnade of Luxor

Bleeker does not give a specific date for the Opet festival; he only mentions it in passing in Chapter III as one of the major Amon festivals. The full treatment is in Wolf (1931) — referenced: W. Wolf, *Das schöne Fest von Opet* (1931).

Note: The Opet festival occurs in II Akhet (Phaophi) based on other sources; Bleeker does not specify the month.

## SED FESTIVAL (Ḥb-Sd / Jubilee)

### Date
- Bleeker: no fixed calendrical date — celebrated after a variable period of rule, not strictly every 30 years
- When connected to the New Year scheme, it is sometimes placed at **1 Tybi**
- The Hb-sd of Re and Horus at Edfu are dated to **1 Thoth** (festival calendar at Edfu)

### Character
A renewal of the king's royal office — not a "thirty-year festival" (Bleeker is explicit: "not a regnal jubilee" and "not a thirty-year festival").

Key rituals: (a) "appearance" of the king; (b) circuit of the wall/field; (c) ritual of the erection of the djed-pillar (added to the Hb-sd of Amenhotep III — Bleeker says this is a "later, though not arbitrary extension"). Renewal of divine life — the conviction that the gods continuously renew themselves.

## NEW YEAR FESTIVAL (I Akhet day 1 = app month 0, day 1)

### Date
**1 Thoth** (I Akhet day 1) — very great celebration; beginning of the Nile floods.

Hepzefi contracts (Siut): "New Year's eve" and "New Year's day" are listed alongside the Wagy festival as festival days for funerary cult. Lights are kindled on New Year's eve.

At Edfu, the journey of the boat of Amon to Luxor (Opet) occurs on New Year's Day, "marque le renouvellement de la vie du monde" (Foucart).

## INTERCALARY DAYS (Epagomenal Days = app month -1 in app)

Bleeker notes: "the five intercalary days, on which according to the myth the gods of the group of Osiris (viz. Osiris, Seth, Isis, Nephthys and Horus) were born, were celebrated with due festivity" (p. 33). 

In the Hepzefi contracts, the **first intercalary day** is specifically listed as a festival on which Upwaut visits Anubis and offerings are made.

## MONTHLY AND HALF-MONTHLY FESTIVALS

Pyramid texts record festive sacrificial ceremonies performed:
- **1st of every month** (new moon celebration, from time immemorial)
- **6th of every month** 
- **Half-way through the month** (full moon)

Ahmose I inscription listing: "the monthly feast of the first of the month, the feast of the coming forth of the sem, the feast of the night-offerings on the fifth of the month, the feast of the sixth of the month, the feast of Hakro, the feast of Wag, the feast of Thoth, and at the first of every season."

## HATHOR FESTIVALS

Bleeker mentions Hathor festivals known from texts and representations at the temple of Edfu:
- The sacred marriage (hieros gamos) between Hathor and Horus at Edfu — Hathor journeys annually from Denderah to Edfu by boat (with a flotilla escort)
- The festival calendar at Denderah lists 35 festivals; processions in honour of Hathor are frequent
- Christophe's study shows that the festival calendar at Edfu contains Hathor festivals of a "definitely agrarian nature"
- Hathor acts as patroness of the Valley festival (in the background)
- Hathor = patroness of the dead in the graves at Meir (Middle Kingdom); "the mistress of the necropolis"

No specific day-numbers are given by Bleeker for the Hathor/Edfu festival.

## NOTES FOR APP VALIDATION

1. **26 Khoiak = app month 3, day 26**: This is the principal Sokaris festival, the most important of the cycle. The boat circuit (ḥnw procession) is its climactic ritual.
2. **30 Khoiak = app month 3, day 30**: Erection of the djed-pillar; interment of Osiris. End of the Khoiak cycle.
3. **18 Thoth = app month 0, day 18**: Wagy festival (festival of the dead, also dedicated to Osiris). Eve = day 17.
4. **11 Pachon = app month 8, day 11**: Min festival (harvest, renewal of kingship). Uncertain — Bleeker says "probably on the 11th."
5. **Payni (app month 9)**: Beautiful Feast of the Valley. No specific day given.
6. **1 Thoth = app month 0, day 1**: New Year.
7. **Khoiak 14 (app month 3, day 14)**: Older Sokaris festival, Middle Kingdom attestation.
8. **Khoiak 12 (app month 3, day 12)**: Original date of the ḫbf-tꜣ ritual per the Denderah text.
9. Bleeker explicitly argues that the Khoiak festivals (21–30) were **originally Sokaris festivals**, only later absorbed into the Osiris cycle. The Denderah text's attribution to Osiris reflects late-period fusion of Sokaris and Osiris.
10. The Sokaris festival is **not** primarily a vegetation/fertility festival (Bleeker argues against this): Sokaris is the god of death who encompasses potential life, protector of the dead earth before it is broken open for seed.
