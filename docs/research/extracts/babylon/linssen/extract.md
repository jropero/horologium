# Linssen — The Cults of Uruk and Babylon: Festival Extract

**Source:** Marc J.H. Linssen, *The Cults of Uruk and Babylon: The Temple Ritual Texts as Evidence for Hellenistic Cult Practices*, Cuneiform Monographs 25, Brill/Styx, Leiden/Boston, 2004 (originally Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam diss., 2002).

**Scope of this extract:** Chapters II.4 (Annual ceremonies, pp. 61–91), II.4.5 (New Year festival, pp. 71–86), II.4.6 (Brazier festivals, pp. 87–88), II.4.7 (Cultic calendars, pp. 88–91), and II.8 (Other festivals, pp. 117–128). Monthly ceremonies (II.3) are noted where they affect specific days. The book covers Hellenistic Uruk and Babylon only; it does not discuss Nippur, Assyrian, Sippar, or Nippur sources systematically.

**Primary texts cited:**
- Uruk: TU 39–40, TU 41, TU 42+, TU 43, TU 48, BRM 4,7, KAR 132, K 3753, LKU no. 51, OECT 9, VAT 7815, VAT 7816
- Babylon: RAcc. (Thureau-Dangin, *Rituels accadiens*), SBH VII–VIII, BRM 4,25, BM 32206+, BM 32656, BM 41577, Çağirgan *Festivals* 1, AB 244, Astronomical Diaries (Sachs–Hunger), Grayson *Chronicles* no. 13b

## Month 1 — Nisannu

### Attested events

**Babylon (Bēl/Marduk cult):**
- Day 1: New Year festival of Bēl begins — meager evidence; offering-table arranger enters courtyard of Bēl with key; regular offerings attested (Çağirgan *Festivals* 1; AB 244, rev. 30) (pp. 79, 85, 90)
- Days 2–5: Standard daily procedure for each day: high priest rises at exact double hour of night, washes in Tigris/Euphrates water, approaches Bēl and Bēltīja (= Zarpanītu), removes curtain; prayers recited; pīt bābi-ceremony performed; temple enterers (*ērib bīti*) and lamentation priests perform (RAcc., 127ff.) (pp. 79–80, 90)
- Day 2: Small part of ceremony preserved; prayer to Bēl; pīt bābi (RAcc., 130–41) (p. 80)
- Day 3: High priest calls in metalworker, carpenter, goldsmith to make two figurines (used days 3–6, destroyed day 6 in ceremony involving Nabû); figures kept in temple of Madānu and Eḫursagtila until day 6 (RAcc., 160ff.) (p. 80)
- Day 4: Hand-raising prayer (*šuillakku*) for Bēl and Bēltīja; festival begins with high priest's praise of Bēl three times; **Enūma Eliš recited or enacted by the high priest** in the late afternoon (RAcc., 222ff.; fn. 424) (p. 81)
- Day 5: **King enters Babylon with Nabû; preparations of Esagila (cellae of Bēl and Nabû) for royal visit; high priest goes to open country; exorcist and slaughterer purify temple with sheep carcass; carcass thrown into river; craftsmen leave city. Golden canopy brought out, placed over Ezida; high priest and craftsmen utter cry of distress (ikkillu). Offering table set up before Bēl. Then: king enters Esagila, his hands washed; high priest removes scepter, loop, mace and Crown of Kingship, seats them before Bēl, strikes king's cheek, pulls by ears, makes him kneel; king denies neglecting gods/temple/people; high priest assures king of Bēl's support; returns regalia; strikes cheek again (if tears flow, Bēl is satisfied)** (RAcc., 288ff., 338ff., 413ff.) (pp. 81–82)
- Day 6 (or 6/7/8?): BM 41577 fragment — high priest visits cellae of Bēl and Bēltīja, recites Sumerian and Akkadian blessings; possibly deals with preparations before day 8 procession (A.R. George, *Studies W.G. Lambert* no. 1, 260ff.) (pp. 83, 90)
- Day 8: **Clothing ceremony for Marduk; procession of Bēl from Esagila ("taking Bēl by the hand") together with Nabû and all other gods gathered in Esagila, moving to the akītu-temple (*Bīt ikribi*) "in the open country" (*ina ṣēri*); ḥarû-ritual performed in front of Esagila gate; Ištar of Babylon taken by hand from temple of Gula to join procession** (Diaries no. -204; SBH VIII; Grayson *Chronicles* no. 13b; chronicle 13b) (pp. 83–85, 90)
- Day 11: **Return from akītu-temple back to Esagila passing Dais of Destinies (*parak šīmāti*); Bēl determines destinies for next year; marriage festival (*ḥadaššūtu*) probably between Marduk and Zarpanītu; Nabû referred to as bridegroom in SBH VIII** (SBH VIII; Diaries no. -245; AB 244) (pp. 83–85, 90)

**Uruk (Anu cult):**
- Day 7: New Year festival of Anu — detailed procession from Rēš-temple to akītu-temple *Bīt ikribi* (TU 39–40) (pp. 72–77, 88)
- Day (8): Procession of Anu; New Year festival of Anu (KAR 132) (pp. 75–77, 88)
- Day 10: New Year festival of Anu; hand-raising prayer (*šuillakku*) recited (TU 43) (pp. 77, 88)
- Day 11: New Year festival of Anu; hand-raising prayer on Dais of Anu; lamentation priest performs (TU 43) (pp. 77, 88)
- Also: pīt bābi ceremonies on days 4, 6, 7 of Nisannu (VAT 7816, obv. 11, 17, 21) (p. 37)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 1: CONFIRMED — Akītu begins Day 1 in Babylon.
- Day 4: WRONG_RITUAL (partial) — App combines "Enūma Eliš + king humiliated" on a single day. Per RAcc., **Enūma Eliš is Day 4** but **the king's humiliation is Day 5** (RAcc. lines 413ff., p. 81–82). These are distinct events on consecutive days.
- Day 8: CONFIRMED for grand procession and Bēl moving to akītu-temple. "King reinvested" is partly misplaced: the reinvestiture of royal regalia occurs on Day 5 (after the Day 5 humiliation), not Day 8. Day 8 is the procession departure.
- Day 11: CONFIRMED — Festival ends, destinies fixed at Dais of Destinies, Bēl returns. The marriage ceremony is also Day 11.

## Month 2 — Aiaru

### Attested events

**Babylon (Nabû cult, SBH VIII):**
- Day 2: Clothing ceremony for Nabû (*lubuštu*); procession of Nabû from Ezida to Euršaba; **marriage ceremony of Nabû** — Nabû as bridegroom (*ša ḥa-da-áš-šu-tú*) with Ninkali (= Nanāja?) in Euršaba; he shines "like the moon" (SBH VIII, II, 15–21) (pp. 68, 70–71, 90)
- Day 6: Ceremony for Nabû in garden (*ina gipāri*) of Anu, probably in Emeurur; he goes to garden, sits (SBH VIII, II, 22) (pp. 63, 90)
- Day 7: Ceremony for Nabû at Emeurur, the garden of Anu; procession of Nabû to Emeurur (SBH VIII, II, 23ff.) (pp. 63, 68, 90)
- Day 17: Procession of Nanāja from Euršaba to "the garden of the Mountain" (SBH VIII, II, 27–28) (pp. 68, 90)

**Uruk:** No data attested for Ajjaru in the Hellenistic period (p. 88).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 2 (wedding of Nabû + Nana): CONFIRMED in substance — marriage of Nabû on Day 2 in Babylon (SBH VIII). The partner is Ninkali (possibly = Nanāja/Nana), not named simply "Nana." The text describes Nabû as bridegroom, Euršaba as venue.
- Day 6 (public exposition of Nabû): CONFIRMED — ceremony for Nabû in garden on Day 6 in Babylon.
- Day 7 (Nabû crowned king of gods at Uruk): WRONG_DEITY / WRONG_RITUAL — Linssen attests Day 7 ceremony for Nabû only in **Babylon** (SBH VIII), not Uruk. Linssen has no Uruk data for month II. "Crowned king of gods" language is not in these texts; Day 7 is a procession and garden ceremony.
- Day 13 (e-ma of Ištar in Assyria): NOT IN LINSSEN — this event comes from Assyrian sources, outside Linssen's scope (Uruk/Babylon Hellenistic ritual texts).

## Month 3 — Simānu

### Attested events

**Babylon:**
- Day 3–8: Ritual for Ištar of Babylon (Eturkalamma) — *kurgarrû* goes to Emeurur (Nanāja's temple), throws fruit at wall behind Nanāja's cella (symbolizing aphrodisiacs); Love Lyrics ritual; day 9+ *kurgarrû* returns to Emeurur in morning (BM 32656, A.R. George, *Studies W.G. Lambert* no. 2) (pp. 120–121)
- Day 15: Ceremony for Bēlet-ilī (= Lady of the Gods); procession (SBH VIII; BM 32656) (pp. 64, 90)
- Day 15: *Assinnu* places brick in House of Lament (*bīt sipittê*); Love Lyric recited by *assinnu* (BM 32656) (p. 121)
- Day 16: *Kurgarrû* performs ritual at Equlû, then proceeds to Uraš-gate reciting another Love Lyric (BM 32656) (p. 121)
- Day 16: Procession (BM 32656) (p. 90)
- Day 22: Ceremony — Nabû and unknown god (SBH VIII, II, 22) (p. 90)
- Day 25: Temple festival (*isinnu bīti*) at Esabad, the temple of Gula in Babylon (Arsacid document, year 154 A.E. = 94 B.C.); silver purchased for one sheep for the festival (AB 248) (p. 117)

**Uruk:** No data attested for Simānu in the Hellenistic period (p. 88).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 9 (kiln rites at Eanna of Uruk): NOT IN LINSSEN — no Uruk data for month III; "kiln rites" not mentioned.
- Day 15 (festival for Lady of the Gods): CONFIRMED — Bēlet-ilī = "Lady of the Gods," ceremony on Day 15 in Babylon (SBH VIII).
- Day 25 (procession of Bēlet-Babili in Nineveh): WRONG_RITUAL / WRONG_PLACE — Linssen's Day 25 for Simānu is a **temple festival for Gula at Esabad in Babylon** (Arsacid period), not a "procession of Bēlet-Babili in Nineveh." Nineveh is outside Linssen's scope.

## Month 4 — Du'ūzu

### Attested events

**Uruk and Babylon:** No data attested for Du'ūzu (IV) for either city in the Hellenistic period (Linssen cultic calendar, pp. 88–91). Footnote 478 (p. 92) notes that the first attested day of Du'ūzu in the kettledrum text LKU no. 51 is day 17; no ceremony name given.

The pīt bābi astrological table (p. 38) assigns the zodiac sign Cancer to Du'ūzu and the pīt bābi of Šamaš and Šulpae — a generic cultic association, not a specific dated festival.

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 11 (goddesses exchange houses — solstice rite): MISSING from Linssen — no Hellenistic Uruk/Babylon source.
- Day 26 (day of the cry): MISSING from Linssen.
- Day 27 (Dumuzi captured): MISSING from Linssen.
- Day 28 (Day of Tammuz): MISSING from Linssen.

**Note:** Linssen does not cover Sumerian lament traditions or Dumuzi/Tammuz cultic calendars — these derive from Sumerian literary sources and month-name traditions, not from the Hellenistic temple ritual texts this book publishes.

## Month 5 — Abu

### Attested events

**Uruk and Babylon:** No data attested for Abu (V) for either city in the Hellenistic period (Linssen cultic calendar, pp. 88–91). The pīt bābi astrological table assigns Leo to Abu with pīt bābi of Enlil (p. 38) — a cultic association only.

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 1 (ab/pum opens — ancestors return): MISSING from Linssen.
- Day 16 (Gilgameš games begin, nine days): MISSING from Linssen.
- Day 28 (Maqlu exorcism): MISSING from Linssen. The Maqlu anti-witchcraft ritual is documented separately (Abusch); Linssen does not discuss it.

**Note:** The month name Abu is glossed as "month of the opening of the gate" (*araḫ pīt bābi*) in older sources (Neo-Assyrian letters, p. 37) — this connects to ancestral cult practices but Linssen provides no Hellenistic attestation of specific dated festivals in Abu.

## Month 6 — Ulūlu

### Attested events

**Uruk (K 3753):**
- Day 3: Clothing ceremonies (*lubuštu*) for Anu and Adad (K 3753) (pp. 66, 89)
- Day 3: Procession of Nusku and Ningal; separate procession of Nusku (K 3753) (pp. 68, 89)
- Day 3: **Marriage ceremony of Ištar** (*ḥadaššūtu* or wedding ceremony, K 3753; "the whole month of Ulūlu deals with Ištar," K 3753, I, 9) (pp. 70–71, 89)

**Uruk pīt bābi:** Day 4 of Ulūlu attested as pīt bābi day (Neo-Assyrian letters *LAS* I, no. 285; *ABL* 496); Day 5 and 6 also mentioned (*LAS* I, no. 285) (p. 37).

**Babylon:** No data attested for Ulūlu in the Hellenistic period (Linssen cultic calendar, p. 90).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 1 (lustration of goddesses in sacred river): NOT IN LINSSEN — Day 1 of Ulūlu has no attested ceremony in Linssen's Uruk or Babylon data.
- Day 3 (sacred marriage of Anu and Antu at Uruk): WRONG_DEITY — K 3753 confirms a **marriage ceremony on Day 3 of Ulūlu at Uruk**, but the marriage is of **Ištar**, not of Anu and Antu. Clothing ceremonies for Anu and Adad are also on Day 3, but as separate rites.
- Day 17 (Akītu of Ištar of Arbela at Milkiya): NOT IN LINSSEN — Assyrian-specific source; outside Linssen's scope.

## Month 7 — Tašrītu

### Attested events

**Uruk (Anu cult — the second New Year festival, 11 days):**
- Days 1–11: New Year festival of Anu (TU 39–40) (pp. 72–77, 89)
- Day 1: Marriage ceremony (*parṣi ša ḥa-šá-du*) in Eḫilikuga-chapel (bed-chamber), temple of Nanāja (TU 39, obv. 4–5) (pp. 70, 89)
- Days 1 and 6: Various gods robed (*nalbušu*) (TU 39, obv. 2–3) (p. 74)
- Day 7: Clothing ceremonies (*lubuštu*) for Anu, Antu, and Ištar; preparations for procession including clearing the route; pīt bābi at day's end (TU 39, obv. 7–15) (pp. 66, 74, 89)
- Day 8: Anu and all gods gather in Grand Courtyard of Rēš-temple; Anu proceeds to akītu-temple (*Bīt ikribi*) via seven stages; Papsukkal and king lead procession; people and king sprinkled with water (TU 39, obv. 16ff.; BRM 4, 7) (pp. 74–75, 89)
- Days 8–9: Procession of Anu (BRM 4, 7; TU 39–40) (p. 89)
- Day 9: Anu at Grand Gate (*KÁ.MAḪ*), sits on Dais of Destinies; offerings and libations; same rites as Day 8; then Anu returns to his cella (TU 39, rev. 1ff.; TU 40, obv. 1ff.) (pp. 74–75)
- Day 10: Lamentations recited; gate not closed; vigil all night (TU 39, rev. 10ff.) (p. 75)
- Day 11: Lamentations recited; dawn: gate opened; gods gather around Anu in Grand Courtyard; Lugalbanda and Ninsun arrive; main meal of the morning served (TU 39, rev. 15ff.) (p. 75)
- Day 17: Ceremony for Papsukkal (OECT 9, 40; 42; 44; 45 // 46; 60) (pp. 62, 89)

**Babylon (Bēl cult):**
- Day 7(?): New Year festival of Bēl; clothing ceremony for Bēl; *alû*-drum arranged for procession to akītu-temple(?) (BM 32206+) (pp. 78, 90)
- Day 8: New Year festival of Bēl; procession of Ninurta and Bēl (Enlil?) to akītu-temple (BM 32206+) (pp. 78, 90)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 3 (preparation of Bēl for festival): NOT IN LINSSEN — Day 3 is not specifically attested in either the Uruk or Babylon Tašrītu data. Linssen notes that the first seven days of Tašrītu (in the Uruk version) are described in abbreviated form, with Day 7 being the main preparation day for the procession (TU 39, obv. 7–15).
- Day 8 (second Akītu — Enūma Eliš recited + procession to Bīt Akitu): PARTIALLY CONFIRMED for procession (Day 8 procession confirmed for both Uruk and Babylon). "Enūma Eliš recited in Tašrītu" is NOT attested in Linssen. The Enūma Eliš is explicitly associated with **Nisannu Day 4** (RAcc.); a footnote (fn. 424) tentatively suggests it may have been recited on the 4th of every month in Babylon, but this is speculative.
- Day 27 (Festival of the Sacred Mound at Nippur): NOT IN LINSSEN — Nippur is outside the scope of this book.

## Month 8 — Araḫsamnu

### Attested events

**Uruk:**
- Day 5: Procession of Mīšaru (god of justice) from Eḫenunna, temple of Adad (TU 39, rev. 26; K 3753) (pp. 68, 89)
- Day 18: Procession of an unnamed god from the presence of Anu to Šamaš (K 3753, damaged) (pp. 68, 89)
- [Colophon of TU 39–40 (rev. 26) reads: "The eighth month Araḫsamna, day 5: Mīšaru will rise from Eḫenunna, the temple of Adad and (will come here)" — indicating TU 39–40 is part of a series covering the whole year] (p. 75)

**Babylon:**
- Day 18: Ceremony for Šamaš with offerings in various temples; procession and brazier festival (*kinūnu*) of Šamaš in Babylon and neighbouring cities (BRM 4, 25; SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (pp. 64, 90)
- Day 18: Procession of "the Sons of Bēl" to Enamḫe, the temple of Adad in Babylon (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25; obv. 6) (pp. 69, 90)
- Day 25: Ceremony for Zababa of Kiš and for the city Isin and the temple Esabad; *abgalgungunnu*-drum and kettledrum (*lilissu*) used (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (pp. 64, 90)

**Monthly Araḫsamna (both cities):**
- Special lamentation recitations (*taqribtu*-lamentations) for Araḫsamna Days 14 and 18 are attested in the Uruk calendar text TU 48 (rev. 7, 9); these are yearly special recitations added to the monthly cycle (pp. 32–33)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 1 (gipir Ištarāte — goddess lustration): NOT IN LINSSEN — no Day 1 ceremony for Araḫsamnu in either Uruk or Babylon data. The term *gipir* (= *gipāru*, sacred enclosure) appears in a Nabû garden ceremony (Day 6 of Ajjaru) but not in this context.
- Day 18 (Dispute of the Hoe and Plow recited): WRONG_RITUAL — Day 18 in Babylon has a **ceremony for Šamaš with procession and brazier festival**, plus the "Sons of Bēl" procession. "Dispute of the Hoe and Plow" is not mentioned in Linssen for Araḫsamnu.

## Month 9 — Kislīmu

### Attested events

**Uruk — Brazier festival (LKU no. 51):**
- Days 1, 2, 6, 7, 14, 15: **Festival of the Brazier (*kinūnu*) at Eanna of Uruk** — ceremonies for the gods of Eanna on these six days; brazier (*kinūnu* = KI.NE) used; offerings of loaves, cakes, oxen and sheep; ceremonies take place during night vigils (LKU no. 51) (pp. 87–88, Table XVI)

**Babylon — Extensive series (BRM 4, 25; SBH VII//VIII; BM 32206+):**
- Day 3: Two clothing ceremonies (*lubuštu*) — one for Bēl's *dumuniglala*-priest, one for Bēltīja and Tašmētu; clothing ceremony for Ištar of Babylon (SBH VIII; BM 32206+) (pp. 64, 67, 91)
- Day 3: Events in Esagila lead to procession on Day 4 — several ceremonies for Bēl and other participants (BM 32206+, I, 4–45) (pp. 68–69)
- Day 4: **Ceremony for Marduk and Zarpanītu**; procession of a slave riding a bull, and Ea's vizier Usmû, from entrance of Esagila cella (*bāb papāḫi*) to the akītu-temple; return to Esagila same day; Enūma Eliš recited for Bēl (by singer, *nāru*); lamentations recited; kettledrums of Bēl and Bēltīja present (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25; SBH VIII; BM 32206+) (pp. 69, 81 fn. 424, 91, 118)
- Day 4: Procession of Ninurta and Bēl (Enlil?) (BM 32206+) (p. 91)
- Day 4: Brazier festival (*kinūnu*) (BM 32206+) (p. 91)
- Days 6/7: Ceremony for Enlils and Adads (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 8: Ceremony for Lugalbanda (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 9: Ceremony for Lugalasal (Bēl-ṣarbati); brazier festival of Lugalasal (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 10: Ceremony for Ea with *ḥalḥallatu*-drum; brazier festival of Madānu and Gula (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 64, 91)
- Day 12: Ceremony for Ištar of Babylon (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 13: Ceremony for Anunītu and Šarrat-Sippar of Babylon (BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 14: Ceremony for Ašratu (BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 15: Ceremony for Anaššat(?) (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 16: Clothing ceremony (SBH VIII) (p. 91)
- Days 16/17: Ceremony for Nabû (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25; SBH VIII) (pp. 64, 91)
- Day 17: Brazier festival of Nabû of Borsippa (*ḥarû*-house of Nabû mentioned, BRM IV, 25, rev. 43) (BRM 4, 25//SBH VII) (pp. 119, 91)
- Day 22: Ceremony for Uraš; brazier festival of Uraš (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Day 25: Ceremony for Lugalmar(a)da (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- Days 28/29: Ceremony for Nergal (SBH VII // BRM 4, 25) (p. 91)
- **Palm festival:** Day 4 — date palm branches (*libbi gišimmari*) brought by boat to tent near Ea's temple; taken back by boatmen; Enūma Eliš recited for Bēl; lamentations recited; tablet BM 78076 (Darius) describes days 2–15 at Gula's temple Esabad (pp. 118, 91)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Days 1, 2, 6, 7, 14, 15 (Festival of the Brazier / Kinunu at Eanna of Uruk): **CONFIRMED** — Linssen's Table XVI (p. 88) explicitly lists these six days for the brazier festival (*kinūnu*) at Eanna, Uruk, from LKU no. 51. The day numbers match exactly.

## Month 10 — Ṭebētu

### Attested events

**Uruk:**
- Days 16–17: **Nocturnal festival in the Rēš-temple of Anu** (*TU 41*) — Scepter and lamp-shoe (*šēnu* = Nusku) descend to Grand Courtyard; Papsukkal, Nusku, Usmû sit in Anu's courtyard; Torch (*gizillû*) procession through temples; fires kindled at gates; brushwood piles (*abru*) kindled from Torch; all-night vigil; *šalām bīti* ("good-functioning") ceremony performed at gates; in morning, gate opened, vigil ended. Possibly month X (Ṭebētu) based on TU 41 context (TU 41) (pp. 59, 68, 89, 122–124)
- Monthly: Day 16 of every month — cleansing of hands ceremony (*tēbibtu qāti*) for Anu and Antu, attested as special yearly ceremony for Day 16 of Ṭebētu (TU 38, rev. 32–34) (p. 40)

**Babylon:**
- Day 10: Ceremony for Palil; brazier festival of Palil of Upī (Opis) (BRM 4, 25//SBH VII) (p. 91)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 3 (goddesses exchange houses — winter solstice rite): NOT IN LINSSEN — no Day 3 data for Ṭebētu in either city.
- Day 16 (Akītu of Ištar at Emagmaš + Nabru festival): WRONG_DEITY / WRONG_RITUAL — TU 41 describes a nocturnal festival around Days 16–17 of (probably) Ṭebētu, but it is **a ceremony for Anu** in the Rēš-temple of Uruk, not an Akītu of Ištar at Emagmaš. The "Nabru festival" is not mentioned in Linssen. The Babylon data for Ṭebētu is the brazier festival of Palil on Day 10.

Note: The content of these ceremonies is unknown due to the damaged state of K 3753's relevant passages.

**Assur/Babylon (Neo-Assyrian, K 2724+):** Days 22–25 of Šabatu at Assur — *dīk bīti*-ceremonies with *taqribtu*-lamentations in the temple of Dagan (for Aššur, Marduk, Ištar); attested for Šabatu only in the Neo-Assyrian text, not in Hellenistic Uruk or Babylon (pp. 28–29).

**Babylon:** No specific Šabāṭu data in Linssen's Hellenistic cultic calendar (p. 91). ### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 7 (hieros gamos: Bēl weds Beltiya + Nabû weds Tašmētu): NOT IN LINSSEN — no Hellenistic Uruk or Babylon source attests a divine marriage on Day 7 of Šabāṭu. The Babylonian calendar has no Šabāṭu data at all in Linssen. Linssen's attested divine marriages are: Day 3-VI (Ištar, Uruk), Day 1-VII (Tašrītu, Uruk), Day 11-I (Marduk/Zarpanītu, Babylon), Day 2-II (Nabû, Babylon).
- Day 16 (lamentations / ershemma begin at Bīt Dagan of Asur, 8 days): NOT IN LINSSEN — this is an Assyrian source; Linssen covers Uruk and Babylon only.

## Month 12 — Addaru

### Attested events

**Uruk:**
- Day 8: Ceremony for Antu (OECT 9, 40; 42; 44; 45 // 46; 60; 68) (pp. 62, 89)
- Day 10: Ceremony for Bēlet-ṣēri (OECT 9, 44; 60) (pp. 62, 89)

**Uruk (monthly):** Pīt bābi and *maḥḥuru*-offerings attested in Addaru from prebend texts (see dīk bīti discussions, pp. 40–42).

**Babylon:** No specific Addaru data in Linssen's Hellenistic cultic calendar (p. 91). An astronomical diary (no. -229, B, rev. 13') attests *maḥḥuru*-offerings (*mah-hu-ru un-daḫ-ḫi-ru*) for "the ritual procedure of the gods" on days 1, 3, 7, 15, 16 of Addaru (p. 40).

**Garratu festival:** The term *garratu* does not appear in Linssen's main chapters (I–IV) or Other Festivals section (II.8). It is absent from Linssen's cultic calendar of Babylon for Addaru.

### Discrepancies vs. app

- Day 6 (grand procession of all gods): NOT IN LINSSEN — no Day 6 data for Addaru in either Uruk or Babylon in Linssen.
- Day 17 (garratu festival day 1): NOT IN LINSSEN — the garratu festival is not mentioned in Linssen's Hellenistic ritual texts. Addaru has no Babylon data in Linssen's cultic calendar. (Garratu derives from Cohen 1993 *Cultic Calendars*, not from Linssen's Uruk/Babylon temple ritual texts.)
- Day 18 (garratu festival day 2): NOT IN LINSSEN — same as above.

**What Linssen does attest for Addaru:**
- Day 8 (Uruk): Ceremony for Antu — this is potentially a valid addition to the app for Addaru.
- Day 10 (Uruk): Ceremony for Bēlet-ṣēri — same note.
