# Thureau-Dangin — Rituels Accadiens (1921): Festival Extract

**Source:** F. Thureau-Dangin, *Rituels Accadiens*, Paris: Ernest Leroux, 1921.
**Archive.org scan:** https://archive.org/details/rituelsaccadiens00thuruoft
**Structure of the book:**
- Part 1 (pp. 1–59): Le Rituel du kalû (lamentation priest; not directly relevant to Akītu calendar)
- Part 2 (pp. 61–125): Le Rituel du temple d'Anu à Uruk
  - Ch. I: Les sacrifices quotidiens du temple d'Anu (p. 74)
  - Ch. II-A: L'akītu du mois de Tišrit (pp. 86–99) — Uruk, for Anu/Antu
  - Ch. II-B: L'akītu du mois de Nisan (pp. 99–111) — Uruk, for Anu/Antu
  - Ch. III: La fête d'Ištar (pp. 111–118) — Abu/Ab month
  - Ch. IV: Cérémonie nocturne dans le temple d'Anu (pp. 118–125)
- Part 3 (pp. 127–148): Le Rituel des fêtes du nouvel an à Babylone — Nisannu Akītu at Babylon, for Marduk/Bēl

**Tablets edited in Part 3 (composite text, pp. 129–148):**
- DT. 15 (= IV R., 40, n°1): six columns; covers days 2–5 of Nisannu
- DT. 114 (= IV R., 40, n°2): fragments of columns I, V, VI
- DT. 109 (= Craig, Rel. Texts): fragments of columns I (face) and I (revers); covers king-humiliation section (Day 5)
- MNB. 1848 (Dhorme): longest fragment; columns II–V; covers Day 5 king-humiliation through the end of the attested text

All tablets are Neo-Babylonian copies of an older (likely Kassite or earlier) original, recovered from Babylon (DT tablets = British Museum).

## I. Nisannu Akītu Ritual (Babylon, Part 3)

### Day-by-day according to the tablet text

**Day 1 — not covered.** The composite text begins on Day 2/3 (DT. 15, col. I = lines 1–40 are lost). Day 1 is not attested in this source.

**Day 2 — DT. 15, cols. I–II (lines 1–79, largely restored).** The beginning of the text is damaged. The reconstruction places some initial ceremonies here, but the specific content of Day 2 is largely hypothetical in T-D's edition.

**Day 3 — DT. 15, col. V, lines 155–189 (p. 131–132).**
The *urigallu* (high priest, *šangû rabi* of the Esagila) rises, washes in water drawn from the Tigris and Euphrates, dresses in linen before Bēl (Marduk), and recites an initial prayer ("Bēl, dans ta fureur, sois apaisé... Que tes villes te soient rendues..."). He then recites a prayer to Bēltia (Zarpanitu). The *kalû*-priests are not yet called; this is a private priestly ceremony.

**Day 4 — DT. 15, col. VI + DT. 114, col. I, lines 190–234 (pp. 133–134 + 215–219).**
- 3.5 *bēru* (hours) before dawn (= c. 3.5 × 2 hours = 7 hours before sunrise, i.e. the middle of the night): the *urigallu* rises and washes in water drawn from the Tigris and Euphrates.
- Dressed in linen, he stands before Bēl and recites the long "u-mu e-ne" prayer ("Mon Seigneur, dans sa fureur..."), a hymn of 50+ lines (pp. 133–134).
- He then recites a prayer before Bēltia.
- **End of Day 4** (after a small meal, lines 280–284): the *urigallu* of the Ekua sanctuary recites the *Enūma Eliš* — the Babylonian creation epic — from beginning to end, while craftsmen cover the tiara of Anu and the throne of Enlil. (Line 284: "...ina ekūti lišlim // Enūma Eliš ana pī šangî Ekua šaqê ana Bēl iqabbî" = "In the darkness, may it prosper // the Enūma Eliš, to the mouth of the *šangû* of the Ekua, the lofty, to Bēl he recites.")

**Day 5 — DT. 109 (face) + MNB. 1848, lines 283–460 (pp. 136–146).**
- 4 *bēru* before dawn: the *urigallu* rises and purifies the Esagila.
- Temple purification: a white ram (*immeru peṣû*) is decapitated; its body and head are thrown into the Euphrates, taking the impurity of the temple away with them. The carcass-thrower (*ašipu*) and the bearer of the ram-head stay outside the city from Day 5 to Day 12 (line 360, p. 141; confirmed by T-D's commentary, p. 147).
- Nabû arrives at Ezida from Borsippa (the day before Day 5; attested by colophon, p. 147).
- **King humiliation** (lines 414–450, pp. 144–145):
  1. The *urigallu* exits the sanctuary (Ekua) and enters the courtyard.
  2. He takes the sceptre (*ḫaṭṭu*), the golden circle (*manšaltu*), and the sword-harp (*harpē*) from the king.
  3. The king kneels before Bēl.
  4. The *urigallu* strikes the king's cheek (*leḫu*). (Line 421: "leḫ šarri imḫaṣ" = "il frappe la joue du roi.")
  5. The king confesses innocence: "Je n'ai pas péché, Seigneur des pays... je n'ai pas méprisé ta divinité..."
  6. The *urigallu* consoles the king.
  7. Omen: If the king sheds tears, Bēl is content; if no tears, Bēl is angry and the enemy will rise.
  8. The king's regalia are returned (line 450). (Line 447–450 in T-D's reconstruction: the sceptre, circle, and sword are returned to the king.)
- **Banquet** (*akalū*) follows: Nabû and Marduk meet at the Upšukkannakku shrine.

**Day 6 — not separately attested in Part 3.** No tablet section assigned to Day 6.

**Day 7 — not separately attested in Part 3.** No tablet section assigned to Day 7.

**Day 8 — commentary only, pp. 146–147 (no surviving tablet text).**
T-D states explicitly: "La partie de ce rituel relative à la procession de l'akītu ne nous est pas parvenue" (p. 146) = "The part of this ritual relating to the Akītu procession has not come down to us."
- Reconstructed from royal inscriptions: Marduk stops at the *parakku*-des-destins (*parak šīmāti*) before leaving the temple.
- The god "prend la main" of the king = he leads the king.
- **Procession departs from Esagila to the Bīt Akītu** (the Akītu house outside the city walls). Seven ritual stages are named (T-D, p. 147): (1) the sanctuary, (2) "entre les toiles" (between the curtains), (3) the seat before a star, (4) the *parak šīmāti*, (5) the street (*sūqu*), (6) the boat, (7) the Bīt Akītu.
- Attestation: "Nbk. n°8, IX, 3" (Nebuchadnezzar inscription) confirms Marduk's presence in the Bīt Akītu at minimum around Day 10.

**Days 9–10 — not separately attested.** Marduk remains at the Bīt Akītu. Day 10: attested in the colophon "Nbk. n°8, IX, 3" (T-D p. 147).

**Day 11 — commentary, p. 148.**
- Marduk **returns to Esagila** (T-D, p. 148).
- Marduk passes through the *parak šīmāti* on Day 11 (attested: "Nbk. n°15, II, 57" = Nebuchadnezzar inscription).
- This is the **last day of the festival** (T-D, p. 148 explicitly states Day 11 = end of the Akītu).
- Day 12: Nabû returns to Borsippa; noted as the day after the festival ends.
- The "fixing of destinies" assembly (*puhur ilāni*, the gods' assembly) is implied by the *parak šīmāti* passage, but T-D does not describe a specific "Assembly of Gods" ceremony by that name. The Upšukkannakku shrine (*Upšukkannakku* ≠ *Upuruppuu*) is where the Day 5 banquet of Nabû and Marduk occurs; it is not presented as the Day 11 venue.

### Discrepancies vs. app Nisannu data

**Nisannu Day 1 (app):**
> "El Akītu del año nuevo da inicio. La estatua de Marduk abandona el Esagila y parte en procesión hacia el Bīt Akitu extramuros de Babilonia."

- **MISSING / UNATTESTED** in Thureau-Dangin. The tablet text begins at Day 2/3; Day 1 is not covered. More critically, the procession from Esagila to the Bīt Akītu occurs on **Day 8** per T-D's commentary (p. 146–147), not Day 1. Day 1 events are unknown from this source.

**Nisannu Day 4 (app):**
> "En la noche del cuarto día, los sacerdotes recitan el Enūma Eliš — el poema de la creación — ante Marduk. El rey es humillado: depone su cetro y confiesa sus pecados ante el dios."

- **Enūma Eliš on Day 4: CONFIRMED.** Lines 280–284 place the recitation at the end of Day 4.
- **King humiliation on Day 4: WRONG_DAY.** The king-humiliation rite (sceptre removal, cheek-slap, confession) occurs on **Day 5** (lines 414–450, pp. 144–145). This is the central finding of Part 3 and is not ambiguous.

**Nisannu Day 8 (app):**
> "La gran procesión de Marduk regresa del Bīt Akitu al Esagila. El rey recupera su cetro y es reinvestido de autoridad real para el año venidero."

Two errors:
- **WRONG_RITUAL.** Day 8 is the **departure** of Marduk from Esagila to the Bīt Akītu (T-D, p. 147), not the return. The return is on Day 11 (T-D, p. 148). The app has the direction of the Day 8 procession reversed.
- **WRONG_DAY for reinvestiture.** The king's sceptre and regalia are returned to him on **Day 5** (line 450, p. 145), immediately after his confession and the tear-omen. There is no reinvestiture scene on Day 8 in this text. Day 8 is the procession to Bīt Akītu — the tablet section for Day 8 is entirely lost and T-D cannot confirm a reinvestiture rite on that day.

**Nisannu Day 11 (app):**
> "El Akītu de once días concluye. Los destinos del año son fijados en la Asamblea de los Dioses en el templo Upuruppuu de Babylon."

- **Day 11 = last day of festival: CONFIRMED** (T-D, p. 148).
- **Marduk returns to Esagila on Day 11: CONFIRMED** (T-D, p. 148, citing Nbk. n°15, II, 57).
- **"Asamblea de los Dioses": UNATTESTED** in Thureau-Dangin. T-D mentions the *parak šīmāti* on Day 11 (the shrine of destinies), but does not describe a "gods' assembly" (*puhru*) ceremony on that day.
- **"Templo Upuruppuu": INVENTED / WRONG.** T-D does not mention any temple named "Upuruppuu" in his commentary on Day 11. The Upšukkannakku shrine is where Nabû and Marduk meet on Day 5 (T-D, p. 144). The name "Upuruppuu" does not appear in this source. The app has apparently corrupted or invented this name.

## II. Tašrītu Akītu (Part 2, Chapter II-A, pp. 86–99)

**Critical note on scope:** Thureau-Dangin does NOT edit a Babylon Tašrītu Akītu text. What he covers in Part 2, Ch. II-A is the **Uruk** Tišrit (= Tašrītu) Akītu, a festival for **Anu and Antu** at Uruk, not for Marduk/Bēl at Babylon. The two festivals share the same basic Akītu structure (procession to a Bīt Akītu, return), but they are distinct rites at different cities for different deities.

**Tablets:** AO. 6459, AO. 6465 (Louvre, Seleucid-era copies from Uruk).

**Context (T-D, pp. 86–88):**
T-D observes that the double New Year — one in spring (Nisan/Nisannu), one in autumn (Tišrit/Tašrītu) — is very ancient, traceable to Sumerian calendars at Ur and Nippur. At Uruk, the Tišrit Akītu mirrors the Nisan Akītu, with the procession on **Day 7** of Tišrit (vs. Nisan Day 7 at Uruk; the Babylon Nisannu Akītu's procession is on Day 8). "Au septième jour, jour où avait lieu la procession solennelle au temple d'akītu" (T-D, p. 86).

### Day-by-day (Uruk Tišrit Akītu, AO. 6459 face, p. 89)

- **Day 1 (l. 1–4):** "araḫ tišrītu ūmu 1kam... iš narkabat dAni kaspi iš narkabat dAni ḫurāsi ū-mu 1-su a-di ūmi 8kam" = "Month of Tišrit, Day 1... the silver chariot of Anu, the golden chariot of Anu, from Day 1 to Day 8." The chariots are prepared for the full duration of the festival.
- **Days 1–5 (l. 1–5):** Temple of Nanā opened ("bīti dNa-na-a up-tar-ra-aš", l. 5); general preparations.
- **Day 6 (l. 6):** "ūmu 6kam dAdad dŠamaš dLugal-már'-da ū dNin-sun illabbasu' ina massarti šīmétan" = Day 6: Adad, Šamaš, Lugal-mārduk, and Nin-sun are dressed in the evening watch.
- **Day 7 (l. 7–14):** Main festival day — procession to the Bīt Akītu. The text is explicit: the house rejoices ("ḫidāti"), the kalû-priests perform, first-quality beer offered; "ki-ma ša ūmi 7kam ša araḫ nisanni šaniš" (l. 13–14) = "like [the ceremony of] Day 7 of the month of Nisan, likewise" — the Day 7 Tišrit ceremony explicitly mirrors the Nisan Day 7 ceremony at Uruk.
- **Day 8 (l. 15+):** "ūmu 8kam..." — something on Day 8, tablet continues but details not fully legible in the scan.
- **Festival duration:** Day 1 to Day 8 (confirmed by l. 3–4: "ū-mu 1-su a-di ūmi 8kam").

### Discrepancies vs. app Tašrītu data

**Tašrītu Day 3 (app):**
> "Los sacerdotes visten a la estatua de Bēl-Marduk con sus ropas de festival..."

- **UNATTESTED** in Thureau-Dangin. The Uruk Tišrit Akītu (AO. 6459) has dressing of statues on **Day 6** (Adad, Šamaš, Lugal-mārduk, Nin-sun), not Day 3. No Babylon Tašrītu tablet is edited in this book; there is no textual basis for this app entry in T-D. Day 3 dressing at Babylon is not attested here.

**Tašrītu Day 8 (app):**
> "Los cantores recitan el Enūma Eliš ante Marduk... La procesión de Bēl parte hacia el Bīt Akitu otoñal..."

Two observations:
- **Enūma Eliš for Tašrītu: UNATTESTED** in T-D. The Uruk Tišrit Akītu has no Enūma Eliš recitation; that text is specifically Marduk's creation-epic, associated with Babylon (Nisannu) only.
- **Procession day: WRONG_DAY** (if we extrapolate from Uruk). The Uruk Tišrit Akītu procession is on **Day 7**, not Day 8. However: the Uruk Tišrit Akītu is not identical to the (unattested) Babylon Tašrītu Akītu; the day number may differ. Use with caution.

**Tašrītu Day 27 (app):**
> "En Nippur, el festival del Montículo Sagrado..."

- **UNATTESTED** in Thureau-Dangin. This source covers no Nippur festivals and no Tašrītu Day 27 ceremonies. The day-27 entry cannot be confirmed or contradicted by T-D.

## III. Other months/rituals attested

### Abu (Month 5) — La fête d'Ištar (Part 2, Ch. III, pp. 111–118)

**Tablet:** AO. 7439.
The text describes the festival of Ištar of Arbèles (*Ištar d'Arbèles*) held at Uruk in the month of **Abu** (month 5). T-D notes this feast is not a New Year Akītu but a separate Ištar festival. The *Annals of Assurbanipal* (Rm. 3, col. IV, l. 15 sqq.) attest that Ištar of Arbèles had her own *akītu* in the month of Ab, around Day 16/17. The Uruk text (AO. 7439) describes day-by-day ceremonies of this Ištar festival but T-D's commentary does not assign precise day numbers from the tablet text itself; the link to Day 16/17 comes from the Assurbanipal annals.

**App relevance:** If the app has an Abu/Abu (month 5) day event for Ištar, check against Cohen 1993; this source provides context but not crisp day numbers from the tablet.

### Nocturnal ceremony (Part 2, Ch. IV, pp. 118–125)

**Tablet:** AO. 6460.
A nocturnal ceremony in the Anu temple at Uruk on the **16th and 17th day** of a month. T-D p. 118 explicitly states: "La tablette AO. 6460 décrit une cérémonie nocturne du 16e au 17e jour d'un mois non précisé dans le titre." The month is not specified in the tablet heading. The ceremony involves a kalû-priest, the moonrise, and star observations.

- Day 16, sunset: ceremonies begin
- Day 17 (p. 124): "17e jour: 40 minutes du jour après le lever du soleil, la porte sera ouverte devant Anu et Antu" — 40 minutes after sunrise the gate is opened before Anu and Antu.

**App relevance:** The 16th-day ceremonies across Babylonian months (incl. Tašrītu day 15 = *sapatu*, full moon) may be informed by this pattern; the 16th/17th at Uruk = post-full-moon ceremonies.

### Uruk Nisan Akītu (Part 2, Ch. II-B, pp. 99–111)

**Tablets:** AO. 6459 (reverse), AO. 6460, AO. 6465, AO. 7439, AO. 6461.
The Uruk Nisan Akītu is for **Anu and Antu** at Uruk, not for Marduk at Babylon.

Key day data (AO. 6461, pp. 110–111):
- **Nisan Day 10:** "araḫ nisannu ūmu 10kam a-na dA[-ni i-na-aš-šu-u]" = "Month Nisan, Day 10, [they will come for] An[u]" — Anu is brought to the Bīt Akītu or the parakku.
- **Nisan Day 11:** "araḫ nisannu ūmu 11kam ki-ma ša dAnum ina muḫḫi parakki dAni ša parak-[šīmāti uššabu]" = "Month Nisan, Day 11, as Anu on the parakku of Anu of the parakku-[of-destinies] is seated" — Anu sits enthroned on the parakku-des-destins; the *kalû* performs.

These are Uruk-Anu ceremonies and are NOT the Babylon-Marduk ceremonies. They are interesting as a structural parallel: both the Uruk and Babylon Akītu culminate with the deity enthroned on the *parak šīmāti*, though at different day numbers (Day 11 at Uruk Nisan vs. Day 11 at Babylon Nisannu — in this case the same, but by coincidence or tradition).

## V. Recommended corrections (within this source's scope)

1. **Nisannu Day 4:** Remove the king-humiliation component from the Day 4 description. Day 4 = Enūma Eliš recitation only.
2. **Nisannu Day 5 (add):** Add a new `dayEvent` for Day 5 = king humiliation: urigallu takes sceptre, slaps king's cheek, king confesses, regalia returned; temple purified with ram decapitation; Nabû arrives from Borsippa. Source: DT. 109 + MNB. 1848, ll. 285–460 (T-D, pp. 136–146).
3. **Nisannu Day 8:** Correct the description: Day 8 = procession DEPARTS from Esagila to Bīt Akītu (not returns). Remove the reinvestiture statement. Source: T-D commentary, p. 147.
4. **Nisannu Day 11:** Remove "Upuruppuu." The venue for Day 11 is the *parak šīmāti* (Shrine of Destinies) within Esagila, where Marduk passes on his return. Source: T-D commentary, p. 148 (Nbk. n°15, II, 57).
5. **Nisannu Day 1:** Either remove (not attested) or mark as "per later sources, not Thureau-Dangin." The Akītu begins on Day 1 per some traditions, but the procession is Day 8 and Days 1–3 rituals are mostly from other sources.
6. **Tašrītu entries:** All three (Days 3, 8, 27) need separate sourcing verification. T-D does not supply a Babylon Tašrītu text. The Day 8 Tašrītu "Enūma Eliš" is particularly suspect — the epic is Marduk's, not a generic Akītu text. Check Cohen 1993 (Cultic Calendars) for Tašrītu day events.
