# Cohen — Cultic Calendars: Babylonian Festival Extract

Source: Mark E. Cohen, *The Cultic Calendars of the Ancient Near East*, CDL Press, Bethesda, Maryland, 1993. ISBN 1-883053-00-5.

Relevant chapter: "The Standard Mesopotamian Calendar" (pp. 297–342), with supplementary data from "Festival Themes: The Akītu Festival" (pp. 400–453) and "Festivals for the Men and Gods of the Netherworld" (pp. 454–481).

Note: Cohen covers the Standard Mesopotamian (= Babylonian) calendar as used from ca. 1750 B.C. (Samsuiluna's reform) onward. His festival attestations span Old Babylonian, Middle Babylonian, Neo-Assyrian, and Neo-Babylonian periods; many day-level citations come from Assyrian letters to kings (AOAT 5/1–2 = Parpola), Van Driel's *The Cult of Aššur*, and Uruk ritual tablets. Where he says a festival is "in the month" without specifying a day, no day has been entered below.

---

## Month 1 — Nisannu

### Attested events

- (All month): The *akītu*-festival of Nisannu, one of two annual *akītu*-festivals (the other in month 7), was celebrated at Babylon, Uruk, and Assyria during the first eleven days. It involved the chief deity's grand procession to the *bīt akīti* (akītu-house) outside the city, his ritual return, and the symbolic establishment of his kingship. Astrolabe B: "The month *Nisannu*, Pegasus, the dais of An, the king is installed and invested (with authority); a good [offering] for Anu and Enlil; the month of Nanna-Suen, the first month belonged to the first of the gods." (p. 305)
- Day 2 (Aššur): Aššur left his temple on the 2nd of Nisannu "having possibly arrived at the akītu-house on the 2nd or perhaps on the 3rd" (p. 307–308).
- Day 4 (Aššur/Assyria): "A special rite occurred on the night of the 4th of Nisannu, involving the deities Ninlil and Šerua. During the rite the king entered the temple of Aššur, then the temple of Ninlil, and finally returned to his palace." (p. 308). Special ablutions (*rimkāni*) for Sin on the 4th; for Adad and Aššur on unknown dates. A kettledrum performance (*lilissātu*) before the gods on the 8th (p. 308).
- Day 6–12 (Aššur): Gifts distributed in the month of Nisannu from the 6th to the 12th day — possibly related to the *akītu*-festival (p. 308).
- Day 8 (Aššur): Kettledrum performance before the gods (p. 308). At Uruk, the akītu festival: "the preserved ritual instructions for the *akītu*-festival of the seventh month at Uruk describe activity as occurring in the Rēš-temple of Anu and Antu (with the possible exception of a sacred marriage the first day in the Eanna(?))" — the parallel Nisannu festival at Uruk is presumed similar (p. 329).
- (All month, Babylon): "The *akītu*-festival, the major religious observance of the year, occurred during the first eleven days of the month in Babylon, Uruk, and in Assyria." (p. 306)
- (First millennium, Nippur): "The Nippur Compendium" lists in Nisan: multiple *akītu*-festivals for Marduk (supreme divinity), Ninurta (for his father Enlil), Ištar Queen of Nippur (for Ninurta or Ninurta), Sin (for Ninurta, the net of the gods), and as many *akītu*s as there are, all for Ninurta. (p. 308)
- Day 15 (Uruk, Old Babylonian): "A ritual for the New Year festival is described in the Cuthean Legend of Naram-Sin" — when a sage performed seven extispicies and offered at New Year (p. 306).
- (Babylon, Neo-Babylonian): "The schedule of events at Babylon for the *akītu*-festival in *Nisannu* can partially be reconstructed based upon ritual texts, commentaries and historical inscriptions" — large gaps remain (p. 308).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 1 (Akītu begins)**: PARTIALLY_ATTESTED. Cohen confirms the festival runs 11 days in month 1 but does not explicitly give "Day 1" as the opening date in his Standard Mesopotamian Calendar section. The festival clearly starts at the month's opening. No major problem, but the precise "Day 1" label is an inference, not directly cited by Cohen for Babylon.
- **Day 4 (Enūma Eliš + king humiliated)**: UNCERTAIN. Cohen does not mention Enūma Eliš recitation on Day 4 of Nisannu in his Standard Mesopotamian Calendar chapter. The Enūma Eliš reference for Day 8 of Tašrītu (month 7) is explicit (p. 328). For Nisannu, Cohen mentions a "special rite on night of 4th" in Aššur (p. 308) but does not say Enūma Eliš. The "king humiliated before Marduk" episode belongs to the Akītu Festival chapter (pp. 400+), which Cohen gives fully — it is historically attested for Babylon (Nabonidus Chronicle etc.) but Cohen does not assign it specifically to Day 4 in his Standard Mesopotamian Calendar chapter. Requires cross-check with pp. 437–453.
- **Day 8 (grand procession + king reinvested)**: PARTIALLY_ATTESTED. Cohen confirms the Day 8 theme (king wears crown, Enūma Eliš recited) explicitly for the Tašrītu akītu (p. 329) and says "the Nisannu ritual account of the akītu-festival describes the ceremonies at the *akītu*-house. But no similar instructions occur for this festival" (Nisannu at Uruk, p. 329). The Day 8 procession is inferred from the Tašrītu parallel and from historical records; Cohen does not explicitly cite Day 8 for Nisannu in this chapter.
- **Day 11 (Akītu ends, destinies fixed)**: PARTIALLY_ATTESTED. Cohen says the festival lasted 11 days (p. 307); "During the last four days of the festival, at the Rēš-temple, Anu performed all the functions expected of a ruler who has just entered his new domain...determined the destiny of his subjects" — this applies to the Tašrītu Uruk ritual (p. 329). The same structure is implied for Nisannu. Day 11 as the close is not explicitly numbered for Babylon Nisannu in Cohen's pp. 305–308.

## Month 2 — Aiaru

### Attested events

- (Month name): Named for *Ajaru*, "Donkey Festival" (hijaru), derived from Semitic roots; identified by Durand with the *hāru/ḥa'aru* ("donkey") observance widespread in northern Mesopotamia and the Levant (pp. 309–310).
- Astrolabe B: "The month *Ajaru*, the Pleiades; the oxen are yoked; the open land is cultivated; the great *ensi* of Enlil, the valiant, the month of heroic Ningirsu, the great *ensi* of Enlil." References the *gusisu*-festival of Ninurta during this month at Nippur. (p. 310)
- Day 2: Nabû (from Borsippa/Babylon) proceeded to the Ēhursaba/Ezida; his statue was displayed in the temple gardens. "On the second day he goes out to the garden and shows himself." (p. 311, from a Seleucid Babylon ritual text)
- Day 3 (Calah): Marriage of Nabû and Tašmētum celebrated — "the city of Calah will prepare the bed for the god Nabû on the third of the month *Ajaru*" (letter Nabû-nadin-šumi to Esarhaddon, p. 312).
- Day 4 (Calah): Nabû enters the bedchamber (p. 312). "[The return of] Nabû takes place. On the fourth day Nabû enters the bedroom."
- Day 5 (Calah/Assyria): "the fifth one serves them a royal meal; the *ḥazannu*-official (in charge of the temple) will be present" (p. 312).
- Day 6 (Uruk, Seleucid): "On the sixth day he proceeds to the Emeteurur and [is cleansed/rejoices] at the Eanna. He goes out to the garden and when he enters the Garden of Anu he takes his seat. Because he has assumed Anu's kingship ... he wears Anu's crown (and?) the ... of the date palm." (p. 311)
- Day 7 (Uruk, Seleucid): Nabû's kingship of Anu assumed at the Eanna. Meanwhile the statue of his bride, Nanâ, made her appearance in Babylon on the 17th. (p. 311)
- Day 10 (Assyria): Special ablutions (*rimkāni*) for Sin; for Šamaš on the 10th (p. 312).
- Day 11 (Babylon): Procession of Bēl, "the king arrived on the 11th day of the month *Ajaru*. He *slaughtered* but did not ... the lambs for the procession of Bēl. The sacrifices and table (prepared for) the god which they had [rec]eived up to the day of the Akitu festival were offered for four days in the temples of Babylon (as in normal times)" (Simbar-šīḪU chronicle, p. 313).
- Day 13 (Assyria): "*emaš* of Ištar" observance at the akītu-house in Aššur. "On the 13th of *Ajaru* the *emaš* of Ištar within the town in the akītu-house they make; Aššur, Ninlil, Ninurta and Adad go to the akītu-feast." (p. 312–313; Sennacherib ritual tablet BM 121206)
- Day 17 (Babylon): Nanâ/Tašmētu statue appears in Babylon; the "statue of his bride, Nanâ, made her appearance in Babylon on the 17th" (p. 311).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 2 (wedding of Nabû + Nanâ)**: CONFIRMED in principle. Cohen p. 311 confirms Nabû proceeds to the garden on day 2 and shows himself; the wedding/sacred-marriage context is confirmed from Seleucid ritual text. OK.
- **Day 6 (public exposition of Nabû)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 311 explicitly states day 6 activities at Eanna. OK.
- **Day 7 (Nabû crowned king of gods at Uruk)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 311 confirms Nabû assumed Anu's kingship at Eanna on day 7. OK.
- **Day 13 (e-ma of Ištar in Assyria)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen pp. 312–313 explicitly cite day 13. OK.

## Month 3 — Simānu

### Attested events

- (Month name): Astrolabe B: "The month *Simānu*, Jawbone-of-an-Ox or the Crown-of-Girra; the king uses the brick mold; all lands build their houses; the month of the parching(?) of the land." (p. 314)
- In Assyria: known as the month of the moon-god Sin — "May the month *Simānu*, (the month) of Sin, foremost son of Enlil, absolve!" (Neo-Assyrian inscription, p. 315).
- Day 1: "Special observances at the Eanna in Uruk on the 1st, 9th, and 15th of the month" (p. 314). On the 1st: unclear ritual activities.
- Day 9 (Uruk): Kiln rites at the Eanna — "The festival on the 9th of the month at the Eanna was a religious rite marking the onset of brick making. The goddess Aškajaitu(?) left her chapel and was then served a meal. A rite was performed at the kiln where bricks were baked, eight sheep were sacrificed at the entrances to the Eanna, and then another rite occurred at the kiln." (p. 315)
- Day 15 (Babylon): "At Babylon on the 15th of the month there was a festival for the 'Mistress of the Gods'" (*bēlet ilāni*). (p. 315; cited from SBH VIII = McEwan *Priest and Temple*, 179)
- Day 22 (Babylon): "on the 22nd a ceremony involving Nabû" (p. 315; cited from Arsacid Temple records, Iraq 43 [1981], 141).
- Day 25 (Nineveh): "An Aššurbanipal inscription...mentions a procession of Bēlet-Babili, the Lady-of-Babylon, on the 25th of the month: 'In the month *Simānu*, the month of Sin, the first and foremost son of Enlil, on the 25th day, on which the procession of the Lady-of-Babylon is held.'" (p. 315)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 9 (kiln rites at Eanna of Uruk)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 315 explicitly. OK.
- **Day 15 (festival for Lady of the Gods)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 315 calls her "Mistress of the Gods" (*bēlet ilāni*). OK.
- **Day 25 (procession of Bēlet-Babili in Nineveh)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 315 explicitly. OK.

## Month 4 — Dumuzu

### Attested events

- (Month name): "The fourth month of the Standard Mesopotamian calendar was named for the god Dumuzi." (p. 315). Astrolabe B: "The month Dumuzi, Orion, (the month) of Papsukkal, the exalted vizier of An and Ištar, the month of heaping up of seed, of sprouting of the early sowing, the cry of Ninrurugu, the month the shepherd Dumuzi was bound." (p. 315)
- (Month): A Seleucid Babylonian ritual text: "the month of the binding, [Dumuzi] ... on the dais the shepherd [...]" (p. 315).
- (Month): In early second millennium Mari the fourth month marked the occasion for a cult of Dumuzi which included female mourners. (p. 316)
- Day 11 (Babylon): Rite of daughters of Esagil going between the Ezida and Esagil — "The 11th of *Du'uzi* is when Silluštab and KA.TUN-na, daughters of the Esagil, go to the Ezida and on the 3rd of *Ṭebētu* Gazbaba and Gunisurra, daughters of the Esagil... Why do they do this? In *Tammuzu* the nights [are short]... to lengthen the nights the daughters of the Esagil go to the Ezida — the Ezida is the House-of-the-Night. In *Ṭebētu* the daytime is short (so) the daughters of the Ezida go to the Esagil to lengthen the daytime — the Esagil is the House-of-the-Daytime." (pp. 317–318; Babylonian cultic calendar text)
- Day 15 (Uruk): "A Neo-Babylonian report of temple preparations mentions a procession(?) of gods on the 15th of the month." (p. 318)
- Day 17 (Uruk, Eanna): The kettledrum played for *dPalil*, a form of Ištar — "The 17th of the month at the Eanna involved an apparently simple observance which included the playing of the kettledrum for *dPalil*." (p. 318)
- Day 26 (Nineveh/Aššur/Calah/Arbela): "The 26th is the day of the hue and cry" (Jacobsen quoting the Akkadian passion text). "On the 26th the god [...], on the 27th the god [...], on the 28th the god Tam[uzu ...]" — display of Dumuzi's statue at multiple Assyrian cities. In Nineveh: display begins 26th; in Calah: display 27th–28th; in Arbela: display 27th–29th. (pp. 316–317; ABL 1097 = AOAT 5/1, 5; ABL 35 = AOAT 5/2, 8–9)
- Day 27 (Uruk): "On the 27th day Aškajaitu returns to the E[ḥilsu]. A (wooden) figurine of Dumuzi is brought out and is cast down by the main gate. The *kalû*-priest whispers (the prayer) nī-šu-íl-šê into the right ear and into the left ear of the piece of wood which has been cast down." (p. 317; LKU 51 obv. 28–31)
- Day 28 (Uruk/Nineveh): At Uruk: "Day of the Stall" (*u₄ TÚG.si₄*) — the sheepfold or stall was the place where Dumuzi was captured by the *gallā*-demons (p. 316). On the 29th a couch was erected at Nineveh, Aššur, and Calah where there was wailing for Dumuzi (p. 316). The ritual text cited (Livingstone, *Court Poetry* no. 38) says: "[*The 28th day*] Tammuz rises at the request of [...]" (p. 317).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 11 (goddesses exchange houses — solstice rite)**: CONFIRMED but description needs refinement. Cohen (pp. 317–318) confirms this exchange ritual in Babylon on day 11. The app calls it "solstice rite," which matches Cohen's explanation that it corrects the imbalance of days/nights at the summer solstice. The goddesses are daughters of the Esagil (Silluštab and KA.TUN-na), not named as generic "goddesses." OK overall.
- **Day 26 (day of the cry)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 317 confirms. OK.
- **Day 27 (Dumuzi captured)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 317 confirms day 27 at Uruk. OK.
- **Day 28 (Day of Tammuz)**: PARTIALLY_CONFIRMED. At Uruk, day 28 is "Day of the Stall" (p. 316), not "Day of Tammuz" per se. The ritual text also places Tammuz rising on day 28 (p. 317), suggesting a complex day. "Day of Tammuz" is a reasonable label but "Day of the Stall" is Cohen's actual attested name. WRONG_RITUAL name (should be "Day of the Stall" per LKU sources, with Tammuz rising also occurring).

## Month 5 — Abu

### Attested events

- (Month name): "The month name *Ab/pum* derives from the *ab/pum* festival for the dead. The *ab/pum* was a mound placed over a supposed passage to the netherworld through which the dead could return to the land of the living and through which the living could provide for the dead." (p. 319)
- Astrolabe B: "The month *Abu*, ... of Ninurta, braziers are kindled, a torch is raised to the Anunna-gods, Girra comes down from the sky and rivals the sun, the month of Gilgameš, for nine days men contest in wrestling and athletics in their city quarters." (p. 319)
- (All month): Month of benign and malevolent ghosts returning; the rites called *Maqlû* ("burning") were practiced during the month (Neo-Assyrian letter to king, p. 320).
- (All month): Gilgameš was one of the overlords of the land of the living for the festival for the dead. Athletic contests held during the month; the Nippur Compendium entry for the fifth month: "Abu — Warfare, contest of strength and vigour." (p. 319–320)
- (Not specific to Day 1): *ab/pum* festival observed throughout. Cohen does not assign day 1 as the specific opening of the *ab/pum* passage. (pp. 319–321)
- Day 28: "closing instructions were basically identical to a *Maqlû* ritual, except for adding an instruction to perform the ritual on the 28th of the month *Abu*: '...Thereafter, you recite the incantation 'Evil demon, to your steppe' all the way to the outer entrance; then you encircle the entrance with [flour-p]aste. This rite is to be perfor[med] on the 28th day of Abu, and [he] will [then] recover.'" (p. 320; ABL 56 = AOAT 5/1, 155; Neo-Assyrian letter)
- The *Maqlû* series was recited "at the end of the month, at the time of the disappearance of the moon, an ominous event" — Abusch notes it was performed in Abu (p. 320).
- Also, a W.G. Lambert incantation referred to the 26th of Abu with "they changed the 26th of *Abu*, the end of the Annunaki's guilt [*šer-tú*]" (p. 321).

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 1 (ab/pum opens — ancestors return)**: UNCERTAIN. Cohen does not specifically assign day 1 as the day the *ab/pum* opens. The *ab/pum* is described as a month-long observance; the specific "day 1" start is not directly attested in Cohen's pp. 319–321.
- **Day 16 (Gilgameš games begin, nine days)**: UNCERTAIN. Astrolabe B says "nine days" of athletic contests in Gilgameš's honor (p. 319), but Cohen does not give a start date of day 16. No specific day is assigned by Cohen for the beginning of the nine-day contest period.
- **Day 28 (Maqlû exorcism)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 320 explicitly states "the 28th of Abu." OK.

## Month 6 — Ulūlu

### Attested events

- (Month name): The month was called *Ulūlu* in some areas and *Elūlu* in others. Astrolabe B: "The month *Elūlu*, the work of the Elamite Ištar, the goddesses are purified in the sacred river." (p. 321–322)
- (All month): "the month apparently involved a series of lustrations of the goddesses throughout the cities." The name may derive from *ullulu*, "to purify," or "to consecrate (a deity)." (p. 322)
- Day 2 (Uruk): "At the Eanna in Uruk a special observance, including offerings for the mayor of the city, was held on the 2nd day of the month." (p. 323; LKU 51 obv. 33–35)
- Day 3 (Aššur/Assyria): Nabû left his abode on the third of the month; Bēl and Nabû remained away through at least the sixth of the month (p. 322; ABL 496, 338). "On the 3rd of *Elūlu* there is a dressing of Bēl; on the 4th the opening of the great gate." (Neo-Assyrian letter, ABL 496:10, p. 322)
- Day 3 (Uruk): "A special festival on the 3rd of the month in Uruk is detailed in a text from Aššurbanipal's library. The statues of Anu and Adad were specially dressed for the occasion. The statues of Nusku and Ninimma marched out of their temples in procession...Thereafter there were purification rites for the statues of Anu and Ištar, followed by a sacred marriage ceremony." (pp. 323–324; Weidner *Gestirn-Darstellungen* pl. 11, K.3753 i 7–22)
- Day 3–6 (Aššur): The gate was opened for Bēl and Nabû; "sacrifices were offered...On the night of the 6th of *Ulūlu* Ammu-sālim entered Babylon...on the 4th, 5th and 6th the gate was opened for Bēl and Nabû." (Neo-Assyrian letter, ABL 338, p. 322)
- Day 15 (Assyria): "Aššurnasirpal II (883–859 B.C.) constructed a temple for Ninurta in Calah, saying 'I set his (Ninurta's) festivals in the months *Šabāṭu* and *Ulūlu*.'" (p. 337)
- Day 17 (Assyria): Aššurbanipal constructed an *akītu*-house for Ištar of Arbela in Milkiya, and "the festival of the *akītu*-house on the 17th of *Ulūlu*" (p. 323; L. Jakob-Rost and H. Freydunk, VS 20 no. 98; Neo-Assyrian text). Also: "*akītu*-festival of the sixth month: *itigiti kin á-ki-it*" (Neo-Babylonian tablet, p. 323; LKU 51 rev. 26–38).
- (All month, Assyria): "a special festival of Aššur was observed in Assyria during the month, although the day is not specified: 'In *Ulūlu*, the 'work of the goddesses' (*šipir Ištarāte*), the festival (*i-sin-ni*) of exalted Aššur, the month of Sin, the light of heaven and earth.'" (p. 322; Streck Asb., 118 Cyl B v 77–79)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 1 (lustration of goddesses in sacred river)**: UNCERTAIN/WRONG_DAY. Cohen confirms lustrations throughout the month (Astrolabe B) but the specific act of Day 1 is not attested. Day 2 is the first specific day Cohen gives (for Uruk). Cohen does not cite day 1 as the day of lustration. The month-opening lustration may be an inference; it is not directly cited by Cohen.
- **Day 3 (sacred marriage of Anu and Antu at Uruk)**: CONFIRMED but WRONG_DEITY. Cohen pp. 323–324 confirm a sacred marriage ceremony on Day 3 in Uruk, involving Anu's statue. However, Cohen says "purification rites for the statues of Anu and **Ištar**, followed by a sacred marriage ceremony" — not Anu and Antu. The app says "Anu and Antu." Antu is Anu's consort in the Uruk pantheon (and Ištar/Antu conflation is known), but Cohen's text says Ištar. Flag as possible WRONG_DEITY (Antu vs. Ištar; they overlap in Uruk theology but are technically distinct).
- **Day 17 (Akītu of Ištar of Arbela at Milkiya)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 323 explicitly. OK.

## Month 7 — Tašrītu

### Attested events

- (Month name): "The term *tašrītum* is probably a derivative of Akkadian *šurrû*, 'to begin'...an Old Babylonian tablet lists harvesters for the *ūm tašrītim*, 'the *tašrītu* day (on) the first day of the harvesting of that particular field'." (p. 326)
- (All month): The second annual *akītu*-festival (paralleling month 1) was held in month 7 at Babylon, Uruk, and elsewhere. Astrolabe B passage (pp. 326–327):
  1. The *akītu* festival at the beginning of the month (purification of emblems)
  2. The 7th day: a highly unfavorable day, time of cleansing
  3. A harvest celebration of first fruits around the middle of the month (unattested elsewhere)
  4. A journey of the Anunna-gods (unattested elsewhere)
  5. The Festival of the Sacred Mound on the 27th
- Day 1 (Babylon): "Cultic events during *Tašrītu* were recorded in Assyrian Astrolabe B: (1) the emblems are purified; (2) man and noble cleanse themselves; (3) the first-(fruits?) of the year are sanctified; (4) the Anunna-gods throughout the lands; (5) at the entrance to the *apsû* funerary offerings for Lugaldukuga, Enki, and Ninki, the ancestors of Enlil (are placed)." (p. 326–327)
- Day 3 (Aššur): "Yesterday, on the 3rd, Aššur (and) Ninlil set out safely (and) entered (the *akītu*-temple) intact; all the gods who set out with Aššur took up their residences safely. The king, my lord, can be happy. ... Now, in the month *Tašrītu*, they have filled neither the libation wine nor the vats in front of the god Aššur..." (Neo-Assyrian letter, ABL 42 = AOAT 5/1, 267, p. 328)
- Day 3 (Babylon): "the god Bēl is dressed (for the festival) [on the 3]rd of *Tašrītu*" (ABL 956, p. 328).
- Day 6 (Babylon): "on the 6th day the gate (of the temple) is kept open" — confirmed in multiple Neo-Assyrian letters (p. 328). "A great meal was offered to Bēl following the opening of the gate" (p. 328).
- Day 7 (Babylon): "The 7th day, the bath of [...]" when the great gods were opened (p. 328; unpublished Lambert tablet). Astrolabe B: the 7th day was "a highly unfavorable day...a time when men cleansed themselves." (p. 327)
- Day 8 (Babylon/Uruk): "The 8th day, when the king wears the crown, (is when) he took [kingship] and sat on the royal throne" (Uruk *akītu*-ritual, p. 329). "A couple of lines later tells that sections from *Enūma Eliš* were recited by a chanter (*nāru*) on the same day" (i.e., Day 8, pp. 328; unpublished Lambert tablet). The great *akītu*-procession occurred on the end of day 7 at Uruk. (p. 329)
- Day 9 (Uruk): "On the 9th day, having cu[t off] his head and attained [his desire], he bat[hed] in water and donned the (royal) garb." (Uruk akītu-ritual, p. 329)
- Days 8–11 (Uruk): "From the 8th to the 11th day all activity occurred back at the Rēš-temple...Anu performed all the functions expected of a ruler who has just entered his new domain. He met with his council of gods who would help him rule, determined the destiny of his subjects, and bestowed plenipotentiary power upon the king, his servant." (p. 329)
- Day 27 (Nippur): "The festival of the Sacred Mound on the 27th of the month: this was an ancient festival at Nippur for the ancestors of Enlil at the *dug-kù*." (pp. 326–327; Nippur Compendium)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 3 (preparation of Bēl for festival)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 328 explicitly. OK.
- **Day 8 (second Akītu — Enūma Eliš recited + procession to Bīt Akītu)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 328–329 confirms both the Enūma Eliš recitation on Day 8 and the king's reinvestment. The great procession concluded on end of Day 7/start of Day 8. OK.
- **Day 27 (Festival of the Sacred Mound at Nippur)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 327 explicitly. OK.

## Month 8 — Araḫsamnu

### Attested events

- (Month name): Possibly from Old Persian *Markašan(aš)*, borrowed in the opposite direction from the Standard Babylonian calendar. Astrolabe B: "The month *Araḫsamna*, the releasing of the plow, the hoe and plow hold a dispute in the field, the *akītu*-festival of the seeding (season) is celebrated, the month of Adad, the canal inspector of heaven and earth." (p. 330–331)
- (All month): The *akītu*-festival of seeding season (*á-ki-ti-šu-numun*). "Since the *akītu*-festival involved a procession in which the deity returned back to his home city (after his temporary stay in the *akītu*-house), perhaps the Astrolabe reference to an *akītu* of the seeding season, might refer to the plow's return to its 'home' (i.e., the shed, where it hung until next year)." (p. 331)
- (All month): Recitation of the Sumerian disputation "Dispute of the Hoe and the Plow" (Sumerian: "Song of the Plowing Oxen") as part of the agricultural rites — "Astrolabe B also mentions the recitation of a dialogue between the hoe and the plow as part of the agricultural rites performed during the month." (p. 331). Note: no specific day number given by Cohen.
- Day 5 (Uruk): "In Uruk there was a procession involving Mišarri from the Ehenunna temple to the temple of Adad on the 5th of the month." (p. 332; BM 121206 vii, Van Driel *Aššur*, 90)
- Day 15 (Assyria): Special ablutions (*rimkāni*) for Sin; for Adad on the 16th; for Šamaš on the 17th (p. 332; KAV 79; BM 121206 vii).
- (Month): nabrû festival of Bēlat-šuḫner and Bēlat-terraban celebrated "the following month [after month 7]" = month 8, most probably at Uruk (p. 332; citing TRU 272, PDT I 162).
- (An Assyrian hemerology lists various ceremonies and festivals for certain days of Araḫsamna, but Van Driel suggests the *inbu* series may have combined observances from multiple cities.) (p. 332)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 1 (gipir Ištarāte — goddess lustration)**: NOT ATTESTED in Cohen for Day 1 of Araḫsamnu. Cohen does not use the term "*gipir Ištarāte*" for this month. He mentions nabrû observances (months 8–9), and day 5 procession at Uruk, and day 15 ablutions. No Day 1 goddess lustration cited. Flag as INVENTED or UNVERIFIED in Cohen — requires another source.
- **Day 18 (Dispute of the Hoe and Plow recited)**: WRONG_DAY. Cohen p. 331 confirms the Hoe and Plow disputation was recited during this month (Astrolabe B), but does not give day 18 as the specific date. Cohen cites no day number for this recitation. The day 18 assignment needs another source.

## Month 10 — Ṭebētu

### Attested events

- (Month name): Astrolabe B: "The month *Ṭebētu*, the high festival of Anu, the month of the splendour of Ištar, the city elders hold assembly, Šamaš ... throughout the land, this month is affected(?) up to its end." (p. 335). Also: "*Ṭebētu*, the month of the appearance of the Bow Star, of the feast of the revered Queen (*i-sin-ni šar-ra-ti ka-bit-ti*), the daughter of Enlil." (Aššurbanipal inscription, p. 335)
- Day 3 (Babylon): "The 3rd of *Ṭebētu* in Babylon marked a ritual involving the exchange of goddesses in the Ezida and the Esagil to correct the imbalance between daytime and night caused by the solstices (see our discussion of the month *Tammuzu* above for further discussion). This same ritual is mentioned in the same Babylonian cultic calendar text that mentions the weeping for Enmešarra in *Ṭebētu*." (p. 336; same text as the month 4 ritual)
- Day 6 (Assyria): "An Assyrian record of the distribution of wine for various occasions throughout the year notes the 6th of *Ṭebetu* as a special day." (p. 336; KAV 79)
- Day 10 (Uruk): "The 10th of the month involved special observances at the Eanna temple in Uruk. Aškajaitu left her chapel, the Eḫilsu, was served her midday meal, returned to her chapel, was apparently joined by other goddesses, after which oxen and rams were offered in their presence. Activities then moved to the temple of Nergal (*dU+GUR*)." (p. 335–336; SBH no. viii col. iv 35–47; LKU 51 rev. 19–23)
- Day 16 (Babylon/Assyria): "There was an *akītu*-festival for Ištar on the 16th of the month in which the king participated: 'On the 16th of *Tebet* is her awakening(?) [*e-ra-šâ*], she brightens Emagsmas. The king is clothed in clean garments.' '[To go] to her *Akītu* House...'" (p. 335; Livingstone, *Court Poetry*, 18–19 = ABRT 1 7ff.). "Also: the *nabrū*-festival; '(oil for) four (festivals) in *Ṭebētu*, including the *nabrū*-festival, and four in *Šabātu*, including the vigils.'" (p. 336; BIN 2 129)
- Day 21 (Babylon): "There is a reference to the goddess Ninlil staying in the *akītu*-house on the 21st of the tenth month, *Ṭebetu*." (p. 335; ABL 5, 64 15–16)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 3 (goddesses exchange houses — winter solstice rite)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 336 explicitly links Day 3 of Ṭebētu to this solstice-correcting exchange. OK.
- **Day 16 (Akītu of Ištar at Emagmaš + Nabru festival)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 335 explicitly cites Day 16 for Ištar's *akītu* and pp. 335–336 for the *nabrū* festival in Ṭebētu. OK.

## Month 11 — Šabāṭu

### Attested events

- (Month name): Astrolabe B: "*Šabāṭu*, the yoke-star of Enlil: (1) the emblems are purified; (2) man and noble cleanse themselves; (3) the first-(fruits?) of the year are sanctified; (4) the Anunna-gods throughout the lands; (5) at the entrance to the *apsû* funerary offerings." (p. 337 — note: Cohen assigns this Astrolabe B passage to Tašrītu, month 7; p. 326–327 is where Astrolabe B's Tašrītu text is given. For Šabāṭu, the Nippur Compendium lists: "Month of the feast of Enlil." p. 338)
- (Month): "During this month there may have been a double marriage between Bēl and Bēltiya and between Nabû and Tašmētu." (p. 337; citing Nergal-šarrani to Esarhaddon = ABL 65 = AOAT 5/1, 267–268; Rm.2, 127 rev. [AfO 24 (1973), 102])
- (Month): "In Assyria special ablutions (*rimkāni*) for Adad and Anu during the month are recorded for the 18th of the month. Kettledrum performances (*lilissātu*) before the gods occurred on the 16th, 17th, 20th, 22nd, 23rd and 24th of the month." (p. 337; BM 121206 vii = Van Driel *Aššur*, 90)
- Day 12 (Babylon): "This text also mentions a special rite on the 12th of the month of *Šabātu*: 'On the 12th of *Šabātu* before the throne of Šamaš all the Igigū get up, in their own temples they sat down.'" (p. 338; BM 121206 ix 50'–51')
- Day 16–24 (Aššur): Ritual text from Aššurbanipal's reign (A 125 = Van Driel *Aššur*, 124ff.; BM 121206 vii) covering the major festival from the 16th of Šabātu to the 10th of Addaru. Day 16: "Šerua, Kippat-māti and Tašmētum entered the chapel of Dagan in the temple of Aššur; salt (and water(?)) was strewn by the king." Lamentations and *erešemma* texts recited before Aššur and Dagan throughout. (pp. 338–341, detailed day-by-day reconstruction by Van Driel)
- Day 20 (Aššur): "during the day the god Aššur marched around the 'living quarters' (*ina ganini ilabbūni*). The king slaughtered sheep before Aššur and Ninlil" (p. 338; KAV 79)
- Day 22 (Aššur): "On the 22nd of *Šabātu* Aššur goes to the chapel of Dagan; on the 23rd his *pašru* is bound on two chariots of Aššur." (p. 338; BM 121206 x 40'–41')

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 7 (hieros gamos: Bēl weds Beltiya + Nabû weds Tašmētu)**: UNCERTAIN_DAY. Cohen (p. 337) confirms a sacred marriage between Bēl–Bēltiya and Nabû–Tašmētu during this month, citing the letter Nergal-šarrani to Esarhaddon. However, Cohen does not specifically assign this to Day 7 of Šabātu. The Ajaru section (p. 312) says a similar marriage in *Šabātu* (not day 7). The specific day 7 assignment requires another source; Cohen does not confirm it.
- **Day 16 (lamentations / ershemma begin at Bīt Dagan of Aššur, 8 days)**: CONFIRMED but note: Cohen (pp. 338–341) places the beginning of the major Aššur ritual on Day 16 of Šabātu, with the lamentations and *erešemma* recitations throughout. The festival continues through Addaru Day 10 — much longer than "8 days." The app's "8 days" may refer only to the Šabātu portion (days 16–24 = 9 days in Šabātu). Also, the location is "Bīt Dagan of Aššur" (chapel of Dagan within Aššur's temple complex), confirmed by Cohen. OK for day 16 start; the "8 days" count is approximate.

## Month 12 — Addaru

### Attested events

- (Month name): "According to Assyrian Astrolabe B this was the month of Ea and of Enki's(?) 'happiness': 'The month *Addaru*, in the vast fields of Ningirsu the sickle is not left behind, the month of Enki's(?) happiness, the month of Ea.'" (p. 340)
- (Month): The Nippur Compendium: "Table-spread, games — Goddesses of war." (p. 340)
- Day 2 (Aššur): "the lamentation URUḪULAKE of Ištar and the *erešemma* AN SUDAG IZIGIN were recited before Ninlil, followed by offerings." (p. 341; continuation of the Šabātu ritual; Van Driel *Aššur*)
- Day 2, 7, 8, 9 (Aššur): Kettledrum performances (*lilissāti*) before the gods in Addaru (p. 340; BM 121206 vii = Van Driel *Aššur*, 90).
- Day 3 (Aššur): "the lamentation UMUN ŠERMAL ANKIA and the *erešemma* DILMUN NIGINA were recited before Aššur in the chapel of Dagan (the *bit ḫurše*). The king entered before the statues of Aššur and Ninlil; a censer and torches were set before Aššur and Ninlil; the singers recited hymns." (p. 341)
- Day 6 (Babylon): "In Babylonia a processional journey of the gods on the 6th of the month is mentioned in a Neo-Babylonian letter (Nabû-aḫḫē-šullim to Ibni-Ištar): 'On the 22nd of the month *Šabātu* I was released from duty...Now, on the 6th of the month *Addaru* the images of the gods will leave on their processional journey, and I will leave, to travel with the gods, on the 5th of *Addaru*.'" (p. 342)
- Day 6 (Uruk): "During the first five days of the month at the Eanna temple in Uruk special observances were conducted...On the 2nd day the kettledrum was played and the temple or a ritual object was exorcised. On the 3rd day the statue of The-Lady-of-Uruk was paraded around three times with her attendants, a hand-washing(?) ritual was held, the kettledrum was played, the meal was served, animals were sacrificed, the kettledrum was played again, censers were set out, and the musicians took their seats. On the 5th day the ceremonial meal was served to the accompaniment of kettledrums." (p. 342; LKU 51 rev. 26–38)
- Day 8 (Uruk): "A special ceremony for Antu is referenced in the sale of a prebend dated to the 8th of *Adar*." (p. 342; McEwan, *Priest and Temple*, 171; K.3753 ii)
- Day 17, 18 (Assyria): "*qarrātu* festival — 'In the Neo-Assyrian period there is mention of the *qarrātu* festival on the 17th and 18th days of the twelfth month of *Addaru*'" (p. 341; KAV 79 r. 9, quoted CAD Q 145 sub *qarrātu*). "UD.18.KAM *šá* *iti*ŠE *ina qar-rat*." (KAV 79)

### Discrepancies vs. app

- **Day 17 (garratu festival day 1)**: CONFIRMED. Cohen p. 341 cites the *qarrātu* festival on days 17–18 of Addaru. OK.

## Items requiring follow-up

1. **Nisannu Day 4 (Enūma Eliš + king humiliation)**: Cohen's Akītu Festival chapter (pp. 400–453) likely has the full day-by-day Babylon akītu sequence; the CLAUDE.md says this chapter exists but is beyond what was systematically extracted here. Cross-check needed.
2. **Araḫsamnu Day 1 ("gipir Ištarāte")**: Not attested in Cohen. This term and Day 1 assignment may come from the inbu-series or a later Babylonian source not covered here. Flag for removal or sourcing.
3. **Abu Day 16 (Gilgameš games start)**: Cohen's Astrolabe B gives "nine days" of athletic contests, but no start day. Day 16 may come from a specific text not covered in Cohen's Standard Mesopotamian Calendar chapter.
4. **Šabāṭu Day 7 (sacred marriage)**: The month is correct, but Day 7 needs a specific source. Cohen's Ajaru section references the Nergal-šarrani letter which says "in the month Šabātu" but not Day 7.
5. **Ulūlu Day 3 (deity)**: The app says "Anu and Antu." Cohen says "Anu and Ištar" (pp. 323–324). In Uruk theology Antu is Anu's consort and the distinction matters. The source text should be checked — Antu and Ištar overlap but are not identical.
