## Ludi Florales (closing days: May 1–3)

- **Day**: Apr 28 – May 3 (closing days fall in Maius)
- **Fasti status**: not stated (ludi, variable)
- **Deity**: Flora
- **Ritual**: Not discussed in this chapter; Fowler treats the Floralia in the April chapter (printed pp. before 98). No content for May 1–3 closing days appears in the Maius chapter.
- **Aition**: not given in this range
- **Page**: not covered in pp. 98–129
- **Notes**: Assigned page range begins at the end of the Aprilis chapter. The Floralia's May-closing days are not discussed in the Maius chapter text; consult Fowler's Aprilis section.

## Lares Praestites

- **Day**: 1 (Kalends of May)
- **Fasti status**: F (Fasti Venusini: "Kal. [lares]. (ven.) Mai. F.")
- **Deity**: Lares Praestites
- **Ritual**: An altar and *parva signa* (small images) of the twin Lares had been erected on this day, according to Ovid (*Fasti* 5.129 ff.). The images bore a dog at their feet; Plutarch records they were clothed in dogs' skins. By Ovid's time the *signa* had fallen into ruin — he looked for them but could not find them. The description was taken from Varro. Fowler infers from the dog imagery and the skins of sacrificed victims that the Lares were originally chthonic deities appeased by dog-sacrifice, like Hecate in Greece.
- **Aition**: Not explicitly given.
- **Page**: pp. 101–102
- **Notes**: Fowler is cautious about the connexion with the Lares domestici and Compitales: "There is no trace of any connexion with the dog in the cult of the Lares domestici."

## Bona Dea (temple dedication, Aventine)

- **Day**: 1 (Kalends of May — traditional, but "date is uncertain": Fowler p. 102 note)
- **Fasti status**: not stated in the calendar entry Fowler quotes; he says only that the temple dedication day was the Kalends
- **Deity**: Bona Dea (identified by Macrobius with Maia, Terra, Fauna, Ops, Fatua)
- **Ritual**: A temple on the Aventine, under a great natural rock (*saxum*). Cult celebrated by women only; men excluded. Wine was brought in under the name of milk; the vessel was called *mellarium*. Myrtle was forbidden in the temple. The temple served as a kind of herbarium with healing herbs; snakes were kept there. Victim: a *porca* (sow). The Flamen Volcanalis sacrificed to Maia (= Bona Dea) on the Kalends (*Gell.* 13.23). The goddess's real name was unknown.
- **Aition**: The exclusion of wine is explained by a legend that Faunus beat his daughter Fauna with a myrtle rod because she would not yield to his love or drink wine — but Fowler explicitly treats this as a myth arising *from* the cult practice, not a genuine aition.
- **Page**: pp. 102–106
- **Notes**: Fowler distinguishes this temple-cult sharply from the December rite in a magistrate's house. He regards Bona Dea as a chthonic Earth-mother figure, equivalent to the *Magna Mater* but indigenously Italian. Date uncertain; temple predates 123 BCE (Vestal Licinia added to it then).

## Lemuria (Lemuralia)

- **Day**: 9, 11, 13 (VII, V, III Id. Mai.)
- **Fasti status**: N (marked in major calendars: Venusini, Maffeianus, Tusculanus)
- **Deity**: No deity named; directed at *lemures* (hostile ghosts)
- **Ritual**: A private, domestic rite performed by the *paterfamilias*, not a public ceremony, though the days are marked in the public calendars. At midnight, barefoot and with washed hands, making a sign with fingers to ward off ghosts, the householder walks through the house spitting black beans out of his mouth nine times, saying "with these I redeem me and mine." He then washes, clashes bronze vessels, repeats nine times "manes exite paterni," and only then looks around — the ceremony is over. Ovid (*Fasti* 5.421 ff.) is the sole source for the ritual details. The Vestal Virgins also began preparing the *mola salsa* (sacred salt-cake from the first ears of corn) during the seven days preceding the Ides of May (May 7–14), for use at the Vestalia, September Ides, and Lupercalia.
- **Aition**: Ovid derives *Lemuria* from *Remus*, claiming Remus's violent death as the occasion — but Fowler treats this as a folk-etymology, calling it Ovid's "curious etymology," not a historical account.
- **Page**: pp. 107–111
- **Notes**: Fowler stresses the antiquity and pre-civic character of the Lemuria vs. the Feralia (February): the N days vs. F/FP suggests the dead were viewed as hostile rather than friendly. He floats (but does not adopt) the theory that the Lemuria was once a communal expulsion of demons (Frazer). The three days are nefasti; marriages in May were ill-omened.

## Argei (sacra Argeorum)

- **Day**: 15 (Ides of May)
- **Fasti status**: not stated for this rite specifically (it was *publica* but not *feriae stativae* in the standard sense; it is noted in the Fasti Venusini under May 16 in error, per Fowler p. 111 note)
- **Deity**: Disputed — Ovid and Dionysius attributed it to Saturnus; Verrius Flaccus to Dis Pater; modern scholars suggested Tiberinus; Jordan regarded the question as irrelevant. Fowler returns verdict of *non liquet*.
- **Ritual**: Twenty-seven (or thirty, per Dionysius) rush puppets (*simulacra*) resembling men bound hand and foot were carried in procession by the Pontifices and magistrates to the *pons sublicius*, and there cast into the Tiber by the Vestal Virgins. The Flaminica Dialis was present in mourning. The puppets were called *Argei*. The procession likely visited each of the twenty-four *sacella Argeorum* (small chapels in the four Servian regions), collecting or depositing the puppets. A related procession in March visited the same chapels.
- **Aition**: Two competing Roman explanations: (1) the chapels were graves of Greeks who came with Hercules, and the puppets were proxies for them; (2) the common people believed old men (*sexagenarii*) were once thrown from the bridge, and the puppets were substitutes. Fowler rejects both as unhistorical and sides with Mannhardt: a semi-dramatic rite representing the departure of the old season and a rain-spell/purification — not a survival of human sacrifice.
- **Page**: pp. 111–120
- **Notes**: Fowler's extended discussion (9 pages) covers etymology of *Argei* (possibly *arg-* = "white/old"), the mourning of the Flaminica, the roles of Pontifices and Vestals (connected to agricultural fruitfulness), and parallels from Slavic and Germanic folk custom. He calls it, citing Plutarch-Varro, "the greatest of the purifications."

## Feriae Iovi, Mercurio, Maiae

- **Day**: 15 (Ides of May)
- **Fasti status**: not stated separately; calendars (Tusculum, Venusini, Caere) record: *Feriae Iovi. Mercurio. Maiae. Maiae ad circum Maximum. Mercurio.*
- **Deity**: Jupiter (Ides of every month are sacred to him), Mercurius, Maia
- **Ritual**: The Ides were Feriae Iovi as in every month. Mercurius was associated with this Ides because his oldest temple in Rome (*ad circum maximum*) had its dedication day on the Ides of Maius (dedicated 495 BCE). The *mercatores* (organized as a guild, the *Mercuriales*) celebrated the anniversary. Maia was linked to Mercurius through the Greek myth of Hermes' birth from Maia.
- **Aition**: Fowler notes the conjunction of the three deities is partly accidental: Jupiter belongs to every Ides; Mercurius's temple happened to have its *dies natalis* here; Maia was attached because of the Greek mythological connexion.
- **Page**: pp. 120–121
- **Notes**: Fowler sees the Roman Mercurius primarily as a god of trade, introduced under Tarquinian influence via the corn trade with Sicilian Greeks or Etruscans, not as a genuine old Italian deity. He suggests there may have been a pre-Hermes Mercurius at Rome but cannot prove it.

## Agonalia (for Vediovis)

- **Day**: 21 (XII Kal. Iun.)
- **Fasti status**: NP (OCR reads "IP" but context and standard calendrical practice indicate *nefas piaculare*; Fowler's text reads "IP"; calendars: Esquilinus, Caeretan, Venusini, Maffeianus)
- **Deity**: Vediovis (Veiovis)
- **Ritual**: A sacrifice with a goat killed *humano ritu* (meaning unclear; Paulus: "humanum sacrificium quod mortui causa fiebat"). Vediovis had temples *inter duos lucos* (between the arx and Capitolium) and on the Tiber island. He was depicted as young, holding arrows, with a goat at his side — generally identified with Apollo by ancient scholars (*Gell.* 5.12).
- **Aition**: None established. Fowler notes the Agonalia occurred also on Jan 9, Mar 17, and Dec 11 — each associated with a different deity. He rejects Preller's conjecture that Vediovis was a god of criminals seeking asylum (Greek idea, anachronistic).
- **Page**: pp. 121–123
- **Notes**: Vediovis is "wrapped in profound obscurity." Name seems to mean "the opposite of Jupiter" (Ve- = negation, Diovis = Jupiter), or "Jupiter in a sinister sense." Cult attested at Rome and Bovillae (C.I.L. i.807, gens Iulia dedication). Fowler gives up on establishing the god's original nature: "we must here, as occasionally elsewhere, give up the attempt."

## Tubilustrium

- **Day**: 23 (X Kal. Iun.)
- **Fasti status**: NP (OCR "IP"; calendars: Esquilinus, Caeretan, Venusini, Maffeianus)
- **Deity**: (no deity named for the lustration itself; see Feriae Volcano below)
- **Ritual**: Purification (*lustratio*) of the *tubae* (trumpets) used to summon the Comitia calata. Mommsen's view, accepted by Fowler: May 23 is the day the trumpets were lustrated; May 24 was the day the *comitia calata* could make and witness wills. A corresponding pair exists in March (March 23 = Tubilustrium; March 24 = *Q.R.C.F.*). Ovid (*Fasti* 5.725): *"Proxima Volcani lux est: Tubilustria dicunt; / Lustrantur purae, quas facit ille, tubae."*
- **Aition**: not given
- **Page**: pp. 123–124
- **Notes**: Fowler keeps the Tubilustrium and Feriae Volcano as separate entries coinciding on the same day. He rejects Wissowa's argument that the deity was originally Mars (not Vulcanus) on this day, because two calendars (Venusini, Amiterninum) explicitly attest *Feriae Volcano*.

## Feriae Volcano (Volcanalia minor)

- **Day**: 23 (X Kal. Iun.)
- **Fasti status**: NP (same day as Tubilustrium; calendars: Venusini, Amiterninum)
- **Deity**: Volcanus (Vulcan)
- **Ritual**: Feriae of Volcanus coinciding with the Tubilustrium. Fowler notes the Flamen Volcanalis sacrificed to Maia (Bona Dea) on May 1, and Maia was addressed as *Maia Volcani* in invocations (*Gell.* 13.23), so Volcanus had a real presence throughout the month. Ovid's interpretation — that Vulcan as smith made the trumpets and so was honoured at their lustration — is treated by Fowler as a later rationalization through Greek influence (Hephaestus as armour-smith).
- **Aition**: not given; Ovid's version explicitly rejected by Fowler as secondary.
- **Page**: pp. 123–124
- **Notes**: Fowler sees the coincidence of Tubilustrium and Feriae Volcano as accidental, but resists Wissowa's extreme position of removing Volcanus from the day entirely.

## Fortuna Publica Populi Romani Quiritium (temple on Quirinal)

- **Day**: 25 (VIII Kal. Iun.)
- **Fasti status**: C (comitialis; OCR reads "0"; calendars: Caeretan; also Esquilinus and Venusini record related Fortuna temples on same date)
- **Deity**: Fortuna Primigenia (imported from Praeneste); also Fortuna Publica Populi Romani on the Quirinal
- **Ritual**: Dedication day of one of three temples of Fortuna on the Quirinal, the *tres Fortunae*. The specific temple was that of Fortuna Primigenia, vowed after the Second Punic War (204 BCE), dedicated ten years later (194 BCE; *Livy* 34.53).
- **Aition**: not given
- **Page**: pp. 124–125
- **Notes**: Fowler defers extended treatment of Fortuna to June 24 (Fors Fortuna), calling that the older Roman form. This entry is brief. The Quirinal complex had three Fortuna temples; this date was the *dies natalis* of the Primigenia one.

## Ambarvalia

- **Day**: 29 (IV Kal. Iun.) — not in the Julian public calendars; attested in rustic calendars as *Segetes lustrantur*; Fowler gives May 29 as the fixed date, confirmed by an inscription from the Anauni of northern Italy as late as 393 CE.
- **Fasti status**: C (comitialis; Fowler gives "C" for IV Kal. Iun.); but the festival itself was a *sacrum conceptivum*, not a *feria stativa*
- **Deity**: Mars (primary in Cato's formula; R.R. 141); Ceres (prominent in Virgil's parallel description, *Georg.* 1.338 ff.)
- **Ritual**: A procession of victims — bull, sheep, and pig (*suovetaurilia*) — driven three times around the fields by garlanded worshippers carrying olive branches and chanting. At the end of the third circuit the victims were sacrificed and a solemn prayer offered. Cato (*R.R.* 141) preserves the farmer's formula, addressed to *Pater Mars*, asking for protection of household, slaves, crops, vineyards, flocks, and health, with threefold repetition. Fowler identifies the Ambarvalia with the *sacra Fratrum Arvalium* (citing Mommsen, Henzen, Jordan; Paulus p. 5: *"Ambarvales hostiae dicebantur quae pro arvis a duodecim fratribus sacrificantur"*). The procession originally circled the entire Roman territory; as that territory grew, the Fratres Arvales presumably abandoned the actual perambulation.
- **Aition**: not given
- **Page**: pp. 125–128
- **Notes**: Fowler calls this "the only Roman festival which can be said with any truth to be still surviving" — via the Church's *Rogation* processions (Litania maior). He draws parallels to the Iguvine Tables. The identity of Ambarvalia with the Fratres Arvales sacra is accepted by Fowler against Marquardt's doubts. Ceres replaces Mars in Virgil's description, reflecting the later shift of Mars toward a war-god role.

## Summary

Fowler's Maius chapter (printed pp. 98–129) covers eight main festival dates or groups:

| Day | Festival | Fasti Status | Deity |
|-----|----------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | Lares Praestites altar | F | Lares Praestites |
| 1 | Bona Dea temple (Aventine) | uncertain | Bona Dea / Maia |
| 9, 11, 13 | Lemuria | N | Lemures (hostile dead) |
| 15 | Argei ceremony | — (publica, non-stativa) | disputed (Saturn / Dis / Tiberinus) |
| 15 | Feriae Iovi, Mercurio, Maiae | — | Jupiter, Mercurius, Maia |
| 21 | Agonalia | NP | Vediovis |
| 23 | Tubilustrium | NP | — |
| 23 | Feriae Volcano | NP | Volcanus |
| 25 | Fortuna Publica / Primigenia | C | Fortuna Primigenia |
| 29 | Ambarvalia | (sacrum conceptivum) | Mars / Ceres |

**Key interpretive positions:**
- Bona Dea = Earth-mother figure, identical with Maia, Fauna, Ops, Fatua in priestly indigitation; her cult is pre-civic and chthonic.
- Lemuria: private domestic rite of bean-throwing to lay hostile ghosts; marked N (not F) because the dead are threatening, not friendly (contrast Feralia).
- Argei: Fowler sides with Mannhardt — semi-dramatic rain-spell and purification, not a survival of human sacrifice; the Vestals' role ties it to agricultural fruitfulness.
- Ambarvalia: identified with Fratres Arvales sacra; Fowler sees it as the oldest layer of Italian agricultural religion still faintly visible in the Christian Rogation processions.
- Ludi Florales (Apr 28–May 3): **not covered** in this page range (Aprilis chapter, before printed p. 98).
