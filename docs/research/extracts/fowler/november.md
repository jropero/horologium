# November — Fowler (1899)
**PDF pages used**: 263–265 (printed 252–254)

---

## General Introduction

Fowler characterizes November as "the least important from a religious point of view" of all Roman months — the month of ploughing and sowing, not holiday-time. He notes: "in the calendars, with the exception of the ludi plebeii, not a festival appears of any importance." Later Isis-worship gained a hold on the month, "which remained open to intruders long after city-life had taken the place of November agricultural operations."

---

## Ludi Plebeii

- **Days**: Nov. 4–11 (originally only Nov. 13, gradually extended like the ludi Romani). Fowler states: "they lasted from Nov. 4 to Nov. 11" (C.I.L. i² 335). **Note**: the task prompt cites Nov. 4–17; Fowler gives Nov. 4–11.
- **Fasti status**: not stated (ludi are not individually listed in the old pre-Julian calendars)
- **Deity**: Jupiter (the Epulum Iovis on Nov. 13 is the culmination)
- **Ritual**: Circus games in the Circus Flaminius. "As a public festival, date from 220 BC; they took place in the Circus Flaminius, which was built in that year." The games "were probably at first on a single day (Nov. 13), and were gradually extended, like the ludi Romani."
- **Aition**: Established when the Circus Flaminius was built in 220 BC. First mentioned by Livy four years later (i.e., 216 BC context).
- **Page**: p. 252
- **Notes**: Fowler provides no discussion of the ritual content of the games themselves in the November chapter. The Epulum Iovis is discussed only by cross-reference ("already discussed in connexion with the ludi Romani").

## Feronia in campo

- **Day**: 13 (Ides of November)
- **Fasti status**: NP (the PDF renders "IP" — OCR artifact for "NP")
- **Deity**: Feronia
- **Ritual**: "This is the only mention we have of Feronia in Rome. She was a goddess of renown in Latium and central Italy, but never made her mark at Rome, as did others of her kind — Diana, Fortuna, Ceres, Flora." The Roman campus cult is placed in the middle of the Ludi Plebeii, "probably on the day which was the original nucleus of the games." The most famous shrine elsewhere was at Trebula Mutusca — "the centre of a great fair or market held on the feast-days of the goddess." Her attributes are "those of a deity of fertility and plenty." The temple at Terracina was "specially devoted to the manumission of slaves." Connection to libertini: "In the year 217 BC the Roman freedwomen collected a sum of money as a gift to Feronia." No Roman ritual is described beyond the calendar notation.
- **Aition**: Fowler does not give a founding narrative for the Roman cult. He offers two conjectures for the connexion with the Ludi Plebeii: either the cult was established simultaneously with the games in 220 BC, or it predated them in the Campus Martius and was subsequently eclipsed by Jupiter's cult.
- **Page**: pp. 252–254
- **Notes**: Calendar entry is AKV (Arval calendar) — explicitly marked "a later addition to the original." Fowler notes: "In the Arval calendar the entry is opposite the 14th, but from its position may be really meant as an additional note to the Ides. There is no other example of religious rites on a day after Ides" (citing Henzen, An. 240; C.I.L. i² 296). In the Arval text: "Probably these notes belong to the Ides." The conjunction of Feronia with Fortuna Primigenia is "curious, as both were goddesses of Praeneste." In Praenestine legend, Feronia was mother of Erulus, "a daemon with threefold body and soul, who had to be killed three times by Evander" (Serv. Aen. 8.564) — Fowler calls this "Graeco-Etruscan."

## Fortunae Primigeniae in colle

- **Day**: 13 (Ides of November)
- **Fasti status**: NP (same "IP" notation in PDF)
- **Deity**: Fortuna Primigenia
- **Ritual**: Not described. "A later addition to the original" calendar (AKV). The collis location is not specified further; Fowler provides no ritual detail.
- **Aition**: not explicitly given. "The date of the introduction of this cult of Fortuna at Rome is 204 BC" (dedication 194 BC; Livy 29.36, 34.53).
- **Page**: p. 254
- **Notes**: Treated extremely briefly by Fowler, as a coda to the Feronia discussion. The conjunction on the same day as Feronia is described as "curious": both were goddesses of Praeneste, and Feronia in legend was mother of Erulus there.

## Summary

Fowler discusses only five November items, none with substantial ritual detail:

| Festival | Day | Fasti | Deity | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ludi Plebeii | Nov. 4–11 (orig. Nov. 13) | — | Jupiter | From 220 BC; Circus Flaminius |
| Mundus patet | Nov. 8 | — | — | Cross-ref to Oct. 5 only |
| Feronia in campo | Nov. 13 (Ides) | NP | Feronia | AKV addition; only Roman attestation |
| Fortunae Primigeniae in colle | Nov. 13 (Ides) | NP | Fortuna Primigenia | AKV addition; from 204 BC |
| Epulum Iovis | Nov. 13 | — | Jupiter | Cross-ref to ludi Romani only |

**No aition is given for any November festival.** Fowler explicitly characterizes November as the calendrically emptiest month, dominated by agricultural work. The two Ides entries (Feronia, Fortuna Primigenia) are both Arval calendar additions, later than the original calendar layer. The Ludi Plebeii and Epulum Iovis are substantive but discussed without ritual detail in this chapter.
