# Review: Hekatombaion
Sources: Parke (1977), Deubner (1932)
Date: 2026-10-25

---

## Kronia — Day 12

**Parke**: Kronos was originally a god of the grain harvest; the festival was a holiday for
slaves who were allowed to **dine with** their masters (not served by them); originally a
harvest supper in which all who worked the farm participated; by the classical period the
only survival was the slave-dining privilege; Plutarch mentions the "howling and uproar"
that caused masters to take an early exit; the state council (boulē) was "let go" (state
business suspended); Aristophanes treats it as antiquated. The aition Parke gives is
agricultural: the festival originally marked the end of the grain harvest, not the Golden
Age myth. The month was earlier called "Kronion."

**Deubner**: "masters of households dine with their slaves after the completion of the
harvest, with general loud merriment; the month was formerly called Kronion." No Golden
Age narrative. Kronos paired with Rhea.

**App**: "Fiesta de la cosecha donde los esclavos cenaban junto a sus amos en un ambiente
de total libertad." Aition: "Este festival recuerda la mítica Edad de Oro de Crono, cuando
no existían clases sociales ni trabajo. Los amos servían la cena a los esclavos, aunque
Plutarco se quejaba de que los 'aullidos insoportables' de los siervos hacían que los amos
huyeran tras el primer plato."

**Verdict**: UNSOURCED_AITION | MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues**:
1. **"Los amos servían la cena a los esclavos"** — Neither Parke nor Deubner says masters
   *served* dinner to slaves. Both say they *dined together/with* slaves. The serving
   detail comes from the Roman Saturnalia (a cognate festival), not from the Attic Kronia.
   This conflation should be removed or clearly distinguished.
2. **"Edad de Oro de Crono / no existían clases sociales ni trabajo"** — The Golden Age
   framing (Hesiodic) is not invoked by Parke or Deubner in the context of the Kronia.
   Parke's aition is explicitly agricultural: the harvest-end supper. This embellishment
   imports a Saturnalian (Roman) interpretation. It is not wrong that Kronos was associated
   with the Golden Age in ancient literature, but neither source uses it to explain this
   festival; flagged as unsourced gloss on the aition.
3. **State business suspended** (boulē "let go") — attested by Parke via Demosthenes; not
   mentioned in the app. Minor missing detail.

**Fix**:
- Change "Los amos servían la cena a los esclavos" → "Los esclavos cenaban junto a sus
  amos" (masters and slaves dined together).
- Trim the Golden Age gloss from the aition or make it secondary; the primary aition
  should be the harvest-end supper. The Plutarch quote ("aullidos insoportables") is
  correctly sourced and can stay.
- Optionally add: "El consejo del estado (boulé) era disuelto ese día (Demóstenes, 24.26)."

## Synoikia — Day 16

**Parke**: State festival. Annual Synoikia on the **16th**; every second year an extended
form on the **15th and 16th**. Deity: **Athena** (day 16 primary); second day also **Zeus
Phratrios** (god of the tribal brotherhoods). Expenses paid from the Phylobasileīs fund;
they controlled shares of sacrifices. Tribe Geleontes had a special role on both days; deme
Skambonidai sacrificed to Athena (meat sold raw, not shared communally). Cult of **Eirene
(Peace)** added in 374 BC (Cephisodotus' statue of Eirene holding Ploutos). Aition:
commemorates the Synoikismos attributed to Theseus; Thucydides accepts the Thesean
foundation; modern scholars are sceptical.

**Deubner**: "public festival (δημοτελής) commemorating the Synoikismos of Theseus; the
deme Skambonidai sacrificed a full-grown animal on the Akropolis; a sacrifice to Eirene
with an unbloody altar was added after Timotheos' naval peace of 374 BCE."

**App**: Deity "Atenea". Desc: "Conmemora la unificación mítica de Ática por Teseo."
Participants: "Fylobasileis (Reyes de las tribus)". Aition: "Se celebra la unificación de
los pueblos del Ática como una epopeya sagrada. El mito asegura que Teseo viajó pueblo por
pueblo convenciendo a los clanes para fundirse en la gran ciudad mágica de Atenas."

**Verdict**: WRONG_DEITY | UNSOURCED_AITION | MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues**:
1. **Missing Zeus Phratrios** — Both Parke and Deubner confirm the second day (16th, the
   annual day) included sacrifice to Zeus Phratrios. The app lists only "Atenea." The
   correct deity pair is Athena + Zeus Phratrios (with Eirene from 374 BC onward).
2. **Eirene omitted** — Both sources document the cult of Eirene added in 374 BC; it is
   sufficiently important to mention (the cult statue by Cephisodotus is famous).
3. **Biannual vs. annual structure omitted** — The biennial two-day festival (15th+16th)
   alongside the annual one-day form (16th only) is a documented distinction
   (Parke/Thucydides) not reflected in the app. Minor, but the app implies only day 16.
4. **Aition: "Teseo viajó pueblo por pueblo convenciendo a los clanes"** — Neither Parke
   nor Deubner describes Theseus as traveling village to village to persuade clans. Parke
   says only that the Synoikismos was "traditionally created by Theseus" and Thucydides
   accepts this. The wandering-persuader narrative is an embellishment of the myth not
   present in either source; flagged as UNSOURCED_AITION.

**Fix**:
- Deity field: add "Zeus Phratrios" (e.g., "Atenea y Zeus Phratrios [+ Eirene desde 374 a.C.]").
- Add note on Eirene cult (374 BC, Cephisodotus' statue).
- Aition: tone down to "Conmemora el Synoikismos atribuido a Teseo, la unificación de los
  demos del Ática bajo una sola ciudad (Tucídides 2.15)." Removing the invented wandering
  narrative.

## Panathenaia — Day 28

**Parke**: Day 28 Hekatombaion for both annual and Great (quadrennial) Panathenaia. Deity:
Athena — variously Polias, Hygieia, Nike Apteros (three sacrifice points). Pannychis on the
night of 27/28: young men sang hymns, maidens performed choral dances on the Acropolis
through the night. Torch race from the **altar of Eros in the Academy** to the Acropolis
altar (single runners, winner's flame lit the altar). Procession at sunrise with the peplos
(the Ergastinai in the place of honour). Peplos: ship-sail-sized, woven from Pyanepsion to
Hekatombaion, depicting **Athena defeating Enceladus** in the Gigantomachy. Sacrifice: cows
from a public fund; meat distributed at the Kerameikos by deme. Agons: athletic (stadion,
pentathlon, wrestling, boxing, pankration; boys/youths/men), equestrian, musical
(Citharoidoi, aulodoi, etc.), tribal (Pyrrhic dance, **Evandria**, torch-race, regatta);
**Apobatai** race. Founded: games added by Hippocleides in 566/5 BC (Parke's inference);
the festival itself pre-dates this. No "Asterión" appears anywhere.

**Deubner**: Great procession with kanephoroi, thallophoroi (handsome old men with olive
branches), and metoikoi bearing trays and water-jars; double sacrifice (old temple + hekatomb);
Peplos hung from a ship-cart; gymnastic, hippic, and musical agons at the Great Panathenaia;
"instituted by Erichthonios, reorganized 566/5 BCE." Night 27/28: pannychis on the Akropolis
with paian, ololyge by women, torch-race whose winning flame lit the pyre.

**App**: festivalDesc: "Gran procesión para entregar el nuevo Peplos a la diosa, acompañado
de hecatombes de vacas y competiciones atléticas." pannychisDesc: "Vigilia nocturna con
coros de doncellas y danzas. Se encendía el fuego del altar mediante una carrera de
antorchas desde el altar de Eros." agonDesc: mentions Apobatai and Evandria. Aition: "Se
teje un gigantesco vestido (peplo) a modo de vela de barco para Atenea, en el cual se borda
la Batalla de los Dioses y los Gigantes. Esto conmemora la victoria épica de Atenea sobre
el gigante Encélado **(o Asterión)** en los albores del tiempo."

**Verdict**: WRONG_DEITY (partial) | UNSOURCED_AITION (one element)

**Issues**:
1. **"o Asterión"** — Parke names **Enceladus** as the giant depicted on the peplos
   (explicitly: "Athena's exploit in overcoming Enceladus in the battle of the Gods and
   Giants"). Neither Parke nor Deubner mentions Asterión in this context. Asterión is a
   name for a Cretan figure or a river deity — not a Gigantomachy giant. This appears to
   be a fabrication or confusion with another myth. It must be removed.
2. **Deity (minor)** — The app correctly names "Atenea Polias" but the secondary sacrifice
   points (Athena Hygieia, Athena Nike Apteros) noted by Parke are absent. This is a
   minor completeness issue rather than an error.
3. **Annual vs. Great Panathenaia distinction** — The app treats it as a single festival
   without distinguishing the annual from the quadrennial Great Panathenaia. Both sources
   make this distinction. Minor missing detail.
4. **Erichthonios as mythical founder** — Deubner notes it was "instituted by
   Erichthonios." The app does not mention this founding tradition. Minor.
5. **Pannychis and torch race** — These are correctly attested in the app (Parke confirms:
   young men, maiden choirs, torch race from altar of Eros). The pannychisDesc is accurate.
6. **Agons (Apobatai, Evandria)** — Both correctly attested in Parke. No issues.
7. **Peplos as ship-sail** — Correctly attested in Parke. No issues.

**Fix**:
- Remove "o Asterión" entirely. Enceladus alone is the attested figure.
- Optionally note the annual vs. Great (penteteris) distinction: "El Gran Festival
  (penteteris) se celebraba cada cuatro años con agones panhelénicos; el festival anual era
  más modesto."

## Apollon Hekatombaios — Day 7 — POTENTIAL MISSING FESTIVAL

**Parke**: Parke's chapter introduction explicitly states: "The festival of the
*Hecatombaia* had **vanished from importance in Attica before classical times**, leaving
only the month-name as its survival." Parke does not treat it as an active classical
festival.

**Deubner**: Lists "Day 7: Apollon Hekatombaios sacrifice" as an inferred festival,
citing the Mykonos calendar which confirms day 7 as Apollon's day in the equivalent month.
Deubner distinguishes this from the eponymous Hecatombaia by calling it "Apollon
Hekatombaios sacrifice."

**Verdict**: MISSING_FESTIVAL (with important caveats)

**Issue**: Deubner identifies an Apollon Hekatombaios sacrifice on day 7; Parke says the
underlying Hecatombaia had ceased to function. The two scholars are not strictly
contradicting each other — Deubner is reasoning from a non-Attic calendar analogy, while
Parke comments on the main state festival. The monthly day-7 deity assignment (Apollon) in
the app's `getDailyAtticDeity` does generically cover day 7, but there is no specific
entry for "Apollon Hekatombaios."

**Fix**: Given Parke's explicit statement that the festival had "vanished before classical
times," omitting a specific festival entry is defensible. However, a day-7 entry could
acknowledge the month's Apollonian origin: e.g., a low-key entry noting the old sacrificial
connection to Apollon Hekatombaios with a note that it had fallen out of formal state
observance by the classical period.

## Herakleia Marathonic — Early Month (Approximate) — MISSING FESTIVAL

**Parke**: Not covered in the extracted range.

**Deubner**: "penteteris (quadrennial) with agones and an Athenian theoria sent to
Marathon; the deme-assembly discussing it met four days before the end of Skirophorion,
placing the festival in early Hekatombaion."

**Verdict**: MISSING_FESTIVAL (defensible omission)

**Issue**: Deubner places this in early Hekatombaion but cannot pin an exact day. It is a
quadrennial festival, not an annual one. Parke does not discuss it in the Hekatombaion
chapter. The omission is defensible, especially since no fixed day can be assigned, but the
festival is attested by Deubner as belonging to this month.

**Fix**: Could add an approximate or floating entry noting the Herakleia at Marathon (early
Hekatombaion, quadrennial) if the app supports non-annual or approximate-date entries.

## Aphrodisia (Aphrodite Pandemos) — Month Approximate — MISSING FESTIVAL

**Parke**: Not mentioned.

**Deubner**: "pompe; the Astynomoi were charged with purifying the sanctuary with dove's
blood, whitewashing altars, and washing the statues; hetairai and the Tetradist association
also participated." Placed "soon after new year" in Hekatombaion, but no fixed day.

**Verdict**: MISSING_FESTIVAL (defensible omission)

**Issue**: Deubner places this in Hekatombaion but with no fixed day and no Parke
corroboration in this month. The app's omission is defensible for an unanchored
Deubner-only entry.

**Fix**: No change strictly required; if the app gains support for approximate-date entries,
consider adding it with the note that the exact date within Hekatombaion is unknown.
