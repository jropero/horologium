# Review: Pyanepsion
Sources: Parke (1977), Deubner (1932)
Date: 2026-10-25
Note: Thesmophoria (days 11–13) SKIPPED — already reviewed and corrected.

## Proerosia — Day 5

**Parke**: Day 5 Pyanepsion. Deity: Demeter (Apollo also connected — the Delphic oracle commanded the sacrifice; a separate sacrifice to Pythian Apollo was made two days later, on the 7th). Ritual: Hierophant and Sacred Herald announce the feast; first-fruits (diezmos) of barley and wheat tithed from Attic farmers and collected from all Greek cities; later an ox-sacrifice by Epheboi. Aition: a plague struck all Greece; the Delphic oracle commanded the Athenians to sacrifice to Demeter on behalf of all.

**Deubner**: Day 5. Demeter + Kore. Public ox-sacrifices with epheboi; connected to the sacred plowing on the Rarian Plain; alternative name Proarkturia attested.

**App**: Day 5. Deity: Demeter. "Sacrificio preliminar antes de la arada. Se ofrecían los primeros frutos (diezmos) de toda Grecia." Aition: "Una plaga prehistórica destruyó las plantas del mundo, y el Oráculo dictaminó que los campos solo volverían a ser fértiles si Atenas sacrificaba a Deméter en nombre de toda la humanidad."

**Verdict**: MISSING_DETAIL

**Issue**: Day, deity, and aition are all substantially correct. Two gaps: (1) The app omits Apollo's secondary role — Parke notes an explicit Pythian Apollo sacrifice at Eleusis on the 7th, two days after the announcement; (2) The app omits Deubner's secondary deity Kore; (3) Parke's aition concerns crops/grain (not "all plants of the world" broadly), and the oracle commanded a sacrifice, not that crops "would only be fertile if" Athens sacrificed — the condition framing is an app invention. Minor inflation.

**Fix**: Change festivalDesc to note the first-fruits were of grain specifically (barley and wheat), collected from all Attica and Greek cities. Soften the aition to: the Delphic oracle commanded Athens to sacrifice to Demeter on behalf of all Greece in response to a plague; in return, tithes of grain were to be sent from Greece to Athens.

## Pyanepsia + Oschophoria — Day 7

**Parke**: Pyanepsia: Day 7, Apollo's special day. A dish called *panspermia* (all seeds, leguminous vegetables and cereals boiled together) was offered to Apollo and shared. The *Eiresione* was an olive bough decorated with wool, model instruments, pastry figurines, and actual fruits, carried by boys door-to-door singing a begging song; left at the house to exercise its influence for the year. Aition: Theseus's companions boiled mixed provisions on landing from Crete; the Eiresione was linked to Theseus's suppliant boughs used at Delos. Parke: "The origin of the rite is probably to be sought in the season of the year." Oschophoria: **Day 7** per Parke, same day as Pyanepsia. Deity: Athena Skiras (primary), Dionysus (secondary). Main ritual: procession from a Dionysus sanctuary to the shrine of Athena Skiras at Phaleron, led by two Oschoporoi (outstanding in birth and wealth) in female robes carrying grape-clusters (*oschoi*). A choir sang special hymns. Deipnophoroi (women) carried food and shared in the banquet at Phaleron. Peculiar features: herald wore garland on his staff rather than his head; the crowd shouted "Eleleu, Iou, Iou" combining triumph and grief. The two branches of the Salaminians each received equal halves of the raw meat.

**Deubner**: Pyanopsia Day 7, Apollo (+ Artemis, Helios, Horai secondarily). Bean-dish + Eiresione procession by amphithalēs boys. Oschophoria: **Day 8 (Deubner's preferred date; 7th also possible)** — "The 7th is occupied by the Pyanopsia; the 9th begins the Stenia; so the 8th is most probable but unconfirmed." Deity: Dionysus + Athena Skiras. After the procession, epheboi from each phyle raced; winner drank from a pentaploa cup; a komos returned to Athens.

**App**: Day 7. Deity: "Apolo / Dionisio y Atenea Skiras." FestivalDesc: "Se ofrecía un caldo de legumbres (Pyanepsia) y la rama Eiresione. Carrera de jóvenes vestidos de mujer portando ramas de vid (Oschophoria)." Aition: Panspermia because Theseus boiled leftovers; Eiresione invented by Theseus to thank Apollo; herald wears crown on staff; shouts of joy and grief for Theseus's return and Aegeus's death; Deipnophoroi imitated mothers of tribute-youth.

**Verdict**: WRONG_DEITY | MISSING_DETAIL | UNSOURCED_AITION (partial)

**Issues**:

1. **"Carrera" is wrong for the Oschophoria main rite.** The Oschoporoi led a *procession* (pompe) carrying grape-clusters to Phaleron, not a race. The epheboi foot-race was a *secondary* element (attested by Deubner) that took place in addition to the procession, not the central ritual. Calling it "carrera de jóvenes vestidos de mujer" conflates the two. The Oschoporoi were precisely two men, not a general group of youths.

2. **Day discrepancy**: Parke gives Day 7 for the Oschophoria; Deubner prefers Day 8. The app places both on Day 7, which follows Parke. Combining them on Day 7 is a defensible reading of the sources, but the scholarly uncertainty should be noted. Since the app treats them as one entry, the day assignment is acceptable with a note.

3. **Deity order**: App lists "Apolo / Dionisio y Atenea Skiras." Parke (confirmed by the 1938 Agora inscription) establishes Athena Skiras as the *primary* deity of the Oschophoria, with Dionysus secondary. The order as written implies Dionysus has the leading role, which inverts the scholarly consensus.

4. **Aition — Eiresione**: App says "Teseo inventó la rama decorada para agradecer a Apolo haberlos salvado." Parke says Theseus used suppliant boughs (*hiketeria*) at Delos on the voyage, not that he "invented" the branch to thank Apollo. This is a slight invention; Parke is explicit that the branch was a pre-existing suppliant object.

5. **Missing ritual details in festivalDesc**: (a) The Deipnophoroi (women dinner-carriers who share in the banquet at Phaleron); (b) the choir singing the Oschophoric hymns; (c) the peculiar shout "Eleleu, Iou, Iou" at the conclusion; (d) the herald's garland wound on the staff — these last two are mentioned in the aition but not connected to their ritual origin in the festivalDesc.

**Fix**: 
- Change festivalDesc: "Pyanepsia: banquete de panspermia (caldo de todas las semillas) ofrecido a Apolo; los niños portaban la Eiresione (rama de olivo con frutas, lana y panes) de puerta en puerta. Oschophoria: procesión solemne de dos jóvenes nobles vestidos de mujer (Oschophoroi) portando racimos de uva, desde un santuario de Dioniso hasta el templo de Atenea Skiras en Falero; les seguían las Deipnophoroi (mujeres portadoras de alimentos) y un coro; al final, el heraldo llevaba la corona en el bastón y la multitud gritaba 'Eleleu, Iou, Iou'."
- Correct deity to: "Apolo (Pyanepsia) / Atenea Skiras y Dioniso (Oschophoria)" — Athena Skiras listed before Dionysus.
- Correct aition for Eiresione: Theseus used suppliant boughs (*hikétériai*) on the voyage to and from Crete (not "invented" them).

## Theseia — Day 8

**Parke**: Day 8, hero cult of Theseus. The 8th was linked to Poseidon (as Theseus's divine father). Ritual: procession, sacrifice, generous public distribution of meat (including to the poor), athletic and military programme (*athara* porridge of milk and grain was served). Aition: in 475 BCE Cimon was directed by the Delphic oracle to find and restore Theseus's bones; on Scyros he found the relics in a tomb, brought them ceremonially to Athens, and a major sacred area was established near the Agora.

**Deubner**: Day 8. State sacrifice with pompe; libations to Theseus; public feast; torch-race and agons; epheboi in armed parade. Day before (Day 7): ram sacrifice to Konnidas (Theseus's tutor) and sacrifice to the Amazons. Enlarged after Cimon brought bones from Skyros (475 BCE).

**App**: Day 8. Deity: Teseo (hero). "Fiesta del héroe, con banquetes, entrega de comida a los pobres y competiciones atléticas y militares." Aition: "En el 475 a.C., el general Cimón conquistó la isla de Esciros tras ver a un águila picoteando un lugar: allí hallaron el esqueleto de un gigante con espada, que creyeron ser Teseo. Se celebra comiendo 'athara' (papilla de leche) en honor al héroe."

**Verdict**: UNSOURCED_AITION (detail) | MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues**:

1. **Eagle detail is unsourced in either Parke or Deubner.** The "eagle pecking at a spot" is from Plutarch's *Life of Cimon* (8.5–6), not discussed in Parke's or Deubner's treatments here extracted. It is not necessarily wrong, but it comes from a source not represented in the scholarly extracts and should be cited to Plutarch directly, not assumed from Parke.

2. **"Esqueleto de un gigante con espada"**: Parke says "the relics" were found on Scyros; Plutarch describes a skeleton of extraordinary size with a bronze spear and sword beside it. Again, the detail comes from Plutarch (Cim. 8.6), not from Parke's or Deubner's text.

3. **Missing**: Deubner mentions a sacrifice to Konnidas (Theseus's tutor) on the day before (Day 7) and a sacrifice to the Amazons — the app has no entry for Day 7 noting these (though Day 7 is already occupied by Pyanepsia/Oschophoria, so the conflict with a combined entry should be acknowledged).

**Fix**: Add Plutarch citation to the aition for the eagle and the giant skeleton. E.g.: "(según Plutarco, Cimón 8.5–6)." The athara detail is correctly sourced (Parke confirms it). Consider noting the Day 7 Konnidas sacrifice in the Day 8 entry's context, or in a note.

## Stenia — Day 9

**Parke**: Day 9, preliminary to the Thesmophoria. Women's night festival at the Thesmophorion with ritual abusive speech (aischrologia). Not extracted from Parke separately (falls within the Thesmophoria chapter section).

**Deubner**: Day 9. Demeter + Kore. Night festival two days before the Thesmophoria proper; women mocked and ritually abused each other (aischrologia); the Prytanes sacrificed to Demeter and Kore on behalf of the Boule and Demos.

**App**: **No entry for Day 9.**

**Verdict**: MISSING_FESTIVAL

**Issue**: The Stenia is a documented festival confirmed by both sources on Day 9 Pyanepsion. It is distinct from the Thesmophoria (days 11–13, which were already reviewed). The Stenia's exclusion from the app leaves a gap between the Theseia (Day 8) and the Thesmophoria (Day 11).

**Fix**: Add a Day 9 entry for the Stenia:
```
festivalName: "Stenia",
deity: "Deméter y Perséfone",
deityDesc: "Diosas de la fertilidad y el mundo subterráneo.",
festivalDesc: "Festival nocturno femenino preliminar a las Tesmoforias. Las mujeres intercambiaban insultos y burlas rituales (aischrologia). Los Prítanos sacrificaban a Deméter y Perséfone en nombre del Consejo y el Pueblo.",
category: "Agrario y Fertilidad",
participants: "Mujeres ciudadanas",
```

## Apaturia — Days 19–21

**Parke**: **No fixed date.** "Each Phratria held it at its own convenience within Pyanepsion." Three consecutive days: Day 1 (Dorpia — communal dinner), Day 2 (Anarrhysis — sacrifices to Zeus Phratrios and Athena Phratria), Day 3 (Koureotis — registration of infants and adolescent sons; consecration of hair-locks). Day 4 (Epibda) sometimes counted. Dionysus had "a footing" in some phratriai. Aition: Melanthus vs. Xanthus — single combat in which Dionysus (in a black goat-skin) distracted Xanthus, allowing Melanthus to strike. Parke: "As an explanation of the festival this myth is quite absurd" — the Apaturia was celebrated in all Ionian cities, making an Athenian local-victory aition untenable.

**Deubner**: Three consecutive days in Pyanopsion, **day not fixed**. Day 1 (Dorpia): communal dinner. Day 2 (Anarrhysis): sacrifice to Zeus Phratrios and Athena. Day 3 (Kureotis): registration of newborns (meion sacrifice), adolescents entering ephebeia (koureion sacrifice), and new brides (gamelia sacrifice) into phratries; solemn Hephaistos torchlight at end.

**App**:
- Day 19: Dorpia. Zeus Phratrios y Atenea Phratria. Aition: Melanthus/Xanthus story linked to "apate" (deception).
- Day 20: Anarrhysis. Deity: **Dionisio Melanaigis.** Aition: "La Anarrhysis conmemora el sacrificio que hizo Melanto tras su victoria engañosa: ofreció un macho cabrío negro para agradecer a Dioniso."
- Day 21: Koureotis. Zeus Phratrios. Registration of infants and adolescents.

**Verdict**: WRONG_DAY | WRONG_DEITY (Day 20) | UNSOURCED_AITION (Day 20)

**Issues**:

1. **Specific day assignment (Days 19–21) is unsourced.** Both Parke and Deubner are explicit: the Apaturia had no fixed calendar date within Pyanepsion. Different phratriai chose different days. Pinning it to Days 19–21 is a scholarly fabrication.

2. **Day 20 (Anarrhysis) deity is wrong.** The app gives the deity as "Dionisio Melanaigis." Parke specifies that sacrifices on the Anarrhysis were to **Zeus Phratrios and Athena Phratria**. Dionysus had a role in some phratriai's worship of the festival, but he was not the dedicatee of the Anarrhysis sacrifice. Deubner confirms: "sacrifice to Zeus Phratrios and Athena." Making Dionysus Melanaigis the primary deity of the second day misrepresents the sources.

3. **Day 20 aition is unsourced.** App: "Melanto ofreció un macho cabrío negro para agradecer a Dioniso por su aparición fantasmal." Neither Parke nor Deubner attributes this specific act (Melanthus sacrificing a black billy-goat after the battle) to the Anarrhysis day specifically. Parke mentions "Dionysus Melainaigis ('of the Black Goat's Skin')" as the epithet gained, but does not say that Melanthus made a sacrifice specifically on the Anarrhysis. This causal link is an unsourced invention.

4. **Day 21 deity incomplete.** App: "Zeus Phratrios." Parke and Deubner: "Zeus Phratrios **and Athena Phratria**." Minor but consistent omission of Athena.

5. **Deubner adds**: new brides were also registered into the phratry (gamelia sacrifice) on the Koureotis; the app omits this. Deubner also notes a solemn Hephaestus torchlight at the end of the Koureotis.

**Fix**:
- Remove the specific day numbers (19, 20, 21); replace with a note that the Apaturia was held on three consecutive days within Pyanepsion at dates chosen by each phratry.
- In practice, since the app must display something, flag this as a conventional approximation.
- Day 20 (Anarrhysis): Change deity to "Zeus Phratrios y Atenea Phratria." Remove the aition claiming Melanthus specifically sacrificed a black billy-goat to mark this day — not in either source.
- Day 21 (Koureotis): Add Athena Phratria alongside Zeus Phratrios; add new brides (gamelia sacrifice) to participants.

## Chalkeia — Day 30

**Parke**: Last day of Pyanepsion (day 30). Deity: Hephaestus and Athena (both; debate over primacy is described as "scarcely relevant" in a polytheistic culture). Athena's epithets: Ergane (Goddess of Labour) and Archegetis (Founding Goddess). Ritual: procession of craftsmen and metalworkers with baskets of grain as offerings; holiday from the workshops; feast at night on sacrificial meat; on the Acropolis, the priestess of Athena and the Arrephoroi set up the loom to begin weaving the Panathenaic peplos (this allowed just under nine months for its completion). Aition: none discussed by Parke. Name derived from *chalkos* (copper/bronze), implying a Bronze Age origin.

**Deubner**: Last day (eny kai nea = 29th or 30th). Athena Ergane (+ Hephaistos secondary). Sacrifice to Athena Ergane on the Akropolis by the prytaneis. Originally a festival of the whole people, later of craftsmen. Official start of weaving the Panathenaic Peplos, with two Arrephoroi setting up the warp.

**App**: **No entry for Day 30 / Chalkeia.**

**Verdict**: MISSING_FESTIVAL

**Issue**: The Chalkeia is one of the two major closing festivals of Pyanepsion confirmed by both Parke and Deubner, on the last day of the month. Its absence leaves an 8-day gap after the Koureotis (Day 21) and omits the important Panathenaic peplos-loom ritual.

**Fix**: Add a Day 30 entry for the Chalkeia:
```
festivalName: "Chalkeia",
deity: "Atenea Ergane y Hefesto",
deityDesc: "Atenea 'la Artesana', diosa del trabajo manual; Hefesto, dios de los herreros.",
festivalDesc: "Festival de los artesanos y metalúrgicos. Procesión de los trabajadores con cestos de grano; festín nocturno. En la Acrópolis, la sacerdotisa de Atenea y las Arreforias instalaban el telar para comenzar el tejido del peplo panatenaico — la prenda sagrada que se entregaría a la diosa en las Panateneas, nueve meses después.",
category: "Cívico y Patriótico",
participants: "Artesanos y metalúrgicos, Sacerdotisa de Atenea, Arreforias",
ritualOffering: { item: "Cestos de grano ofrecidos por los artesanos; sacrificio oficial costeado por el Consejo", icon: "⚒️" },
aition: "El nombre deriva del bronce (chalkos), evocando un origen en la Edad del Bronce. El comienzo del tejido del peplo se situaba aquí porque nueve meses —el resto del año ritual— era el tiempo mínimo para completar la compleja prenda bordada.",
```
