## Arrephoria — Day 3

**Parke**: The Arretephoria is placed in Skiraphorion (scope note in `skiraphorion.md` confirms: "Arretephoria, day uncertain"). No specific day is given anywhere in Parke's Skiraphorion chapter. The ritual: two Arrephoren carry covered sacred objects down through an underground passage near Aphrodite en Kepois, leave them, receive other objects in return, and are then dismissed. Parke discusses the connection to the daughters of Kekrops as an aetiological myth but treats it cautiously.

**Deubner**: "Skirophorion (no day named): Arretophoria (summer rite) — Athena Polias — the two Arrephoren carried covered objects down via an underground passage near Aphrodite en Kepois, left them, and brought back something else. The month Skirophorion is explicitly named by the Etym. magnum." No day is given. Deubner treats this as the depositing phase; the retrieval is a separate autumn rite in Pyanopsion.

**App (day 3)**: "Dos niñas bajan por un pasadizo secreto de la Acrópolis llevando paquetes misteriosos." Deity: Atenea Polias. Aition: "Las niñas recrean el mito de las hijas de Cécrope: Atenea les dio una caja cerrada, la abrieron por curiosidad, vieron a un bebé serpiente (Erictonio) y, locas de terror, se lanzaron por el acantilado."

**Verdict**: WRONG_DAY | UNSOURCED_AITION

**Issues**:
1. **Day 3 is not attested.** Neither Parke nor Deubner gives a specific day for the Arrephoria. No ancient source fixes it to the 3rd of Skiraphorion. The month is correct; the day is an invention.
2. **The aition conflates two distinct myths.** The myth of the daughters of Kekrops opening the basket (and seeing infant Erichthonius) belongs to a separate narrative (Pausanias 1.18.2; Apollodorus 3.14.6). The Arrephoria girls, by contrast, carry objects they are explicitly forbidden to examine — the ritual's point is that they do NOT look. Conflating "they opened the box" (Pandrosos/Aglauros myth) with the Arrephoria (where the whole mystery is that the objects remain unseen) is a substantive error. The Pausanias passage on the Arrephoria (1.27.3) says only that the girls carry down what the priestess of Athena gives them "without knowing what it is" — nothing about opening a box or throwing themselves off a cliff.

**Fix**:
- Remove the day 3 assignment; the day is unknown. Either omit the day entirely or note "day unknown (Skiraphorion)" if the data model permits.
- Rewrite the aition: "Pausanias (1.27.3) records only that the Arrephoren carried down covered objects without knowing what they contained, then brought back other objects equally unseen. The myth connecting the rite to the daughters of Kekrops is an aetiological gloss found in later sources; the ritual meaning is debated." Do not describe them opening anything or jumping from the Acropolis — that episode belongs to a different myth.

## Dipolieia (Bouphonia) — Day 14

**Parke**: 14th of Skiraphorion. Zeus Polieus. On the Acropolis. Grain laid on the altar; an ox approaches and eats from it; the Bouphonos (Bull-slayer, from the hereditary Thaulonidai family) kills the ox with a pole-axe, throws it down, and flees. Others bring the pole-axe to trial at the Prytaneion; the trial convicts the axe annually (attested in Aeschines). Porphyry's fuller version: the chain of blame passes from participant to participant until the knife is convicted and thrown into the sea; the hide is stuffed with straw and yoked to a plough. Parke treats the Bouphonia as a named ceremony within the Dipolieia, not a separate festival.

**Deubner**: "Day 14: Dipolieia / Buphonia — Zeus Polieus (+ Athena Polias) — barley and wheat on the altar; an ox that ate from the grain was struck by the Thaulonidai clan's βουφόνος, who then fled; the ox-hide was stuffed with straw and set back on its feet; a formal mock-trial shifted blame down a chain until the knife was convicted and thrown into the sea; the ox was eaten."

**App (day 14)**: Deity: Zeus Polieus. Description: "Extraño rito antiguo donde se mataba a un buey de trabajo y luego el hacha ejecutora era llevada a juicio por 'asesinato'." Aition: "El sacerdote huye y se juzga al hacha porque el primer hombre que sacrificó un buey (Sopatro) huyó aterrado por la culpa. Al no poder castigar al hombre, se juzgó al arma."

**Verdict**: OK with minor notes

**Notes**:
- Day 14 and deity Zeus Polieus are both correct per both sources.
- The combined name "Dipolieia (Bouphonia)" correctly reflects that Bouphonia is the named ceremony within the Dipolieia rather than a separate festival.
- The aition references Sopatros (app: "Sopatro") — traceable to Porphyry's account cited by Parke; this is acceptable.
- The description says "hacha" (axe/pole-axe): Parke's Pausanias version has the pole-axe tried; Porphyry's version has the knife convicted and thrown into the sea. The app says "hacha" throughout, which follows the Pausanias version — internally consistent and sourced.
- Missing details (not errors): the trial location at the Prytaneion by name; the stuffing of the ox-hide (Porphyry's version); the role of the Kentriadai who drove the ox to the altar; that Deubner explains the ox as a yoke-ox at harvest-time. These could enrich the entry but their absence is not wrong.

## Diisoteria y Carrera de Pan — Day 30

**Parke**: Two distinct entries in the Skiraphorion chapter:
1. **Diisoteria** (Piraeus): "Towards the end of Skiraphorion" (after the Dipolieia on the 14th). Zeus Soter + Athena Soteira. Big Piraeus festival: procession with a maiden and basket, large bull sacrifices, theoxenia couch/table, ephebe regatta around the cape to Munichia harbour. Parke calls it "the biggest annual occasion in the Peiraeus." Managed by the archon.
2. **Year-end sacrifice** (Athens): Last day of Skiraphorion. Zeus Soter. A modest institutional sacrifice at the Agora (before the Stoa of Zeus, per Deubner), led by the King Archon, with city magistrates and the council. Not a popular festival; purely official. Earliest reference: Lysias 382 BCE.
3. **Pan torch-race**: Parke discusses this in the "Other Festivals" chapter (not Skiraphorion). He tentatively conjectures the date as "the 9th of the lunar month, probably Boedromion" — the anniversary of Pan's epiphany to Philippides at Mount Parthenion. He does not place it in Skiraphorion at all.

**Deubner**: Two distinct entries under Skiraphorion:
1. **Diisoteria** (Piraeus): "Before day 20 (decrees honouring officials dated 20th and 28th)" — Zeus Soter + Athena Soteira. Pompe with epheboi and sacrificial bull; rich animal sacrifices; sacrifice also to Asklepios and Hygieia; ephebe regatta.
2. **Eisiteria** (city festival, day 30): "Zeus Soter + Athena Soteira — a modest state sacrifice by the priest of Zeus Soter on the Agora before the Stoa of Zeus 'for the health of the Boule'." Deubner explicitly says this is "entirely separate from the Piraeus festival above" and "never called 'Diisoteria'."

**App (day 30)**: Deity: "Zeus Soter y Pan." Category: Cívico y Patriótico. Description: "Gran festival de cierre de año con una espectacular carrera de antorchas nocturna para el dios Pan." Aition: "La carrera de antorchas para Pan fue la disculpa oficial de Atenas después de que el dios se quejara a Filípides de que lo ninguneaban. Se hizo para evitar su maldición antes de Maratón."

**Verdict**: WRONG_DAY | WRONG_DEITY | UNSOURCED_AITION (for placement) | MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues**:
1. **Wrong festival identity on day 30.** What belongs on the last day of Skiraphorion is the year-end sacrifice to Zeus Soter (Parke) / Eisiteria (Deubner) — a modest official sacrifice at the Agora by the King Archon and magistrates. This is not the big Piraeus Diisoteria. The app description ("Gran festival de cierre de año con espectacular carrera de antorchas") dramatically overstates what happens on day 30.
2. **Diisoteria is not day 30.** The Piraeus Diisoteria falls before day 20 per Deubner, or "towards the end of Skiraphorion" per Parke — but it is explicitly a Piraeus festival with large bull sacrifices and an ephebe regatta. It is not placed on day 30 by any source. Deubner and Parke treat the two events (Piraeus Diisoteria and Agora year-end sacrifice) as entirely separate.
3. **The Pan torch-race is not in Skiraphorion.** Parke places it tentatively in Boedromion (9th of the month). No source places Pan's annual torch-race on 30 Skiraphorion. The aition given (Pan complained to Philippides before Marathon — Herodotus) is correct, but the placement on this day and month is an invention.
4. **"Zeus Soter y Pan" is a fabricated pairing.** The year-end sacrifice on day 30 is to Zeus Soter alone. Pan has no attested connection to this date or to Zeus Soter.
5. **The description inverts what actually happens.** The "Gran festival" language matches the Piraeus Diisoteria (large-scale), not the modest Agora magistrates' sacrifice that actually falls on day 30.

**Fix**:
- **Day 30**: Replace the current entry with the year-end sacrifice / Eisiteria: deity Zeus Soter alone; description = modest official sacrifice at the Agora Stoa of Zeus led by the King Archon and the outgoing council, as an institutional farewell to the old year and prayer for the new; participants = city magistrates and council, not the general public; aition = none explicitly given; Lysias 382 BCE is earliest reference.
- **Diisoteria (Piraeus)**: Move this to an earlier slot. The day is not precisely fixed; Deubner's "before day 20" suggests somewhere in days 14–19. If the data model requires a fixed day, day 16 or 17 is a defensible placeholder (not attested but consistent with Deubner's bracket). Deity: Zeus Soter + Athena Soteira. Description: procession with basket-bearer in Piraeus, large bull sacrifices, theoxenia for Zeus and Athena, ephebe regatta round the cape to Munichia.
- **Pan torch-race**: Remove from Skiraphorion entirely. It belongs in Boedromion if anywhere, but even there the date is a conjecture by Parke. This festival is not in the Attic calendar proper and can be dropped from this month.

## Festivals in sources but absent from app

**No additional state-level Skiraphorion festivals are unrepresented**, given that Skira (day 12) is already reviewed and all three remaining state festivals — Arrephoria, Dipolieia/Bouphonia, and the year-end sacrifice — are present in some form.

The deme-level festivals (Erchia 3rd Skiraphorion; Marathon Tetrapolis pre-Skira sacrifices) are local cult calendars and their absence from the app is appropriate.
