# Review: Anthesterion
Sources: Parke (1977), Deubner (1932)
Date: 2026-10-25

---

## Pithoigia — Day 11

**Parke**: Pithoi opened; samples of new wine carried to the shrine of Dionysus in the Marshes; first mixed wine offered to Dionysus; male worshippers then drank their first tastes of the season, praying beforehand that partaking of the new wine would be "harmless and actually beneficial." No mention of terror or demonic fermentation (Parke pp. 107–108).

**Deubner**: People brought pithoi and drinking vessels to the Limnaion; poured a libation and prayed for the wine's use to be "harmless and wholesome" (ἀβλαβῆ καὶ ὠφέλιμον); drank, danced, and sang invoking Dionysos; slaves permitted to participate.

**App**:
- Description: "Se abrían las jarras del nuevo vino y se llevaban muestras al santuario en las Marismas." — Correct.
- Participants: "Ciudadanos y Esclavos" — Correct (Deubner explicitly notes slaves participated).
- Aition: "Los atenienses rezan aterrados para que el nuevo vino sea 'inofensiva'. Consideraban la fermentación un espíritu demoníaco; solo Dioniso podía domesticar el líquido y evitar que envenenara la ciudad."

**Verdict**: UNSOURCED_AITION

**Issue**: The prayer for harmlessness is attested in both sources, but the framing is fabricated. Neither Parke nor Deubner describe worshippers as "aterrados" (terrified), and neither characterizes fermentation as a "demonic spirit" to be tamed. Parke treats the prayer as a standard precautionary libation before drinking, not as an apotropaic rite against malevolent spirits.

**Fix**: Change the aition to: "Antes de beber la primera copa del año se derrama una libación y se reza para que el nuevo vino sea inofensivo y beneficioso — un gesto de precaución ritual al abrir el zumo fermentado que lleva meses sellado." Drop the demonic fermentation framing entirely.

## Choes (Feast of the Wine-jars) — Day 12

**Parke**: Four distinct elements for this day: (1) Ship-chariot procession of Dionysus arriving at the Marshes sanctuary; (2) Basilinna's secret rites and the 14 Gerarai performing offerings at 14 altars in the sanctuary of Dionysus in the Marshes (sanctuary opened this one day per year); (3) Hieros Gamos — Basilinna married to Dionysus, consummated at the Boukoleion at nightfall; (4) private drinking parties with individual choes (silence, no sharing), drinking contest signalled by trumpet (winner received a cake at private parties, a full wine-skin at public banquets), garlands carried at end to priestess at the Marshes. Children's miniature choes mentioned in the Overview (p. 107). Aition of Orestes: blood-guilt, individual jars, silence — Parke calls this legend "fictitious" but notes it probably reflects the dangerous liminal quality of the feast of the ghosts (pp. 108–119).

**Deubner**: Explicitly lists four components of the Choes: (a) city-wide drinking contest with own chous each; winner received wineskin from Basileus; garlands to priestess at Limnaion; (b) Hieros Gamos via ship-wagon procession + Basilinna + 14 Geraeren at 14 altars + consummation at Bukoleion; (c) dead souls roamed the city — people chewed whitethorn, smeared doors with pitch, temples cordoned off; a ἡμέρα μιαρά; (d) children in their third year crowned with flower-wreaths and received gift-choes.

**App**: Description captures the drinking contest (in silence, trumpet signal) and the Hieros Gamos. Aition is the Orestes legend — attested. economyDesc conflates prize from private parties (cake) and public banquets (wineskin) into a single "odre lleno de vino o un pastel especial," which is acceptable shorthand. Participants correctly name Basilinna and Gerarai.

**Verdict**: MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues (three separate gaps)**:

1. **Ship-chariot procession entirely absent.** The procession of Dionysus arriving by ship-chariot is attested by Attic black-figure vases (Parke pp. 109–110) and is the ceremonial opening of the day. Not mentioned anywhere in the app entry.

2. **Children's choes festival entirely absent.** Deubner explicitly lists this as component (d): children in their third year crowned with flower-wreaths and given miniature choes as gifts. Parke mentions it in the Overview. Not in the app.

3. **Dead souls / apotropaic customs on Day 12 absent.** Deubner assigns to the Choes day (component c): dead souls roaming the city, people chewing whitethorn and smearing doors with pitch, temples cordoned off, the day treated as a ἡμέρα μιαρά. The app's apaphrades block assigns pitch and buckthorn to Day 13 only. Parke places pitch and buckthorn on Day 13 (Chytroi), so there is a genuine scholarly discrepancy, but Deubner's association of the ghost-roaming theme with Day 12 is significant and entirely absent from the Day 12 entry.

**Fix**:
- Add to festivalDesc: mention of the ship-chariot procession of Dionysus arriving at the Marshes.
- Add to festivalDesc or a new `childrenDesc` field: children in their third year crowned with flowers and given miniature choes.
- Consider noting (at least in a comment) that Deubner places the apotropaic ghost customs on Day 12, even if the app chooses to follow Parke and assign them primarily to Day 13.

## Chytroi (Feast of the Pots) — Day 13

**Parke**: Mixture of all kinds of vegetables boiled in a pot, offered to Hermes of the Underworld on behalf of the dead — no household members ate. Doorways smeared with pitch; residents chewed buckthorn from daybreak; sanctuaries closed. Expulsion cry: "Get out, Goblins (Keres), the Anthesteria is over!" Hydrophoria at the Olympieion: wheaten flour mixed with honey thrown into a chasm identified with Deucalion's Flood. Aition: the Flood myth (Deucalion). Parke treats the Orestes rationalization of these customs as "fictitious" (pp. 116–119).

**Deubner**: Pots with παμπέρματα (all-seed/grain mixture) set out for chthonic Hermes; prayer for the dead; no one ate from the offering; Hydrophoria (water-libations into the chasm near the Olympieion) for flood victims; expulsion cry "θύραζε, κῆρες, οὐκέτ' Ἀνθεστήρια"; comedy contests also attested.

**App**:
- Deity: "Hermes Ctónico" — Correct.
- isApaphrades: true — Correct.
- Description: "Día aciago. Se cocían vegetales en ollas para los fantasmas que vagaban por la ciudad." — Adequate.
- apaphrades instruction correctly mentions pitch on doors, chewing buckthorn, and the expulsion cry — all sourced.
- Aition: "Se cocina una olla de vegetales que nadie come para conmemorar el Gran Diluvio: representa el pánico de los supervivientes que cocinaron pero huyeron antes de probar bocado. También recuerda el suicidio de la princesa Erígone."

**Verdict**: UNSOURCED_AITION (two counts) + MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues**:

1. **Erigone's suicide not in sources for Chytroi.** Neither Parke nor Deubner connect Erigone's death to the Chytroi. Erigone appears in Parke only tangentially (the swinging/Aiora custom linked to the Anthesteria by a Callimachean reference, fn. 144), and Parke is explicitly cautious: "the identification is at least doubtful" (p. 118). Deubner's sources do not connect Erigone to the pot-offering at all. This is an invented aition that must be removed.

2. **Flood narrative "panic" detail invented.** Parke gives the Deucalion flood as aition for the Hydrophoria specifically (citing Pausanias and Apollonius of Acharnai), not as a narrative of survivors cooking and fleeing. The "pánico de los supervivientes que cocinaron pero huyeron antes de probar bocado" is an elaboration with no basis in either source.

3. **Hydrophoria not mentioned in the description.** Both Parke and Deubner describe the state ritual at the Olympieion chasm (flour/honey thrown in; water poured in — Hydrophoria) as a significant component of Day 13. It does not appear in the app entry at all.

**Fix**:
- Remove the Erigone reference entirely.
- Replace the invented flood narrative with: "La Hidroforia: el Estado vertía agua en la grieta del Olimpeion, la abertura por la que se tragó el agua del Gran Diluvio de Deucalión."
- Revise aition to: "Se cocina una olla de todo tipo de semillas para los muertos, y se vierte agua en la grieta del Olimpeion en memoria de los ahogados en el Gran Diluvio de Deucalión — la misma fisura por la que escaparon las aguas."

## Diasia — Day 23

**Parke**: Zeus Meilichios — "kindly / open to propitiation" (from μείλιχος). Celebrated by "the whole populace" outside the city at a sanctuary near the Ilissos. Thucydides (1.126) calls it "the greatest feast of Zeus Meilichios held outside the town." Offerings: bloodless, of "a native kind" — cakes shaped like animals (ancient commentators). Wealthy families gave whole burnt-offerings (sheep or pig). Families assembled for a communal meal but good joints were unavailable (festival of holocausts); ordinary families bought offal. Tone: "a very primitive institution" (p. 120); "a feeling of appeasement"; "not likely to have been very cheerful" (p. 122); yet Aristophanes' *Clouds* shows it also as a popular holiday with presents for children. Cylon connection: Thucydides mentions the Diasia to explain why Cylon's oracle about "the greatest feast of Zeus" referred to the Diasia rather than the Olympics — NOT as an aition for the festival (pp. 119–122).

**Deubner**: Celebrated outside the city (probably near the Ilissos); holocausts of sacrificial animals; common people made unbloody offerings (cakes in animal shapes) entirely burnt; private banquets also held; children received gifts (toy carts); gloomy (σκυθρωπός) character combined with private jollity.

**App**:
- Deity: "Zeus Meilichios" ✓
- deityDesc: "Zeus 'el Apacible' (subterráneo)."
- festivalDesc: "El mayor festival de Zeus. Holocausto de víctimas o pasteles con forma de animal para expiar la culpa y apaciguar al dios."
- Participants: "Toda la población" ✓
- ritualOffering: animals or cakes in animal shapes ✓
- Aition: "Se queman víctimas enteras para calmar a Zeus Meilichios por terror. El festival es una penitencia lúgubre por la infame masacre de Cilón, para limpiar a Atenas de aquella sangre derramada."

**Verdict**: UNSOURCED_AITION + WRONG_DEITY_DESC + MISSING_DETAIL

**Issues**:

1. **Cylon penance is a fabricated aition.** Parke explicitly states the Diasia "is a very primitive institution" that predates the Cylon affair. Thucydides mentions it only to identify which festival the oracle referred to — not to say the Diasia was founded as penance for Cylon's massacre. Neither Parke nor Deubner gives any aition for the Diasia; its origins are simply unknown. This inversion of Thucydides must be corrected.

2. **deityDesc: "subterráneo" mistranslates Meilichios.** Meilichios derives from μείλιχος ("honey-sweet," "gentle," "easy to appease") — cognate with μέλι (honey). While Zeus Meilichios does have chthonic associations (snake form, holocaust offerings), "subterráneo" is not a translation of the epithet. Parke glosses it as "kindly or open to propitiation."

3. **Festival's gloomy character understated; location outside city omitted.** Both sources emphasize that the Diasia was held outside the city (near the Ilissos) — not mentioned in the app. Deubner explicitly uses "σκυθρωπός" (gloomy/sullen). Parke says it was "not likely to have been very cheerful." The app's "penitencia lúgubre" catches part of this, but anchoring it to the invented Cylon story undermines the genuine attested character, which is one of propitiation of a chthonic deity, not civic penance.

4. **Children's gifts absent.** Both Parke (citing Aristophanes, *Clouds*) and Deubner note that the Diasia was also an occasion for giving presents to children (toy carts in Deubner). This popular holiday aspect, noted by Parke as the ironic contrast with the gloomy character, is missing.

**Fix**:
- deityDesc: "Zeus 'el Apacible' o 'el que se deja propiciar', con culto ctónico."
- aition: Replace the Cylon penance entirely. Thucydides (1.126) calls the Diasia the greatest festival of Zeus Meilichios and uses it to date Cylon's coup attempt — but he does not explain the festival's origin. Parke treats it as a pre-historic festival of propitiation with unknown origins. Remove the fabricated aition or replace with: "Tucídides lo llama el mayor festival de Zeus Meilichios. Sus orígenes son anteriores a la historia escrita: una ofrenda antiquísima al Zeus ctónico para aparcar su cólera al término del invierno."
- festivalDesc: Add "celebrado fuera de la ciudad, junto al Iliso" (location attested by Thucydides and Parke).
- Add mention of children receiving gifts.

## Lesser Mysteries at Agrai — Month of Anthesterion (day uncertain)

**Parke**: Obligatory preliminary initiation for Eleusis, held at Agrai on the Ilissos in Anthesterion. Deity: Demeter and Persephone. Ritual included purification by bathing in the Ilissos. Content of the Mysteries never described. Heracles legend as aition. Daiduchos officiated. Exact day within Anthesterion not specified (pp. 122–124).

**Deubner**: Obligatory preliminary initiation for the Great Mysteries; purification bath of the mystai in the Ilissos at Agrai; sacrifice by the Epimeletes; pantomimic performances with Dionysiac content; celebrated twice in years that also had the Eleusinia.

**App**: No entry at all for this festival.

**Verdict**: MISSING_FESTIVAL

**Issue**: Both sources treat the Kleine Mysterien as a significant Anthesterion event — a formal prerequisite for initiation at Eleusis in Boedromion. The absence is a meaningful gap for any user trying to understand the month.

**Fix**: Add an entry for the month of Anthesterion (no specific day, or show it loosely attached to the end of the month): deity Demeter and Persephone; description notes the purification bath in the Ilissos at Agrai, the preliminary character relative to Eleusis, and that the Torch-bearer (Daiduchos) officiated. Category: "Mistérico y Ctónico."

## Summary of Findings

| Day / Festival | Verdict | Primary Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Day 11 Pithoigia | UNSOURCED_AITION | "Demonic fermentation" framing invented; prayer for harmlessness is real but not terror-based |
| Day 12 Choes | MISSING_DETAIL | Ship-chariot procession missing; children's choes absent; Deubner's ghost-day customs on Day 12 unrepresented |
| Day 13 Chytroi | UNSOURCED_AITION + MISSING_DETAIL | Erigone connection fabricated; flood narrative elaboration invented; Hydrophoria omitted |
| Day 23 Diasia | UNSOURCED_AITION + WRONG_DEITY_DESC + MISSING_DETAIL | Cylon penance aition inverts Thucydides; "subterráneo" mistranslates Meilichios; location outside city omitted; children's gifts absent |
| Kleine Mysterien (Agrai) | MISSING_FESTIVAL | Both sources treat it as significant Anthesterion event; no app entry |

### Pattern note
The app's aitiā for Anthesterion systematically invent dramatic narratives (demonic fermentation, Erigone's suicide, Cylon massacre) where the sources either give no aition or give a simple one (Flood for Chytroi, Orestes for Choes). The Orestes aition for Day 12 is the one case where the app's aition is sourced — and Parke himself warns it is probably fictitious. The fabricated aitiā should be replaced with source-traceable explanations or omitted.
