## Astronomical core

`solar.ts` wraps `astronomy-engine` (sunrise/sunset via `SearchRiseSet`, moon phase via `MoonPhase`, conjunctions via `SearchMoonPhase`, equinoxes/solstices via `Seasons`, twilight crossings via `SearchAltitude`). All `*TimeUtils` files depend on `getSunTimes` and `getMoonPhase` from this module. `getSunTimes(date)` takes the device-local Y/M/D of `date` but places the day window at the observer's mean solar midnight, so a device in a far-away timezone still gets exactly one sunrise and one sunset per day.

`getTwilightTimes(date, lat, lng)` returns `{ civilMorning, nauticalMorning, civilEvening, nauticalEvening }` — the UTC moments when the sun crosses -6° (civil) and -12° (nautical) on a given calendar day. Any field is `null` during polar phenomena. Results are cached per UTC day × coordinates.

`getMoonLimbTilt(date, lat, lng)` returns `{ tiltDeg, litFraction }`: the direction of the Moon's sunlit side as the observer sees it (degrees counterclockwise from "up": 0 top, −90 right, +90 left), by the method of Astronomy Engine's demo `demo/nodejs/camera.js` (camera frame from `Rotation_EQD_HOR` + two `Pivot`s). Memoised per minute. `moonRotation(phase, tilt)` in `sceneKit.ts` uses it to orient every hero moon; `EgyptianStarMap` rotates its canvas moon by the same angle. Tests: `tests/moonLimb.test.ts`.

`RomanClockProps` includes `locationTimezone: string` (IANA timezone of the selected location). Ortus/Occasus Solis and the time-travel banner both format times in this timezone.

**Day boundaries and planetary hours (all civilizations).** Planetary hours run continuously in Chaldean order and the planetary day begins at sunrise: night hours (also those after midnight) belong to the weekday of the preceding daylight. The weekday is taken at the observer's mean local solar time from the sunrise/sunset that opened the current 12-hour period, never from `now.getDay()`.

**Observational lunar calendars (Babylonian, Attic)** share `lunarCivilCalendar.ts`: the civil day runs sunset → sunset; a month begins at the first sunset when the Moon is at least `minAgeDays` old (Babylon 0.75 d, Athens 1.5 d), which guarantees 29/30-day months; the year begins with the first month starting on or after the spring equinox (Babylon) or summer solstice (Athens); month starts are computed for the user's coordinates (Attic falls back to Athens when no location is passed). Day N = N-th sunset counted from the month-start sunset. Date pickers represent each civil day by the noon of its daylight half.

## Roman roads layer (Itiner-e)

- **Data**: `public/assets/roman-map/roads.json` (2.9 MB, 0.95 MB gzip), all 14 769 segments of Itiner-e static version 2024 (de Soto, Pažout, Brughmans et al., _Scientific Data_ 2025; doi:10.5281/zenodo.17122148; **CC BY 4.0**, attribution shown under the map while the layer is on). Generated by `npx tsx scripts/buildRomanRoads.ts <itinere_roads.geojson>`: EPSG:3395 → WGS84, Douglas–Peucker at 0.001°, 1e-4° integer deltas, string table. The script documents every normalisation. Don't hand-edit the JSON.
- **Loading**: `romanRoads.ts` `loadRomanRoads()` fetches it from the same `DATA_BASE`, only while the toggle is on (`localStorage` key `romanMapRoads`, default on). Precached by the service worker via `includeAssets`, but kept out of `globPatterns` (`globIgnores` in `vite.config.ts`) because it exceeds Workbox's 2 MiB glob limit.
- **Names**: Latin road names (`Via …`, `Strata …`) are the only ones used as map labels. Ancient itineraries (Tabula Peutingeriana, Itinerarium Antonini…) are listed in the card. Modern names (Watling Street, Fosse Way…) are shown as _Nomina recentiora_ only: never as labels. Builders (`Cons_per_e`) map to their standard Latin names. Period labels with no ancient form ("Tetrarchy", "Severan"…) are dropped; only their dates are shown.
- **Dating**: Itiner-e dates only 477 segments. The build adds the start year of `Cons_per_e` (≈ 2 800 more), giving 3 300 dated segments; 11 469 have no date. Errata fixed in `DATE_FIX` with source (Via Aemilia 187 BCE, Livy 39.2.10). `roadExistence()` returns `yes`/`no`/`unknown`.
- **Time-awareness**: when time-travelling, dated roads outside their range are hidden. Undated roads are **drawn faded, not hidden**: the dataset can't say they did not exist. The road card's _Iter per tempora_ slider sets `cardYear` that overrides the app date for the roads layer.
- **Drawing** (`components/RomanMapCanvas.tsx`): roads are grouped into 5° tiles. Path2Ds are built lazily per LOD × tile × style, cleared when the year changes. Road names are set letter by letter along chained segments of the same via (`buildRoadChains`), from zoom 2.5, in IM Fell English italic at ≥ 12 px.
- **Performance** (Chrome, 6× CPU throttle, dev build): vertical pan median 5.8 ms / max 9.6 ms per frame with roads (1.7 ms without).

## Roman festival data — sources and quality standards

Roman calendar day data lives in `romanYearData.ts` (`ROMAN_YEAR_DATA`). Keys are `"month-day"` (0-based month). Each entry is a `YearDayEntry`:

```ts
interface YearDayEntry {
  status: string; // "F" | "C" | "N" | "NP" | "EN" | "FP" | "QRCF" | "QRCI" | "COMP"
  statusFull: string; // Latin expansion of status abbreviation
  deity?: string; // primary deity or deities
  deityDesc?: string; // description of the deity's role
  festivalName?: string; // name of the festival if any
  festivalDesc?: string; // description of the festival
}
```

Sources cross-referenced (134 corrections applied 2026-10-25):

- H. H. Scullard, _Festivals and Ceremonies of the Roman Republic_ (1981) — primary reference
- W. W. Fowler, _The Roman Festivals of the Period of the Republic_ (1899) — secondary

**Rules for editing entries:**

1. `status` codes follow Mommsen's _Fasti_. Do not invent new codes.
2. `festivalDesc` must be sourced. If Scullard or Fowler gives no description, leave the field empty rather than paraphrasing encyclopaedias.
3. Deity order: primary deity first. Joint cults (e.g. Jupiter Optimus Maximus) are not split.

## Hellenic festival data — sources and quality standards

Festival data lives in `atticCalendarData.ts` (`getAtticFestivalInfo(monthIndex, dayOfMonth)`). Every entry has been cross-referenced against:

- H. W. Parke, _Festivals of the Athenians_ (1977) — primary source
- L. Deubner, _Attische Feste_ (1932) — primary source
- J. D. Mikalson, _The Sacred and Civil Calendar of the Athenian Year_ (1975) — date attestation

Research files in `docs/research/`: `hellenic-festivals-review-2026-10-25.md` (77 findings, 6 categories); `extracts/review/` (per-month files); `extracts/parke/`, `extracts/deubner/`, `extracts/mikalson/`.

**Rules for editing festival entries:**

1. **`aition` field is sourced, not invented.** Only add an aition if Parke, Deubner, or Mikalson explicitly discusses the myth or founding narrative.
2. **Day numbers are often uncertain.** Mark uncertain days as approximate in `festivalDesc`; do not present them as fact.
3. **Deity fields must match sources.** Check which deity is primary per inscriptional and textual evidence; order matters.
4. **Do not conflate separate festivals.** E.g. Munichia ≠ Brauronia; Rural Dionysia ≠ City Dionysia phallus-carts.
5. Open items (17 missing festivals, 25 unsourced aitia, 16 minor issues) are catalogued in the review report — tackle them before adding new data in those months.

## Egyptian festival data — sources and quality standards

Festival data lives in `egyptianFestivalsData.ts`. Two collections:

- **`CIVIL_FESTIVALS`**: keyed `"month-day"` (0-based month, e.g. `"3-1"` = I Peret day 1) plus epagomenal keys `"e-1"` … `"e-5"`. 27 entries as of 2026-10-25.
- **`LUNAR_FESTIVALS`**: array of festivals tied to lunar phases (new moon, full moon), not civil dates.

Each `Festival` entry has `name`, `description`, optional `icon`, and optional `fonte: FestivalFonte` for a Book of the Dead quote:

```ts
interface FestivalFonte {
  spell: string; // "Capítulo CXXX"
  excerpt: string; // verbatim excerpt in Spanish
  source: string; // edition and papyrus citation
}
```

`HALL_OF_MAATI` (42 `MaatiAssessor` entries) comes from Ch. CXXV, Papiro de Nebseni (BM 9,900). It drives the Hall of Maāti modal in `EgyptianCalendarInfo.tsx`.

Sources cross-referenced:

- S. El-Sabban, _Temple Festival Calendars of Ancient Egypt_ (2000) — primary civil festival reference
- C. J. Bleeker, _Egyptian Festivals_ (1967) — ritual interpretation
- A. von Lieven, _Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne_ (2007) — astronomical calendar
- E. A. Wallis Budge, _The Book of the Dead_ (1898), Theban Recension — `fonte` quotes; papyri Ani (BM 10,470), Nu (BM 10,477), Nebseni (BM 9,900)

Research files in `docs/research/`: `egypt-festivals-review-2026-10-25.md`; `extracts/egypt-by-month.md`; `extracts/book-of-the-dead/extract.md`.

**Rules for editing festival entries:**

1. **`fonte` field is sourced, not invented.** Only add a `FestivalFonte` if Budge's Theban Recension explicitly links the spell to a date or rite. Cite the chapter number and papyrus.
2. **Epagomenal days** (`"e-1"` … `"e-5"`) have their base text from `getEpagomenalDayInfo()` in `egyptianCalendarData.ts`. The `fonte` from `CIVIL_FESTIVALS["e-N"]` is surfaced separately in the epagomenal card — both paths must be kept consistent.
3. **Do not invent festivals.** Every entry must trace to El-Sabban or Bleeker.
4. **Key format**: civil keys are `"${monthIndex}-${day}"` with 0-based month (I Akhet = 0 … IV Shomu = 11). Epagomenal keys are `"e-${1..5}"`.

## Babylonian festival data — sources and quality standards

Festival day-event data lives in `babylonianLoreData.ts` as `BABYLONIAN_MONTH_LORE` — an array of 12 `BabylonianLore` entries. Each month entry has `dayEvents?: Record<number, BabylonianDayEvent>` keyed by day number (1–30):

```ts
interface BabylonianFonte {
  title: string; // e.g. "Enūma Eliš · Tablilla IV"
  excerpt: string; // verbatim excerpt in Spanish
  source: string; // edition and line citation
}

interface BabylonianDayEvent {
  shortLabel: string; // 1–3 word label shown in day grid dot tooltip
  description: string; // full ritual description shown in tap panel
  type: 'akitu' | 'procession' | 'mourning' | 'marriage' | 'ritual' | 'festival' | 'astronomy';
  fonte?: BabylonianFonte; // optional primary-text quote panel (collapsible)
}
```

Sources cross-referenced (scholarly review completed 2026-10-25, 8 HIGH + 6 MEDIUM corrections, 7 missing entries added):

- M. E. Cohen, _The Cultic Calendars of the Ancient Near East_. CDL Press, 1993 — primary day-level reference
- M. J. H. Linssen, _The Cults of Uruk and Babylon_. Cuneiform Monographs 25, Brill, 2004 — Hellenistic ritual series
- F. Thureau-Dangin, _Rituels Accadiens_. Ernest Leroux, 1921 — primary Akītu tablet (DT.109, MNB.1848)
- T. Jacobsen, _The Treasures of Darkness_. Yale UP, 1976 — mythological/theological background

Sources used for `fonte` excerpts: King 1902 (Enūma Eliš), Thureau-Dangin 1921 DT.109+MNB.1848 ll. 414–450, Rogers 1908 (Descent of Ištar), Jacobsen 1976 (sacred marriage hymn of Inanna), Abusch 2016 (Maqlu Tablet I). Future pass: Sachs & Hunger _Astronomical Diaries_ for `BabylonianSkyDiary`.

Research files in `docs/research/`: `babylon-festivals-review-2026-10-25.md`; `extracts/babylon/{cohen,linssen,thureau-dangin,jacobsen}/extract.md`.

**Rules for editing day events:**

1. **`fonte` is sourced, not invented.** Only add a `BabylonianFonte` if a verbatim cuneiform text passage is directly linked to the rite of that specific day. Cite edition and line numbers.
2. **Uncertain days must be flagged.** Many month-level rites have no attested day number. Mark them as approximate in `description`.
3. **Do not conflate city traditions.** Uruk and Babylon often held parallel rites; check Cohen and Linssen separately. Note the city in `description`.

## Babylonian calendar system

- **Era**: Seleucid Era (SE), epoch = 1 Nisannu SE 1 = 3–4 April 311 BCE (Julian). Code: `seYear = nisannuGregYear + 311` (Babylonian spring reckoning). Using `+ 312` would be the Macedonian autumn reckoning — wrong for this context.
- **Months**: 12 lunisolar months, each 29 or 30 days (synodic). Month names: 1 Nisannu · 2 Aiaru · 3 Simanu · 4 Dumuzu · 5 Abu · 6 Ulūlu · 7 Tašrītu · 8 Araḫsamnu · 9 Kislīmu · 10 Ṭebētu · 11 Šabāṭu · 12 Addaru. **"Warḫum"** (𒌗) = Akkadian word for "month", displayed as "Warḫum N" in the UI.
- **Intercalation**: 7 intercalary months per 19-year Metonic cycle. The extra month is historically **Addaru II** (or occasionally Ulūlu II). The app does not use a lookup table; a year is intercalary when 13 crescent months fall between two Nisannu 1s. Always shown as Addaru II.
- **Special days**: 7, 14, 15 (Sapatu — full moon "rest" day, shown with amber gold dot, NOT the Judaic Sabbath), 19, 21, 28 had restrictions on royal/priestly activity per cuneiform texts.

**Key algorithms in `babylonianCalendarUtils.ts`:**

- `getBabylonianDate(date, lat, lng)` — main entry point, returns `BabylonianDateInfo`.
- Month boundaries from real conjunctions + crescent sunset (`lunarCivilCalendar.ts`), at the user's location.
- `getNisannu1` — first crescent sunset on or after the vernal equinox.
- `getBabylonianCalendarMeta` — reports 13-month years (`isIntercalaryYear`, `intercalaryPositions`).

**Month-level metadata** in `BABYLONIAN_MONTH_LORE`:

- `monthRegent` = canonical regent from cuneiform month-regent lists (IV R 33; Astrolabe B). Shared source of truth: `BABYLONIAN_MONTH_REGENTS` in `types/babylonia.ts`.
- `festivalDeity` = deity of the month's main festival per the Astrolabe B menology. This is the evocative hero of the month card; it is **NOT** the regent.
- `regentNote` = note on variants/ambiguity.

## Chinese calendar — time system

- **Shichen** (時辰): 12 double-hours keyed to the Twelve Earthly Branches (地支). Each spans 2 solar hours. `getCurrentShichen(date)` in `chineseTimeUtils.ts` returns the current branch and progress within it.
- **Solar Terms** (二十四节气): 24 points of the ecliptic at 15° intervals, each ~15 days. Each is further divided into 3 pentads (候) of 5 days. Computed in `chineseCalendarUtils.ts`.
- **Lunisolar calendar**: month boundaries follow actual lunar conjunctions. `chineseLunisolarUtils.ts` computes the current month, day, and whether the year is intercalary.
- **Sexagenary cycle** (干支): 60-year cycle combining 10 Heavenly Stems (天干) and 12 Earthly Branches. Year, month, day, and hour each have their own pillar — together forming the Four Pillars of Destiny (八字, bāzì).
